RUDI/Approach/RUDI Method

The RUDI Method

The steps we use to assess, plan, and implement AI.

The method connects organizational evidence, leadership choices, work analysis, capability building, governance, implementation, and adoption measurement.

The objective

Build the organization’s capacity to understand, decide, implement, govern, and learn.

Ten steps

From baseline to measured adoption.

Not every engagement requires every step at the same depth. Together, the steps describe the full operating system RUDI is building and refining across organizations.

Organizational AI baseline

Document current activity, leadership questions, tools, policies, capabilities, opportunities, concerns, and context.

AI readiness assessment

Evaluate the conditions affecting adoption across leadership, people, workflows, technology, data, culture, and governance.

Leadership strategy

Align on value, priorities, decision principles, ambition, risk posture, and organizational commitments.

Jobs and workflow analysis

Examine tasks, information, decisions, expertise, handoffs, friction, and the human dimensions of the work.

Use-case portfolio

Define and prioritize candidate applications using value, feasibility, readiness, risk, dependencies, and learning potential.

Technology and tool decisions

Select appropriate platforms, access models, agents, integrations, and technical boundaries around validated needs.

Governance

Establish principles, policies, decision rights, data rules, human oversight, testing, monitoring, and escalation.

Workforce enablement

Build leadership and employee capability, role-specific practice, workflow resources, champions, and support.

Pilot implementation

Design, build, test, govern, and operate targeted workflow changes under realistic conditions.

Adoption measurement

Evaluate behavior, workflow change, quality, value, responsible use, user experience, and the conditions for scale.

Throughout the engagement

Gather evidence, involve your team, and review results throughout.

Evidence

Observe before prescribing.

Use leadership perspectives, employee input, artifacts, workflows, system context, pilots, and outcomes to ground decisions.

Participation

Design with the organization.

Engage the people who lead, perform, support, govern, and are affected by the work.

Learning

Use pilot results to revise the plan.

Treat each pilot, program, and implementation as a source of evidence about value, adoption, risk, and readiness.

The practical entry point

Most organizations should begin with a readiness conversation.

The conversation clarifies which decisions matter, what is already underway, what evidence exists, and whether a full readiness assessment is the right next step.

Start a readiness conversationExplore the assessment

Where to begin

Start with the decision or project you face.

The method helps diagnose the whole system. An engagement may begin with executive strategy, a workforce program, a governance need, or a candidate implementation—then connect to the missing conditions.
