AI News for October 21, 2026

One report puts AI-made political ad spending above $137 million, while a New Mexico court orders Meta to pay $25 billion and Canadian publishers sue Anthropic. Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 and new efficiency claims put local memory and data-center power back on the hardware agenda. All 71 stories from the day, below.

October 21, 202671 stories · 9 categories

Political campaigns have spent more than $137 million on AI-made ads ahead of the 2026 midterms, according to one report—a large single-source figure worth treating cautiously. The report says most of those ads lack AI disclosure and Republican campaigns account for 88% of the spending. Another report documents manipulated images, cloned voices, and deepfake audio in campaigns across Spain, Slovakia, and the United States. State lawmakers are responding unevenly: California and Arizona legislators described different attempts to regulate deceptive campaign content, while researchers found that AI-generated pre-bunking reduced belief in false election claims for at least a week. The persuasion tools are already in circulation; disclosure, regulation, and inoculation are trying to catch up.

CNBC reports that a New Mexico court ordered Meta to pay $25 billion after a jury found 43 million state-law violations tied to the Cambridge Analytica scandal; Meta says it will appeal. In Ontario, major Canadian publishers filed a copyright lawsuit against Anthropic over the use of their content to train Claude. Public institutions face their own mismatch between ambition and control: a GAO report found the Pentagon’s Responsible AI office had filled only three of ten authorized positions, even as Google Public Sector made agentic Gemini generally available to U.S. government agencies. The systems are arriving faster than the institutions assigned to govern them.

Nvidia released its Nemotron 3.5 open-model lineup, including a 74B model designed to run locally with 24GB of memory. At data-center scale, Nvidia says DSX can cut power consumption by up to 30% while raising inference throughput by 49% within a fixed power budget, and it announced eight-way DGX Spark clustering with up to 1TB of pooled memory. Elsewhere, ElevenLabs introduced a low-latency managed voice-agent stack without transcript access or component-level control, while Anthropic opened a waitlisted preview of the proactive Claude Duet agent. Buyers are getting more capable systems—and increasingly specific constraints on memory, power, access, and control.

What People Are Asking

How much are political campaigns spending on AI-made ads?

One report says campaigns have spent more than $137 million, up from under $50,000 in 2024. It attributes 88% of the spending to Republican campaigns and says most of the ads lack AI disclosure. Fortune →

Can AI pre-bunking reduce belief in election misinformation?

Yes. Research reported by Futurity found that AI-generated pre-bunking articles reduced U.S. voters’ belief in false election claims, with effects persisting a week later and no partisan backlash. Futurity →

Was Meta ordered to pay a $25 billion penalty?

Yes. A New Mexico court ordered Meta to pay $25 billion after a jury found 43 million violations of the state’s unfair-practices law tied to Cambridge Analytica; Meta says it will appeal. CNBC →

Why are Canadian news publishers suing Anthropic?

A coalition including Postmedia, Torstar, The Globe and Mail, and The Canadian Press alleges that Anthropic used its content to train Claude without permission. Radio-Canada →

Is Claude Duet publicly available?

No. Releasebot reports that Anthropic opened Claude Duet as a waitlisted research preview; the proactive agent monitors connected apps, sends daily briefs, and completes tasks with built-in safety guardrails. Releasebot →

Can Nvidia Nemotron 3.5 run locally with 24GB of memory?

Nvidia’s 74B-parameter Bolt model is designed to run locally on PCs with 24GB of memory. The Nemotron 3.5 lineup also includes a 120B-parameter Super VL enterprise model. shattered.io →

What did the GAO find about Pentagon AI oversight?

The GAO found that the Pentagon’s Responsible AI oversight office had filled only three of ten authorized positions and completed fewer than half of its strategy efforts, raising concerns about accountability and human control. Navy Times →

How much power does Nvidia say DSX can save?

Nvidia claims DSX can lower power consumption by up to 30% and deliver 49% more inference throughput within a fixed power budget. The reported evidence includes an Nscale test in Iceland, while CoreWeave and Google Cloud are evaluating adoption. 24/7 Wall St. →
