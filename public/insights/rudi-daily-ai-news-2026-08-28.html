AI News for August 28, 2026

A Wisconsin race puts political deepfakes to a real-world test, a study links greater AI understanding to greater fear of job loss, Andreessen Horowitz launches a $1.1 billion hardware fund, and Novo Nordisk partners with AWS on drug discovery. All 37 stories from the day, below.

August 28, 202637 stories · 38 source links · 9 categories

A Wisconsin congressional candidate used a deepfake video, turning AI-generated political content into a live regulatory problem. CNET examines how deepfakes can manipulate voters and erode trust as the midterms approach, while one report says plans for a U.S. AI self-regulating body have stalled. Meanwhile, a former CISA official urged a measured response to China’s AI progress—protect critical systems without turning concern into overreaction.

A new study found that people who understand AI better are more afraid of losing their jobs, especially in roles that can be digitized. Experts cited in another report warn that job displacement could provoke a stronger public backlash, and The Age describes recruiters’ bots screening applications written by jobseekers’ bots. In schools, one report says 86% of students and 85% of teachers used AI during 2024–25, while a Fraser Institute survey found most Canadian teachers believe AI is doing students’ homework as critical-thinking skills decline. Across work and school, wider use is arriving with sharper concern about lost jobs and weaker thinking.

Andreessen Horowitz launched a $1.1 billion fund for AI hardware startups as data-center racks approach one megawatt. Yet new research says 91% of banks have an AI strategy but only 21% measure its revenue impact—the hardware bill is becoming easier to see than the return. In drug development, Novo Nordisk and AWS formed an AI partnership with a London co-innovation hub, while Huawei said it plans more AI partnerships with pharmaceutical firms. Investors face another measure of the boom’s durability when the next U.S. jobs report tests the AI-driven market rally.
