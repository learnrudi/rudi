AI News for October 22, 2026

China tightens export licensing for large AI models, UN researchers warn about shrinking human control in military systems, Anthropic launches Claude Duet, and Barclays ties thousands of job cuts to automation. All 53 stories from the day, below.

October 22, 202653 stories · 54 source links · 10 categories

AICOPSE reports that China announced stricter export licensing for AI models, with added restrictions above 30 billion parameters, citing national security. The head of UNIDIR warned that AI is crowding human judgment out of military decisions, while the UN pushes for a legally binding treaty on lethal autonomous weapons. In the workplace, Italy's new safety mandate requires employers to assess AI systems that affect work organization beginning October 23, with no transition period. A Fox News live blog gathered the broader argument over safeguards, copyright, cybersecurity, investment, and public attitudes. Governments are writing limits around AI at the same time institutions are deciding how much judgment to hand it.

Companies kept handing agents more work. Anthropic launched Claude Duet, a proactive background agent built on Claude Opus 5.5, for task management and workflow automation. Barclays announced thousands of job cuts and explicitly attributed them to AI and software-agent integration, drawing union criticism. For professional work, Thomson Reuters rebuilt CoCounsel around agents, tax research, client data, code execution, and human review. GitLab added Gemini 4 Argon to its multi-model Duo platform, and MarTech reported Salesforce's $5.7 billion acquisition of Braze amid another wave of agentic marketing releases. The agents are moving into ordinary workflows; the fights over jobs, oversight, and control are arriving with them.

October 23, 2026. The report says the rule has no transition period and requires employers to include AI systems affecting work organization in mandatory workplace-safety assessments. TechTimes →
