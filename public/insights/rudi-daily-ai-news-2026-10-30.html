AI News for October 30, 2026

Tencent and Alibaba line up rival flagship models, a federal appeals court rejects fair use for AI training on copyrighted headnotes, Goldman tracks lasting scars from AI-related displacement, and campaigns scale up chatbot texts to voters. All 61 stories from the day, below.

October 30, 202661 stories · 62 source links · 10 categories

Tencent’s Hunyuan Hy4 and Alibaba’s Qwen4 are set to launch in the same week, putting two new model chiefs into a direct contest over agents and workplace productivity. The products around them are moving from chat windows into other kinds of work: Suno launched a beta tool for making songs inside major AI assistants, while an enterprise-agent roundup describes new offerings for customer service, identity supervision, business context, and governance.

Courts and campaigns are defining the boundaries as deployment spreads. The Third Circuit held that training a legal-research AI on copyrighted Westlaw headnotes was not fair use, emphasizing competitive use and market harm. Meanwhile, campaigns are using AI chatbots to text and persuade voters at scale, with accuracy, privacy, and absent disclosure rules still hanging over the conversation.

The labor evidence is harder to compress into a launch announcement. Goldman Sachs research finds that people displaced from AI-exposed jobs are taking longer to find work and facing more persistent unemployment, with older workers hit hardest. A separate survey found that only 31% of more than 1,000 women in tech said AI had improved gender equality. The tools are advancing quickly; the consequences are keeping their own calendar.

When are Tencent Hunyuan Hy4 and Alibaba Qwen4 launching?

Tencent’s Hunyuan Hy4 and Alibaba’s Qwen4 are expected to launch in the same week, in what one report describes as their first same-generation flagship matchup focused on agents and real-world productivity. [BigGo Finance] BigGo Finance →

Is training AI on copyrighted editorial material fair use?

No, not in this dispute. The Third Circuit affirmed that ROSS Intelligence’s use of Thomson Reuters’ Westlaw headnotes to train a legal-research AI was not fair use, citing their competitive function and market harm. [xnulls.com] xnulls.com →

What happens to workers displaced by AI?

Goldman Sachs research found that workers displaced from AI-exposed jobs since 2025 take longer to find new work and face more persistent unemployment; older workers are being hit hardest. [Fortune] Fortune →

Are political campaigns using AI chatbots to text voters?

Yes. A report says campaigns are using LLM-powered chatbots to text and persuade voters at scale during the midterm cycle, while disclosure rules remain absent and concerns persist about accuracy and privacy. [NewsOn6.com] NewsOn6.com →

Can Suno make songs inside ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini?

Suno launched a beta MCP tool that allows users to generate songs inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and other AI assistants. Its developer API also has a sign-in portal but remains in beta. [Music Business Worldwide] Music Business Worldwide →

Which drug companies are using Chai Discovery’s AI antibody models?

Chai Discovery says its generative AI antibody-design models achieved a 16% de novo hit rate. The company licensed the models to Lilly, Pfizer, Novartis, and argenx. [AI Magazine] AI Magazine →

Has AI improved gender equality in tech?

Only 31% of more than 1,000 women surveyed by Web Summit said AI had improved gender equality in tech; 52% reported experiencing sexism, and 42% had considered leaving or scaling back their careers. [Dataconomy] Dataconomy →

Why are dysprosium prices so different inside and outside China?

China’s rare-earth price index reportedly obscures a 16-fold gap between domestic and ex-China dysprosium prices as a November 10 export-control deadline approaches. Treat the single-source figure cautiously. [Tech Times] Tech Times →
