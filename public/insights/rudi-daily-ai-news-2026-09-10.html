AI News for September 10, 2026

Anthropic models an extreme AI economy with 32% GDP growth and deep cognitive-job losses, China’s micro-drama industry leans heavily into generated content, NVIDIA backs a planned 2-gigawatt buildout, and GPT 6 Astra begins a staged rollout. All 94 stories from the day, below.

September 10, 202694 stories · 97 source links · 10 categories

The largest number in this edition belongs to the labor market: Anthropic’s study projects that AI could raise GDP by as much as 32% by 2030 in an extreme scenario while pushing cognitive unemployment to 17.9% and labor’s income share from 60% to 45%. Enterprise vendors are already building around a less theoretical question: how companies control the work. Salesforce unveiled an open, composable infrastructure layer meant to give agents business context and IT governance, while its executives describe Slack becoming an AI work interface with new premium revenue attached. ActivTrak, for its part, says its forthcoming Workflow product will help enterprises decide where automation deserves investment and measure the result. The agents are arriving with governance dashboards close behind.

A Los Angeles Times report says more than 95% of China’s new micro-dramas are made using AI, with generated content and digital humans already reshaping the industry—a single-source figure worth treating cautiously, and a preview of the disruption and legal fights Hollywood may face. Elsewhere, a generative-media roundup spans Hollywood’s AI assessment, industry declarations, real-time video research, and software releases, while MarTech covers Anthropic’s blueprints for Claude-based shopping and merchant agents. Even the crowded coding market is getting harder to summarize: one hands-on review ranks nine assistants while tracking price changes, vendor instability, and different workflow fits.

The physical buildout keeps getting larger. NVIDIA is partnering with Australian operators on a planned 2-gigawatt AI-factory buildout by 2027, including a Sharon AI deployment of up to 68,000 GPUs. A venture roundup reports roughly $4.81 billion across ten deals, about 93% of it concentrated in three infrastructure-heavy companies, suggesting that investors still favor businesses that own scarce capacity. In medicine, one report says Insilico Medicine dosed the first patient in a Phase III trial of Rentosertib, whose drug and target were both discovered using generative AI, while a Frontiers editorial introduces ten studies applying machine learning to drug-resistant infections, virulence discovery, and clinical risk. Compute is scaling; the harder test is whether useful outcomes scale with it.
