AI News for November 26, 2026

Semafor reports banks are shifting AI-loan risk to investors as The HinduBusinessLine says U.S. chip curbs are stalling Asian data-center financing. Nigeria and Taiwan are confronting election deepfakes, while OpenAI reports a 10.9 percent lab binding rate for GPT-6.2-designed proteins pending independent replication. All 68 stories from the day, below.

November 26, 202668 stories · 69 source links · 10 categories

Semafor reports that Citi and JPMorgan are marketing portfolios of AI-related loans and shifting default risk to investors, moving exposure into less-visible parts of the financial system. The HinduBusinessLine says new U.S. chip curbs have prompted lenders to pause billions in data-center loans tied to ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent. One report says Nvidia, AMD, and Anthropic shares fell as investors questioned data-center returns, while a Kotak report warns that agentic AI at global banks could squeeze Indian IT firms’ base-business revenue. The financing question is no longer limited to who can fund the buildout. It now includes who is willing to hold the risk.

Nigeria’s INEC created an AI division to detect deepfakes and coordinated disinformation targeting the 2027 elections. Taiwan accused China of using AI deepfakes and thousands of fake accounts to meddle in its local elections. In the United States, two lawmakers introduced a bipartisan bill requiring quarterly reports on AI-built models and human oversight, while China opened public comment on data-governance and AI-development rules. Policy is moving upstream, from responding to deceptive content toward rules for how models and their underlying data are governed.

OpenAI says 35 of 322 GPT-6.2-designed protein candidates bound their intended targets in lab tests, a 10.9 percent rate the company calls proof of concept; the result still needs independent replication. Statistics Netherlands reports that AI use among Dutch enterprises more than doubled to 41 percent, with the sharpest growth among small and midsize businesses. Yet a Hays survey found three-quarters of tech employers investing in AI but only 38 percent planning to upskill staff, and India’s NITES tech union is asking for transparency, advance notice, severance, and retraining before AI-driven job cuts. Adoption is advancing faster than the training and worker protections around it.

Why have lenders paused some Asian data-center loans?

Is Nigeria using AI to fight election misinformation?

Yes. Nigeria’s INEC created an AI division within its ICT department to detect deepfakes and coordinated disinformation targeting the country’s 2027 elections. allAfrica.com →

Is Taiwan accusing China of AI election interference?

Yes. Taiwan accuses China of deploying AI deepfakes and thousands of fake social-media accounts to interfere in its local elections. The Independent →

What is the Recursive Self-Improvement Safety Act?

It is a bipartisan proposal from Reps. Josh Gottheimer and Nathaniel Moran that would require large AI developers to submit quarterly reports on AI involvement and human oversight in building new models. TokenPost →

Did GPT-6.2-designed proteins bind their targets in lab tests?

OpenAI says 35 of 322 GPT-6.2-designed candidates bound their intended targets, a 10.9 percent rate. The company calls it proof of concept, but the result still needs independent replication. technology.org →

How much did business AI use grow in the Netherlands?

Statistics Netherlands reports that enterprise AI use more than doubled from 2025 to 2026, reaching 41 percent. The sharpest increases occurred among small and midsize businesses. Statistics Netherlands →

Are tech employers training staff for AI?

Not at the same rate they are investing. A Hays survey found that three-quarters of tech employers are investing in AI, but only 38 percent plan to upskill their staff. IT Pro →
