AI News for November 26, 2026

U.S. chip restrictions reportedly stalled billions in AI data-center financing, Switzerland confirmed successful AI-agent-assisted intrusions, a study described China-aligned AI personas targeting Taiwan’s elections, and OpenAI reported a 10.9% target-binding rate for GPT-6.2 protein designs. All 100 stories from the day, below.

November 26, 2026100 stories · 102 source links · 10 categories

New U.S. chip restrictions reached the financing desk: banks reportedly froze billions in overseas AI data-center loans. A second report tied the paused lending to projects serving ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent, while chip distributors warned neoclouds that renting Nvidia capacity to Chinese companies could cost them GPU supply. On another front, Citi and JPMorgan began marketing portfolios of AI-related loans, including synthetic risk transfers, and AI stocks including Nvidia, AMD, and Anthropic fell sharply as investors questioned the returns on massive data-center spending. Export enforcement and balance-sheet anxiety have reached the same buildout from opposite sides.

Security and public trust supplied the sharper warning. Switzerland’s NCSC confirmed its first AI-agent-assisted intrusions, all of which succeeded. Nigeria’s election authority created an AI division to detect deepfakes and coordinated disinformation before the 2027 polls, and a Vanderbilt study described China-aligned “cyberborgs” blending AI imagery with human propaganda in seemingly apolitical posts. The science headlines carried their own demand for restraint: OpenAI said 35 of 322 GPT-6.2-designed proteins bound their intended targets in lab tests, pending independent replication, while a new tracker reported no regulatory-approved cures from AI-discovered treatments as of November 2026. Across attacks, elections, and drug design, the operative question was the same: what can be verified now, and what remains a claim.
