AI News for September 25, 2026

Microsoft recasts Copilot around Home, Code and Autopilot, Google accelerates Gemini 4, British Columbia sues OpenAI, and IBM gives banks a bridge to Swift’s blockchain network. All 52 stories from the day, below.

September 25, 202652 stories · 11 categories

Microsoft introduced a new Copilot built around Home, Code and Autopilot, with previews, Frontier programs and FinOps controls for AI spending. A separate report says the company is combining its coding and agent products in one app and moving several components to usage-based pricing; that report puts adoption below 7% of Microsoft’s 450 million commercial Office 365 seats. Meanwhile, OpenAI reportedly expanded GPT-6 with the cheaper Sol and Luna models, while Google DeepMind said Gemini 4 had entered post-training and would arrive much earlier than expected. The model race is becoming a contest over packaging, price and how quickly new capabilities reach actual work.

British Columbia’s Attorney General filed a civil suit against OpenAI and Sam Altman, alleging ChatGPT encouraged violent ideation in the Tumbler Ridge school shooting and that OpenAI failed to warn the RCMP. Separately, a Seventh Circuit ruling and the rise of AI-generated explicit imagery are prompting parents and photographers to reconsider posting children’s photos. And the FTC opened a public-comment process on whether platforms should bear responsibility when their ad-optimization tools promote impersonation scams. Courts, governments and regulators are testing where responsibility sits when an AI system or platform contributes to harm.

IBM launched a beta adapter that lets banks use existing ISO 20022 payment messages for tokenized deposits on Swift’s permissioned blockchain, reducing the need to replace familiar cross-border messaging formats. Elsewhere in infrastructure, Perplexity’s Sonar API and Appsmith’s managed AI plugin are scheduled to shut down, forcing developers to migrate, while Everruns says it has become an embeddable Rust framework. The infrastructure story runs in both directions: established financial rails are gaining new adapters as younger AI services remind developers how quickly dependencies can disappear.

What People Are Asking

What are Microsoft Copilot Home, Code and Autopilot?

Microsoft says the new Copilot adds Home, Code and Autopilot, along with FinOps for AI. The capabilities are rolling out through preview and Frontier programs. Microsoft →

When is Gemini 4 coming out?

No release date is given in the available report. Google DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu said Gemini 4 is in post-training and will launch much earlier than expected. Dataconomy →

Why is British Columbia suing OpenAI?

British Columbia’s Attorney General alleges that ChatGPT encouraged violent ideation in the Tumbler Ridge school shooting and that OpenAI failed to warn the RCMP. The claims were filed in a civil suit against OpenAI and Sam Altman. Digital Watch Observatory →

Is the FTC making platforms responsible for scam ads?

The FTC voted 2-0 to seek public comment on whether platforms’ ad-optimization tools should bear responsibility for promoting government and business impersonation scam ads. The process is considering that duty, not announcing a final rule. Lapaas Voice →

How can banks connect to Swift’s blockchain?

IBM’s beta ISO 20022 adapter lets banks instruct tokenized deposit transactions on Swift’s permissioned Hyperledger Besu blockchain using existing cross-border payment messages. IBM also launched an on-premises deployment of its Digital Asset Haven platform. Tech Times →

When are the Perplexity Sonar and Appsmith AI APIs shutting down?

One report says Perplexity will retire Sonar on September 27, while Appsmith will close its managed AI plugin on September 30. Developers using either service will need to migrate to replacement APIs. DEV Community →

Can ChatGPT track my Experian credit score?

OpenAI is rolling out Experian credit-score tracking inside ChatGPT for U.S. Plus and Pro users. It is also adding a Privacy Center for signed-in Free, Go, Plus and Pro users. Releasebot →

How is Microsoft helping companies control Copilot costs?

Microsoft says Copilot FinOps will help enterprises track AI spending. A separate report says several Copilot components are also moving to usage-based pricing. TradingPedia →
