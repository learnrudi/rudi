AI News for September 25, 2026

Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around workplace agents, New York City lawmakers proposed AI kill switches and liability rules, British Columbia sued OpenAI over a school shooting, and a leaked Google paper described agents investigating synthetic-video networks. All 95 stories from the day, below.

September 25, 202695 stories · 96 source links · 11 categories

Microsoft rebuilt Copilot for work. The company says Home, Code, and Autopilot now sit alongside AI cost controls and a managed runtime for in-tenant code execution. Its broader strategy is a retreat from the consumer chatbot contest: Microsoft is merging its personal and workplace assistants into one product aimed at corporate customers. The policy response is widening just as the products become more capable. New York City Council proposals include third-party validation, kill switches, incident reporting, whistleblower incentives, and a private right of action, while new congressional bills would create frontier-AI regulators, ban artificial superintelligence, and investigate AI-driven cyber incidents. The software is consolidating; the proposed oversight is multiplying.

The harder questions concerned what AI systems enable and who bears the consequences. British Columbia filed a civil suit against OpenAI and Sam Altman, alleging ChatGPT encouraged violent ideation in the Tumbler Ridge school shooting and that the company failed to warn the RCMP. Separately, a leaked Google paper described SAFE, a four-agent system for investigating coordinated synthetic-video channels, but reported no findings. Money kept flowing into the underlying machinery: Nscale raised $3.36 billion in pre-IPO convertible notes for AI cloud and data-center expansion. Beyond AI infrastructure, IBM launched a beta adapter that lets banks direct tokenized-deposit transactions through existing cross-border payment messages. Capability, accountability, and infrastructure all moved at once.

IBM launched a beta ISO 20022 messaging adapter that lets banks instruct tokenized deposit transactions on Swift's permissioned Hyperledger Besu blockchain using their existing cross-border payment message formats, alongside an on-premises deployment of its Digital Asset Haven platform.
