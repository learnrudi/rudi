AI News for October 12, 2026

Meta released its first frontier models, Microsoft unveiled an in-house inference chip, and one report citing CB Insights put quarterly venture funding at a record $698.3 billion. Publishers also brought a $250 million-plus copyright case against OpenAI. All 56 stories from the day, below.

October 12, 202656 stories · 10 categories

Meta’s Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier models, with pricing of $4 and $20 per million tokens and an “active memory” capability. Microsoft unveiled its third-generation Maia 300 accelerator, saying it will primarily use the chip for inference when production begins in 2027. NVIDIA outlined four components for continent-scale AI factories, including Spectrum-XGS networking that the company says can connect data centers across locations. Google Cloud introduced Gemini Agent for long-running enterprise work, with connections to Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack.

One report citing CB Insights put global venture capital at a record $698.3 billion in the third quarter, driven largely by AI mega-rounds concentrated in OpenAI and Anthropic even as deal counts fell to their lowest level since 2022. A separate weekly roundup listed five startup rounds ranging from $250 million to $2 billion. On the other side of the ledger, USA Today and publishers representing 19 newspapers filed a $250 million-plus copyright lawsuit against OpenAI, while Senate Democrats proposed an AI Safety Board inside the Commerce Department. Capital is concentrating, and so are demands for oversight and compensation.

OrcaRouter launched a cybersecurity model restricted to approved security researchers, claiming scores of 95.8% on CVE-Bench and 100% on Cybench. NTU Singapore researchers developed PLATO and PhaseHub to predict protein phase separation across species, with possible uses in disease, aging, and crop research. And TikTok CEO Zhou Jichu forecast major AI-driven scientific breakthroughs within two to three years, while emphasizing that verification and transparency will remain critical.

What People Are Asking

What is Meta’s Muse Wonder AI model?

Meta’s Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full as its first frontier models. They are priced at $4 and $20 per million tokens and include an “active memory” capability. TechCrunch →

When will Microsoft’s Maia 300 AI chip enter production?

Microsoft says production of its third-generation Maia 300 accelerator will begin in 2027. The company plans to use its own silicon primarily for AI inference, reducing its dependence on Nvidia GPUs. CNBC →

How much venture capital was invested in Q3 2026?

One report citing CB Insights puts global venture funding at a record $698.3 billion in Q3 2026. It attributes the total largely to AI mega-rounds concentrated in OpenAI and Anthropic, while overall deal counts fell to their lowest level since 2022. BeBeez International →

Why is USA Today suing OpenAI?

USA Today and publishers representing 19 newspapers allege that OpenAI’s models and training data contain their journalism. Their lawsuit seeks more than $250 million and the destruction of the models and data at issue. Law Commentary →

What would the AI Risk Management and Security Act do?

The proposed Artificial Intelligence Risk Management and Security Act of 2026 would establish an AI Safety Board within the Commerce Department. Senate Democrats led by Mark Warner introduced it amid a broader bipartisan push for federal AI oversight. PYMNTS.com →

What is Google’s Gemini Agent?

Gemini Agent is a general-purpose enterprise AI agent from Google Cloud. It supports multiple models and can use Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack to perform multistep workflows and long-running tasks. The Elec →

How well does OrcaCyber Zero 1.5 perform on security benchmarks?

OrcaRouter claims OrcaCyber Zero 1.5 scored 95.8% on CVE-Bench and 100% on Cybench for vulnerability detection and exploit verification. Those are company claims, and access is restricted to approved security researchers. IT Business Today →

What are PLATO and PhaseHub?

PLATO and PhaseHub are AI tools developed by NTU Singapore researchers to predict protein phase separation across species. The work may support research into disease, aging, and crops. phys.org →
