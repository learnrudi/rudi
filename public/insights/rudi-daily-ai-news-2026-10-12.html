AI News for October 12, 2026

One report says SpaceXAI released Grok 4.7 at $2/$6 per million tokens; NVIDIA set out Spectrum-XGS for geographically distributed AI data centers; an FCC petition could loosen consent requirements for political robocalls; and Intellizence tracked five startup rounds announced October 5–10. October 12, 202633 stories · 9 categories

One report says SpaceXAI released Grok 4.7, a coding model that nearly doubled its predecessor’s Terminal-Bench 4.0 score while holding pricing at $2/$6 per million tokens, though it still trails Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.1. Google Cloud unveiled a multi-model Gemini Agent that works across Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack to execute multistep and long-running tasks. For security researchers, OrcaRouter launched OrcaCyber Zero 1.5 with claimed scores of 95.8% on CVE-Bench and 100% on Cybench, but restricted access to approved researchers. The launches divide the market by job, workflow, and who is allowed through the door.

The infrastructure underneath those models is spreading out. NVIDIA says its four-part AI factory platform includes Spectrum-XGS networking that promises to remove geography as a data-center constraint. A daily roundup also covered Microsoft’s Maia 300 chip, Amazon’s reported $7 billion AI acquisition, Anthropic limiting live internet access during model testing, and Google defending a $15 billion India data center. The money is similarly concentrated: Intellizence reported five rounds announced October 5–10, led by Axiom’s $2 billion Series A. The software has agents; the build-out has very large checks.

Policy and research supplied two different tests of reach. One report says the FCC is considering waiving consent requirements for political robocalls, including calls using AI-generated voices, weeks before the November midterms; consumer advocates warn that automated calls could mislead voters. Treat the account as a single-source report. At NTU Singapore, researchers used large language models to mine PubMed and create a database of more than 5,100 protein sequences and a platform for analyzing phase separation across 1,106 species. One application may widen access to voters; the other widens the biological terrain researchers can examine.

What People Are Asking

What is Grok 4.7, and how much does it cost?

Tech-insider.org reports that SpaceXAI released Grok 4.7 as a coding-focused model priced at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens. The report says its Terminal-Bench 4.0 score nearly doubled its predecessor’s, although it remains behind Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.1. tech-insider.org →

What is NVIDIA Spectrum-XGS?

NVIDIA describes Spectrum-XGS as scale-across networking for connecting AI data centers across locations. The company says it can remove geography as a constraint on AI infrastructure. techtimes.com →

Is the FCC considering AI-generated political robocalls?

Yes, according to one report. The Republican-led FCC is considering a petition to waive consent requirements for political robocalls, including calls using AI-generated voices; consumer advocates warn that such calls could mislead voters. naturalnews.com →

What is Google’s Gemini Agent for enterprise work?

Gemini Agent is Google Cloud’s general-purpose enterprise AI agent. Google says it supports multiple models, works with Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack, and can execute multistep workflows and long-running tasks. thelec.net →

How much did Axiom raise in its Series A?

Intellizence reports that Axiom raised a $2 billion Series A. It was one of five startup funding deals the publication says were announced October 5–10, 2026. intellizence.com →

What is OrcaCyber Zero 1.5?

OrcaCyber Zero 1.5 is OrcaRouter’s cybersecurity AI model for vulnerability detection and exploit verification. OrcaRouter claims scores of 95.8% on CVE-Bench and 100% on Cybench, with access restricted to approved security researchers. itbusinesstoday.com →

How is AI being used to study protein phase separation?

NTU Singapore researchers used large language models to mine PubMed and build PLATO, a database containing more than 5,100 protein sequences. They also created PhaseHub to predict and analyze phase-separating proteins across 1,106 species. phys.org →

Did Microsoft make its own AI chip?

Yes. A daily technology roundup identifies Maia 300 as Microsoft’s in-house AI chip. techstartups.com →
