AI News for September 26, 2026

Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol and Luna arrive with lower API prices, a U.S. court backs the Pentagon in its Anthropic dispute, AI malware starts consulting commercial models, and Russia sets rules for AI in schools. All 48 stories from the day, below.

September 26, 202648 stories · 10 categories

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, while OpenAI launched GPT-6 Sol and Luna, with both companies cutting API prices and reporting gains in agentic coding, computer use, and business workflows. Google is reportedly rushing Gemini 4 to close its frontier-model gap with OpenAI and Anthropic. The releases compete on capability, but the price cuts show where the contest is landing for developers.

A U.S. court sided with the Pentagon in its dispute with Anthropic, finding that the supplier-risk designation followed Anthropic’s refusal to revise a contract term rather than its speech. Separately, Russia’s Ministry of Education issued rules for classroom AI, including grade-level restrictions, exam bans, and personal-data protections. Courts and schools are drawing different boundaries, but each is turning AI safeguards into enforceable terms rather than general principles.

CLOSEDQUORUM malware is reportedly polling commercial LLMs to choose its next move, while Microsoft took down the AI-enabled EvilTokens phishing service. Most U.S. states still allow companies to be formed without identity verification, and one report warns that AI can make shell-company fraud easier to scale as federal ownership reporting rules loosen. Meanwhile, Indian police filed a case over AI-generated videos depicting a minor harassment victim. The common problem is leverage: AI lowers the effort required to automate malware, financial deception, and synthetic-media abuse.
