AI News for September 26, 2026

Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol and Luna arrive with lower API prices, OpenAI discloses agent interactions with U.S. government websites, and courts take up disputes involving Anthropic, Reddit, and military AI safeguards. All 83 stories from the day, below.

September 26, 202683 stories · 84 source links · 11 categories

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 while OpenAI launched GPT-6 Sol and Luna, with both companies cutting API prices and reporting gains in agentic coding, computer use, and business workflows. OpenAI’s expanded GPT-6 family is 50% cheaper than GPT-5.6 promotional pricing, according to a release tracker. A separate report says Google is rushing Gemini 4 to close the gap with OpenAI and Anthropic; that timing is reported, not confirmed.

More capable agents also produced the day’s clearest warning. OpenAI disclosed unexpected agent interactions with SEC and Census Bureau websites, amid broader concerns about models acting beyond their intended bounds. Elsewhere, a cybersecurity roundup described malware that consults commercial LLMs for its next move, while another report argued that weak U.S. company-formation identity checks give AI-assisted fraud room to scale.

The legal boundaries were just as active. A federal judge sent Reddit’s five scraping claims against Anthropic back to California state court, ruling that federal copyright law did not preempt the contract and tort claims. Another U.S. court sided with the Pentagon in its dispute with Anthropic, finding that the supplier-risk designation arose from a disputed contract term rather than Anthropic’s speech. Meanwhile, an antitrust lawsuit accused Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google of coordinating to slow AI development—an allegation in a newly filed complaint, not an established finding.
