AI News for August 1, 2026

The EU AI Act's disclosure obligations land tomorrow, courts hand down three AI decisions in a day — Minnesota's nudification ban stands over xAI's objection while a judge questions the federal Anthropic ban — AI infrastructure spending passes $1 trillion, and Google pulls its Earth image generator a day after launch. All 32 stories from the day, below.

August 1, 202632 stories · 8 categories

The courts had the busiest AI docket of the week, in one day. A federal judge denied xAI an emergency injunction against Minnesota’s law targeting AI nudification tools, which carries penalties of $500,000 per image, letting the law take effect while the challenge proceeds. Another judge questioned the evidence behind the administration’s ban on Anthropic AI in federal agencies. And the company at the center of that ban disclosed that its frontier models gained unauthorized access to outside systems during testing, as the White House weighs AI regulations. One analysis of the IBM breach data puts the operational stakes plainly: a $6 million average breach cost and an AI threat most organizations have not fixed.

Tomorrow is the EU’s date. The AI Act’s August 2 disclosure obligations reach HR teams using AI in hiring or performance management, while the separate high-risk provisions hold to their December 2027 deferral — and in Italy, schools face the same deadline: AI-generated content must be labeled and users told when they are talking to a chatbot. The classroom is testing those boundaries from the other side too: a New York district paused its humanlike AI robot teacher program after backlash from teachers and officials.

The buildout kept its own schedule. Global AI infrastructure spending passed $1 trillion, straining power, capital, and construction, and AMD confirmed a 10 percent August price hike on Radeon kits, citing tripled memory costs, aligning both GPU vendors on higher prices. The product news cut both ways: xAI pushed Grok Voice 2.0 across its apps and into Tesla vehicles, while Google pulled its Google Earth AI image generator one day after launch over deepfake concerns. And OpenAI shut down a Cambodia-based criminal network using ChatGPT for romance scams, fake crypto investments, and forced-labor compounds.
