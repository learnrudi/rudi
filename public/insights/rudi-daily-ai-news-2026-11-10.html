AI News for November 10, 2026

Google says Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available, Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, Australia announces a new AI safety regulator, and U.S. lawmakers press for tighter AI-chip export controls to China. All 120 stories from the day, below.

November 10, 2026120 stories · 121 source links · 10 categories

Google says Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available, claiming top benchmark performance in agentic coding, cybersecurity and scientific reasoning at a lower cost per task. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2; Mashable reports faster generation, lower pricing and access to Mythos restricted to verified cybersecurity and biomedical organizations. xAI is reportedly preparing a soft rollout of Grok 4.8 to Bot users after overhauling its inference and reinforcement-learning stacks in C/C++ and cleaning its training data. Google DeepMind also announced Gemini Robotics 2.5, saying the model improves dexterity, reliability and safety and reached a 96% success rate on Mercedes-Benz production lines.

Australia’s prime minister announced a new AI safety regulator and is considering criminal-law changes for serious misconduct by frontier companies, according to The Conversation. Republicans joined Democrats in calling for immediate U.S. controls on AI-chip exports to China, with related legislation moving through the defense authorization bill. India’s government is considering mandatory use of indigenous AI models in critical sectors and potentially barring foreign frontier models and agents. A California federal judge allowed a proposed deceptive-advertising class action over Claude Max usage limits to proceed while dismissing several other claims. And rights holders for Elvis Presley and Eminem filed separate lawsuits against Suno, seeking more than $90 million in potential damages over alleged unauthorized training and voice cloning. The pressure runs from which models and chips may cross borders to how subscriptions are marketed and what training may use.

An Acronis report says more than 575 malicious OpenClaw skills delivered malware, including trojans, cryptominers and information stealers. Federal prosecutors charged three foreign nationals with using AI-generated voices and executive impersonation to defraud companies of more than $129 million worldwide, including nearly $3 million from a California hospital system. SHL and Robert Half surveys indicate roughly one in five U.S. workers replaced by AI were asked to return, with Klarna and Ford reversing some automation decisions. At the same time, researchers report that AutoDNA completed a full DNA synthesis and sequencing cycle during a 200-hour test with minimal human intervention, while a CatAI paper reports learning 1.8 million structure-property relationships and finding a catalyst with nine times the activity of a commercial benchmark. The practical record is widening in both directions: autonomous science on one side, malware delivery, voice fraud and failed workforce substitutions on the other.
