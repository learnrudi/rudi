AI News for November 10, 2026

Google says Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, congressional pressure for tighter China chip controls is rising, and Acronis researchers documented more than 575 malicious OpenClaw skills. All 73 stories from the day, below.

November 10, 202673 stories · 11 categories

Google says Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available and claims top benchmark performance in agentic coding, cybersecurity, and scientific reasoning at lower cost per task. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, with Mashable reporting that Mythos access is restricted to verified cybersecurity and biomedical organizations. xAI is preparing a soft rollout of Grok 4.8 after overhauling its inference and reinforcement-learning stacks. And Google DeepMind says Gemini Robotics 2.5 reached a 96% success rate on Mercedes-Benz production lines. The model race now runs from code and cyber work to factory floors, with access rules and per-task cost becoming part of the product pitch.

Pressure around Nvidia is spilling into both policy and competition. CHOSUNBIZ reports that a spy scandal involving Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is adding momentum in Congress for tighter AI-chip export controls to China, with the White House’s position seen as decisive. Seoul Economic Daily reports that Republicans joined Democrats in calling for immediate curbs as defense legislation moves toward a possible December vote. A separate report says Google unveiled eighth-generation TPUs and AMD promoted Helios server racks as rivals sought an opening in Nvidia’s leadership crisis. Meanwhile, India’s government is reportedly moving to require indigenous IndiaAI models and limit foreign frontier systems in defence and aerospace. The contest is now about who can sell the hardware, who can challenge it, and where governments will permit foreign models at all.

One report citing SHL and Robert Half surveys says roughly one in five U.S. workers replaced by AI have been asked to return, with Klarna and Ford reversing some automation decisions. At the same time, Salesforce is expanding Agentforce Coworker across Microsoft Teams, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and dedicated apps while introducing a flat $50-per-user monthly plan. Australia’s prime minister announced an AI safety regulator to enforce penalties on frontier companies and is considering criminal-law changes for serious misconduct. And Acronis researchers documented more than 575 malicious OpenClaw skills delivering trojans, miners, and infostealers to Windows and macOS systems. AI access keeps widening; the labor and security reports keep showing where human judgment and enforceable controls are still required.
