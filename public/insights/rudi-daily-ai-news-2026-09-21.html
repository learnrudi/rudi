AI News for September 21, 2026

One report describes a zero-click Plugin4Shell flaw across major coding assistants, British Columbia is suing OpenAI over alleged missed warnings before the Tumbler Ridge shooting, and Aolani says it will deploy 22,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs. Huawei’s accelerated chip plans and Qwen-Image-2.1 add an infrastructure race to the software and legal risks. All 50 stories from the day, below.

September 21, 202650 stories · 10 categories

One report says a zero-click remote-code-execution flaw called Plugin4Shell can reach Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, and Gemini CLI through malicious marketplace plugins; it says Anthropic and OpenAI have patched, Copilot remains unfixed, and consumer Gemini CLI is permanently vulnerable. That is a dramatic single-source account worth reading skeptically. Separately, CBC reports that British Columbia is suing OpenAI and Sam Altman, alleging the company failed to alert law enforcement about threatening ChatGPT messages before the February Tumbler Ridge shooting that killed eight people. A California antitrust complaint accuses Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google of coordinating to slow AI development, while a weekly OpenClaw update emphasizes atomic updates and plugin hot reload as ways to make deployments safer and reduce downtime. The pressure points differ, but each story turns on software control: what gets installed, what gets reported, and how companies allegedly coordinate.

The infrastructure buildout is splitting between scale and substitution. Aolani says it will deploy 22,000 Nvidia Blackwell Ultra GPUs in Malaysia and the Philippines by early 2027 through a revenue-sharing and credit-support model. In China, one report says DeepSeek plans to deploy at least 160,000 Huawei accelerators, with Huawei moving the Ascend 960DT launch up to the first quarter of 2027; that reported figure remains single-source. At the consumer end, unconfirmed leaks put the next Nvidia and AMD gaming generations in 2028 as the chipmakers prioritize AI data-center hardware. And Alibaba’s Qwen team open-sourced Qwen-Image-2.1, a 7-billion-parameter image generation and editing model with native transparency and multi-reference support, under a non-commercial research license.

Did DeepSeek order 160,000 Huawei AI chips?

What is Qwen-Image-2.1?

A report says Qwen-Image-2.1 is Alibaba’s open-sourced, 7-billion-parameter model for both image generation and image editing. It supports native transparency and multiple reference images but carries a non-commercial research license. Tech Insider →
