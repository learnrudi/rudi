AI News for September 21, 2026

British Columbia sues OpenAI over its alleged failure to warn law enforcement about threatening chats, heise reports a zero-click flaw in major coding agents, Alibaba's September 20 Qwen-Image-2.1 release adds image-generation options, and Nikkei finds 44-day release cycles across nine AI companies. All 36 stories from the day, below.

September 21, 202636 stories · 9 categories

The British Columbia government is suing OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in California, alleging they failed to notify law enforcement about threatening ChatGPT conversations before the Tumbler Ridge mass shooting. In software security, heise reports that Plugin4Shell enables zero-click remote code execution through malicious marketplace plugins affecting Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI; Anthropic and OpenAI have patched their products, while Copilot and the consumer Gemini CLI remain exposed. Meanwhile, a California antitrust complaint alleges that four AI companies coordinated a development slowdown, citing recent public statements by their leaders. And FINCHANNEL reports that Jensen Huang rejected AI-extinction warnings and put the chance of the world ending by 2030 at 0%, setting Nvidia’s CEO against frontier-lab leaders calling for a slower pace.

Alibaba’s Qwen team released Qwen-Image-2.1 on September 20, unifying image generation and editing around a 7B-parameter visual generator with transparent RGBA output and a non-commercial research license. That release comes as Nikkei reports that model-release cycles across nine selected U.S. and Chinese companies averaged 44 days from April through September 2026, about one-third of their previous length. Outside the lab, Perplexity brought its local-first Portable Computer agent to Windows RTX machines, requiring approval before work moves to the cloud, while Hyundai E&C began installing precast concrete for a 40 MW, GPU-focused data center in Pohang, cutting its framing schedule from a year to two months. BusinessToday also reports that an index of average pay in highly AI-exposed U.S. roles rose about 46% from 2021 to 2026 even as AI-linked layoffs increased. Fast model turnover still lands in a world of IT approvals, GPU buildings and employers bidding up AI skills while cutting jobs.

What People Are Asking

Why is British Columbia suing OpenAI?

The province alleges that OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman failed to notify law enforcement about threatening ChatGPT conversations before the Tumbler Ridge mass shooting. It is seeking damages and injunctive relief in California. CBC News →

What is Plugin4Shell, and which AI coding tools does it affect?

Plugin4Shell is a reported zero-click remote-code-execution flaw that works through malicious marketplace plugins. It affects Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI; heise reports that Anthropic and OpenAI have patched their products, while Copilot and the consumer Gemini CLI remain exposed. heise online →

Are AI companies being sued for agreeing to slow AI development?

Yes—one California antitrust lawsuit alleges that Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google illegally agreed to coordinate a slowdown. The complaint cites recent public statements by their leaders; the allegation has not been established as fact. Verdict →

What did Alibaba release with Qwen-Image-2.1?

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen-Image-2.1 on September 20. It unifies text-to-image generation and editing around a 7B-parameter visual generator, with transparent RGBA output and up to 10 reference images. It carries a non-commercial research license. Tech Insider →

Can Perplexity’s local AI agent run on Windows?

Yes, on Windows RTX PCs with at least 24GB of VRAM. Perplexity’s Portable Computer runs locally first and requires the user’s approval before escalating work to the cloud. TechRepublic →

How fast are AI model release cycles now?

Nikkei's sample of five U.S. and four Chinese AI companies found average model-release intervals of 44 days from April through September 2026, down from 125 days in January 2023–March 2026. The sample does not establish a universal release cadence. ChainCatcher →

Are AI-exposed jobs paying more even as layoffs rise?

BusinessToday reports that an average-pay index for highly AI-exposed U.S. roles rose about 46% from 2021 to 2026. It separately reports rising AI-linked layoff announcements. The comparison does not show that AI caused a 46% raise or caused every cited job cut. BusinessToday →

How is Hyundai accelerating construction of its Pohang AI data center?

Seoul Economic Daily reports that precast concrete will cut framing from one year to two months. Hyundai E&C is targeting a 16.5-month schedule for the 40 MW, GPU-focused facility. Seoul Economic Daily →
