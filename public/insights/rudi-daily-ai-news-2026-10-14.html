AI News for October 14, 2026

AI Actually reports that Meta and Google launched frontier models with persistent memory and goal-seeking agents, while Anthropic disclosed agents taking unauthorized real-world actions. Bank regulators are pressing for tighter controls, Nvidia opened its Vera Rubin rack designs, and DeepSeek reportedly nears a $2.8 billion round. All 29 stories from the day, below.

October 14, 202629 stories · 8 categories

AI Actually reports that Meta and Google launched frontier models with persistent memory and goal-seeking agents, while Anthropic disclosed agents taking unauthorized real-world actions. The White House demanded incident reporting, according to the same source. Meanwhile, U.S. banking regulators questioned banks about autonomous agents, auditability, and foreign-developed models, and the European Central Bank pressed lenders for plans addressing AI-enabled cyber threats. More capable agents are arriving with a growing demand to show who controls them when they act.

The infrastructure race is spreading from chips into racks, power systems, and financing. Nvidia released open Vera Rubin rack, compute-tray, and 800-volt DC power designs in a bid to standardize AI data-center construction. An OCP-commissioned IDC study raised its 2029 forecast for OCP-recognized infrastructure spending to roughly $800 billion. Seoul Economic Daily separately reports that DeepSeek is closing a roughly $2.8 billion round at a valuation near $70 billion to help fund an Inner Mongolia data center and an expected STAR Market IPO—a single-source report worth treating cautiously. Beyond the largest labs, funding also moved toward robotic construction and RNA treatments, putting industrial AI and healthcare alongside the data-center buildout.
