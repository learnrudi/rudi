AI News for October 15, 2026

Microsoft says MAI-Base-2 beats GPT-5.6 with one-quarter of the compute, Google describes an “RSI treadmill,” states challenge OpenAI over safety for minors, and new benchmarks expose weak scientific judgment. All 64 stories from the day, below.

October 15, 202664 stories · 11 categories

Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman told employees that MAI-Base-2 outperforms the model behind GPT-5.6 with one-quarter of the compute, a company claim that makes Microsoft’s push for independence from OpenAI unusually concrete. Sergey Brin said Google’s models are beginning to help build their successors as Gemini 4 launches. Meanwhile, NVIDIA introduced a 120B multimodal model and smaller models for local hardware, while one report says Meta released an 816B-parameter open-weight model designed to run on M5 Ultra Mac Studios. The contest is moving from benchmark scores toward who can own the model, the compute bill, and the machine it runs on.

A daily roundup reports that eight states sued OpenAI over ChatGPT’s safety for minors, alongside other efforts to impose liability on AI developers. Seoul Economic Daily reports that Anthropic agreed to a $1.5 billion settlement over pirated training books, as European rules restrict training on copyrighted works after rights holders opt out. In the UK, a Commons committee opened a standing inquiry into AI safety, governance, and adoption. In Australia, the electoral commissioner warned that giving the AEC control over AI in political advertising could compromise its impartiality. Courts, legislatures, and regulators are assigning responsibility while the technology is still changing underneath them.

A 248-case research benchmark found that four of five frontier models performed below chance on pivotal scientific decisions, with the best model reaching 63.5% accuracy against 73.4% for fresh human researchers. A separate physics benchmark found that strong scores on individual tasks often failed to produce complete research closure. Deployment carries its own gap: an OpenText-commissioned report found that 96% of banks are engaged in agentic AI activity but only 19% are deploying use cases. And Perplexity says nine frontier models failed to escape its SPACE sandbox across 118 tests. Ambition is abundant; evidence that agents can make sound decisions and operate safely remains much narrower.

What People Are Asking

Does Microsoft’s MAI-Base-2 beat GPT-5.6?

According to Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman, yes: he told employees that MAI-Base-2 outperforms the model powering GPT-5.6 while using one-quarter of the compute. That is Microsoft’s claim, not an independent benchmark finding. businessinsider.com →

Are eight states suing OpenAI over ChatGPT safety?

According to one daily roundup, yes. It reports that eight states sued OpenAI over ChatGPT’s safety for minors amid a broader push to hold AI developers responsible for harm. aitoolsrecap.com →

Did Anthropic agree to a $1.5 billion settlement over pirated books?

Seoul Economic Daily reports that Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion over pirated books used for AI training. The same report says the EU AI Act bars training on copyrighted works when rights holders opt out. en.sedaily.com →

How well can frontier AI models make scientific research decisions?

Poorly on pivotal decisions, according to SciΠvot. Four of five frontier models scored below chance, while the best reached 63.5% accuracy compared with 73.4% for fresh human researchers. arxiv.org →

Can frontier AI models escape Perplexity’s SPACE sandbox?

Perplexity says no model escaped in its evaluation: nine frontier models underwent 118 tests. The company is releasing the SPACE agent sandbox as an open-source project with CoreWeave, Akamai, and Crusoe. semafor.com →

How many banks are actually deploying agentic AI?

An OpenText-commissioned report found that 96% of banks are engaged in agentic AI activity, but only 19% are deploying use cases. It points to gaps in governance, data foundations, and testing. qa-financial.com →

How many Americans have been targeted by scams?

A Consumer Reports survey found that 91% of Americans have been targeted by a scam and 17% have fallen victim. It also found that 20% of recent attempts were personalized with leaked data and AI tools. blog.knowbe4.com →

Can FLUX 3 Image Klein run on consumer hardware?

Yes. Black Forest Labs released open weights for FLUX 3 Image Klein, a 5.6-billion-parameter image model designed to run on consumer GPUs and laptops. manilatimes.net →
