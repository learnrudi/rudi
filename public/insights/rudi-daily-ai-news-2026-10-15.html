AI News for October 15, 2026

Anthropic’s reported $1.5 billion book settlement sharpens the fight over training rights, the Pentagon seeks roughly $50 billion for defense autonomy, FLUX 3 Image Klein brings open weights to consumer hardware, and election deepfakes test regulators’ limits. All 36 stories from the day, below.

October 15, 202636 stories · 10 categories

Anthropic agreed to a $1.5 billion settlement over pirated books used in AI training, while the same report says the EU bars training on copyrighted works when rights holders opt out. The legal pressure is widening into continuing oversight: a UK Commons committee opened a standing inquiry into AI adoption, governance and safety, while Australia’s electoral commissioner argued that giving the AEC power over AI in political advertising could compromise its impartiality. The public cost is already tangible: a Consumer Reports survey found that 91% of Americans had been targeted by scams and 17% had fallen victim, with leaked data and AI tools used to personalize one-fifth of recent attempts.

A defense brief reports that the Pentagon is seeking roughly $50 billion for autonomy programs, putting government-scale money behind systems that can act with less human direction. At the other end of the hardware curve, Black Forest Labs released open weights for its 5.6-billion-parameter FLUX 3 Image Klein model, designed to run on consumer GPUs and laptops. Creative tooling moved too: Figma added reusable animation styles, timeline audio, per-character text animation and Lottie export to Motion’s open beta. Workers face a less tidy picture, with a 2026 data compilation reporting both rising AI-attributed layoffs and a 62% wage premium for AI skills. The tools are spreading; the rewards and risks are not spreading evenly.
