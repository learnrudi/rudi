AI News for July 24, 2026

Anthropic announces Claude Opus 5 at half the price of Fable 5 and returns Fable 5 globally, the White House's AI pre-release review formalizes by August 1, Delhi's High Court denies ANI interim relief against OpenAI, and Huawei's chip revenue heads for a 60% jump despite US controls. July 24, 202674 stories · 75 source links · 11 categories

Anthropic filled out its lineup in one day. Release notes announce Claude Opus 5, offering near-frontier performance on coding and knowledge tasks at half the price of Fable 5, while voice mode expanded to more models and languages and the company returned Fable 5 globally alongside a proposed industry framework for scoring jailbreak severity. The context is a torrid release cadence: one analysis counts seven model releases in seven days and reads the pattern as efficiency plays over capability jumps — cheaper, faster tiers rather than new ceilings.

The month’s access-control arc reached its deadline. The White House’s AI pre-release review framework formalizes by August 1 — voluntary on paper, as one report puts it, while the Commerce Department’s export orders have already demonstrated the mandatory authority underneath. The controls’ limits got a number the same day: Huawei’s chip revenue is set to jump 60% to $12 billion despite them — even as the US eased export rules for the UAE, upgrading it to the A:5 country group. Europe’s regulators moved on two fronts: an analysis walks through what the EU AI Act actually enforces and when as August 2 arrives, and the European Central Bank ordered banks to submit frontier-AI cyber-risk plans by October.

The courts and the security desk stayed busy. Delhi’s High Court denied ANI interim copyright relief against OpenAI, citing fair dealing — days after a US judge approved a $1.5 billion settlement, an Indian court leaned the other way. Apple’s trade-secret suit alleges OpenAI poached Apple engineers and took confidential information to build its hardware device. TechCrunch reports AI guardrails are impeding legitimate offensive-security researchers, one daily briefing describes a Claude Cowork sandbox escape, ransomware shows a record number of active groups with a 60% surge in victim disclosures, and UC Riverside’s SAGA tool identifies which AI system made a fake video, not just whether one did.
