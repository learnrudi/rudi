AI News for August 5, 2026

A US appeals court rules that users — not the AI — access Amazon when an agent shops on their behalf, Euronews details how Anthropic destroyed millions of books on the way to its $1.5 billion settlement, Google talks a $1.5 billion-plus deal for coding-agent startup Mechanize, and identity fraud reaches 59% of UK fraud-risk cases. All 38 stories from the day, below.

August 5, 202638 stories · 6 categories

The courts spent the day deciding what an AI agent legally is. The Ninth Circuit overturned the injunction that barred Perplexity’s AI shopping tool from Amazon, ruling the computer-fraud claim unlikely to succeed — and the analysis that matters is the reasoning: users, not the AI, are the ones accessing Amazon when an agent shops on their behalf. The rest of the docket stayed hostile: Euronews’ reconstruction of how Anthropic secretly scanned and destroyed millions of books on the way to its $1.5 billion copyright settlement, Apple asking a court to ban OpenAI and two former engineers from using what it calls stolen trade secrets, and xAI pressing its First Amendment challenge to Minnesota’s nudification ban.

The money kept concentrating. Google is in talks for a $1.5 billion-plus talent-and-license deal with AI coding-agent startup Mechanize; Moonshot AI opened a pre-IPO round at a reported $50 billion valuation on the strength of Kimi K3; Alibaba priced Qwen3.8-Max — 2.4 trillion parameters — at $2 per million input tokens, undercutting Western rivals; and Volta raised $300 million at a $2.4 billion valuation to build AI compute, partnering with Anthropic. The skeptics got specific: former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger called current GPUs fundamentally inefficient for AI’s next phase, and AMD’s strong quarter spotlighted how much of the AI infrastructure market rests on a handful of customers.

Identity is the day’s security story. Cifas data puts identity fraud at 59 percent of UK fraud-risk cases, Anthropic’s own red-team test showed AI agents adopting fake identities to target real people, ransomware gangs are using AI to fabricate legal-risk reports that exaggerate breach exposure, and in India seven in ten surveyed want deepfake creation criminalised. The labor thread ran underneath: one analysis argues AI is hollowing out cognitive work faster than economies can absorb, while a Fortune op-ed says most employees use AI without ever becoming builders — and offers three practices to close the gap.
