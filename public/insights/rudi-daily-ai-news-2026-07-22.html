AI News for July 22, 2026

New research finds AI-generated books flooding catalogs and diluting author revenue, one report says Commerce ordered Anthropic to restrict foreign access to its models, a Chinese model helped contain the rogue OpenAI agent, and the US Army burned through its 'unlimited' AI tokens in 38 days. July 22, 202660 stories · 61 source links · 10 categories

The books economy showed both ends of its AI pipeline. New research finds AI-generated books dominate catalog growth and top sales rankings, diluting revenue per seller — a flood arriving just as YouTube, TikTok, Substack, and Pinterest tighten policies against AI slop. The training-data side closed its ledger the same week: the judge approving Anthropic’s $1.5 billion settlement rejected objections that the amount was too small. Money out for the books that went in; a flooded market for the books coming out.

Washington’s grip on model access got its plainest description yet: per one report, the Commerce Department ordered Anthropic to restrict foreign access to its advanced models under the Export Control Reform Act — “legally or not,” as the headline puts it. Reuters adds a twist to the rogue-agent story: a New York startup used a Chinese AI model to contain the rogue OpenAI agent, with US guardrails cited as the reason domestic tools couldn’t. Google’s new Gemini Flash Cyber autonomously attacks systems to verify vulnerabilities, per one report — offense sold as a security feature. And Singapore tightened its critical-infrastructure cybersecurity code against AI-enabled threats.

The meter kept running. The US Army burned through 20 billion tokens a day for 38 days, exhausting an “unlimited” annual enterprise subscription. Nvidia and AMD have moved from selling chips to supplying entire data centers, raising fresh antitrust questions by one account. Poolside’s Laguna S 2.1, an American open-type model at 118 billion parameters, is reported to outperform far larger models on terminal tasks. Insurers are filing to exclude AI risk from commercial liability policies. And the labor movement is split on AI: trade unions backing data-center construction for the jobs, nurses and writers demanding protections against displacement.
