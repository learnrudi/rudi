AI News for July 12, 2026

AI bots are texting voters as the midterms near, Q1's $242 billion into AI startups was 80% of all venture money, Apple reportedly sues OpenAI over trade secrets, and Meta's image-feature retreat becomes a consent argument. All 69 stories from the day, below.

July 12, 202669 stories · 71 source links · 10 categories

The campaign text you just got may be a bot. NPR reports AI-powered bots are sending personalized political texts ahead of the midterms, and one report says some impersonate the candidates themselves. Paul Krugman’s Sunday argument fits the moment: AI’s harms get magnified when they land on extreme inequality and oligarchic politics. The money numbers read like his exhibit A: roughly $242 billion went into AI startups in the first quarter — about 80% of all global venture capital, with just four companies taking 65% of it.

Microsoft spent the weekend consolidating. It made GPT-5.6 the default model for Copilot across Office and began previewing Copilot Cowork, its autonomous assistant — and another report says both GPT-5.6 and Claude are rolling out to all Microsoft 365 Copilot users, with Excel’s AI positioned to replace VBA macros. AI executives insisted demand for compute is “almost unlimited” even as one analyst argued the binding constraint has moved from compute to memory — and the suppliers know it. Late in the day, one report claimed the US government plans to restrict powerful open-weight models within six months — a single-source claim worth watching rather than repeating.

Trust kept presenting its bill. Meta’s retreat on Instagram’s AI image feature became a consent argument — the analysis being that user trust is turning into a competitive factor, not a compliance checkbox. The Guardian profiled the lawyer who took on Meta and Google over addictive platform design — and won. Business Insider called out the distillation double standard: AI giants object to rivals training on their model outputs after years of scraping the web without permission. Mark Cuban urged AI companies to fund job-loss recovery in the communities pushing back against them. And a global survey found 88% of students use AI for coursework while only 29% trust their instructors to guide them. Two more landed at the close: a Forbes report says Apple has sued OpenAI for trade secret theft tied to its hardware program, and Google began requiring AI labels on ads across its five platforms to meet EU, India, and New York rules.
