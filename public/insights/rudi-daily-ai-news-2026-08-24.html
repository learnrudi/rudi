AI News for August 24, 2026

Reports put Nvidia behind a $6 billion Poolside model deal, an anonymous lab is offering Ox Alpha at extraordinary scale, Chinese workers face AI-driven job losses, and photo-provenance tools confront a flood of synthetic fakes. All 40 stories from the day, below.

August 24, 202640 stories · 9 categories

Tech Startups reports that Nvidia announced a $6 billion deal with Poolside to build a U.S. open-weight model aimed at DeepSeek, Kimi K3 and Qwen. One report says an anonymous lab released the frontier-class Ox Alpha with free access to 100 trillion tokens a day—a dramatic, single-source claim worth treating cautiously while its developer remains unknown. Thomson Reuters also announced a proprietary model built from its data and open-source foundations, while Cadence says accelerating chip roadmaps are forcing data centers to change their designs faster. Model competition is reaching from training data to cooling systems.

The Los Angeles Times reports that Chinese workers are losing jobs as employers adopt AI, while TechXplore describes workers adapting under government policies that accelerate AI adoption. The pressure is also changing how professional expertise gets sold: consultants are moving from hours and fixed fees toward outcome-based pricing. An EvalFlow compilation of HR statistics, meanwhile, puts AI adoption at 39% but worker trust in autonomous AI decisions at just 1%.

Experts cited by Chita.ru warn that AI is helping scammers build detailed victim dossiers and produce convincing voice and video deepfakes. Device makers are working the other side of that problem: C2PA and Apple’s planned reference-image feature may help verify photos, though experts say neither is a silver bullet. At the institutional level, Cyabra says it secured AI-powered disinformation-defense contracts with two European national-security agencies. The market for proving what is real now runs from a person answering the phone to governments watching coordinated influence campaigns.

What People Are Asking

Is Nvidia investing $6 billion in Poolside?

One report says yes: Longbridge describes Nvidia as reportedly investing $6 billion in Poolside to develop open-weight AI models. The investment should be treated as reported rather than independently confirmed. Longbridge →

What is the Ox Alpha AI model?

Ox Alpha is described as a frontier-class model released free by an anonymous lab, with access reportedly reaching 100 trillion tokens a day. That extraordinary scale comes from a single report, and the developer remains unidentified. BigGo Finance →

Did Thomson Reuters launch its own AI model?

Yes. Thomson Reuters says its proprietary model, Thomson, combines the company’s data with open-source foundations and claims frontier-level performance at lower cost. Thomson Reuters →

Is AI replacing workers in China?

Some are. The Los Angeles Times reports layoffs tied to AI adoption in China, alongside worker adaptation and concerns about broader economic and social effects. Los Angeles Times →

Can C2PA prove that a photo is real?

They may help, but experts caution that they will not solve the problem alone. C2PA and Apple’s planned reference-image feature are intended to help verify photo provenance as synthetic images proliferate. TechXplore →

How are scammers using AI voice and video deepfakes?

Experts warn that scammers use AI to assemble detailed information about victims and create realistic voice and video deepfakes, making targeted fraud harder to detect. chita.ru →

Are governments using AI to fight disinformation?

Cyabra says it secured contracts with two European national-security agencies to provide AI-powered disinformation defense, producing more than $500,000 in annual revenue. The Manila Times →

Did an AI dog-cancer vaccine startup raise funding?

KuCoin reports that Gamgee raised $4 million after founder Paul Conyngham used AI to create a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine for his dog Rosie. The startup aims to make such vaccines accessible and affordable for dogs. KuCoin →
