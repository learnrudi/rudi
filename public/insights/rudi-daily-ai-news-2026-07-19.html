AI News for July 19, 2026

Alibaba announces Qwen 3.8 at 2.4 trillion parameters — second only to Fable 5, by its own account — Australia moves to curb automated government decision-making, Russia's Federation Council approves a framework AI law, and 88% of teachers now use ChatGPT while only a third of districts have AI rules. July 19, 202642 stories · 43 source links · 7 categories

Alibaba took Sunday. It announced Qwen 3.8, a 2.4-trillion-parameter multimodal model with preview pricing at 10% of standard rates and an open-weight release promised — and performance that is, by its own account, second only to Anthropic’s Fable 5. That is vendor math, worth reading as such until the weights ship. The rest of the model race filled in around it: an analysis piece describes Grok 4.6 as a 2-trillion-parameter model targeting Kimi K3 capability at Grok 4.5 speed, OpenAI has, per one report, started billing for agents while its ChatGPT Work mode folds multi-step agent tasks into existing subscriptions, and one infrastructure analysis argues the competition has moved to 100,000-GPU systems — networking, power, cooling, and reliability rather than chips.

Governments regulated on a Sunday. Australia will curb automated AI decision-making in government, packaged with a digital duty of care and privacy reforms — a response, per one report, to past automated errors. Russia’s Federation Council approved a framework AI law that creates categories for sovereign and national models and requires labeling of AI-generated content. Coverage of the publishers’ copyright suit against Google over Gemini training kept spreading. And China declined to apologize for a state-media AI video mocking Filipinos, folding an AI-content dispute into its South China Sea position.

The adoption-governance gap got a number: 88% of teachers use ChatGPT while only one in three districts has AI rules. India’s market regulator warned of the boss scam: AI voice clones and fake CEO messages aimed at finance employees. One analysis reframes the data-center backlash as a capacity-planning problem for enterprises, not just hyperscalers — state restrictions and local activism now translate into cost and siting risk. And the Meta layoffs ruling got its reasoning in second-day coverage: the plaintiffs failed to show irreparable harm, so the layoffs proceed while the discrimination suit continues.
