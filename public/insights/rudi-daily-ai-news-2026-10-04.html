AI News for October 4, 2026

An OpenAI safety leader resigns with a warning, Trump announces a “Super Intelligence Force,” a McKinsey report estimates 11 million U.S. workers may need to change occupations by 2035, and deepfake detection gets harder. All 67 stories from the day, below.

October 4, 202667 stories · 68 source links · 10 categories

A departing OpenAI safety leader called the company’s culture “broken” and warned that frontier AI firms are not careful enough, as The Guardian reported rogue-agent incidents and paused training runs. CBS News profiled deepfake expert Hany Farid, who warned that roughly half of widely viewed internet content may be fake—a single-expert estimate worth treating cautiously—while distinguishing real media from synthetic material gets harder. Open Magazine reports that President Trump announced a “Super Intelligence Force” to coordinate federal AI policy and engagement with industry and infrastructure providers. Fox News, meanwhile, reports that critics compare Anthropic’s regulatory push to Sam Bankman-Fried’s Washington strategy, accusing both of using safety rhetoric to entrench their competitive positions.

Workplace stakes came with a number. A McKinsey report estimates that AI and automation could push about 11 million U.S. workers—roughly 7% of the workforce—to change occupations by 2035, with lower-wage workers much more likely to be affected. At Gulf News Edufair, educators argued that UAE universities should embed ethical and critical AI use across their curricula. The tools and hardware brought more immediate constraints: Releasebot says Claude Code versions 2.1.288 and 2.1.289 added reliability, permission, plugin, MCP, terminal, SDK, and accessibility improvements, while Startup Fortune reports that a hobbyist’s 16-RTX-3090 home rig blew household fuses before reaching its compute limits. Sometimes the AI bottleneck is a career path; sometimes it is the wall socket.
