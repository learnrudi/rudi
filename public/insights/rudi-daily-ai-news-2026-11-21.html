AI News for November 21, 2026

One report alleges sweeping AI-chip approvals for Chinese companies, major school districts resist mandatory AI Overviews, Anthropic’s IPO tests its safety mission, and an AI-led market selloff exposes the boom’s financial risks. All 100 stories from the day, below.

November 21, 2026100 stories · 104 source links · 10 categories

One report says newly released documents show the Trump administration approved hundreds of thousands of advanced Nvidia and AMD chips for Chinese companies, including military-linked firms. The report says the disclosures prompted bipartisan demands for answers and upcoming congressional hearings; its scale is a single-source claim worth treating skeptically. A Daily Caller poll found most American voters favor pausing advanced AI-chip sales to China pending an investigation. At home, Chicago, New York City, and Los Angeles pushed back on Google’s requirement that schools accept AI Overviews to retain Search, while Shine Lawyers investigated a potential class action over Australian artists’ and musicians’ work being used for AI training without permission. India’s chief justice also urged model rules requiring disclosure of AI assistance in legal research. Across government, schools, legal education, and the arts, the fight was over who can impose a pause, require disclosure, or demand consent.

NPR reports that a broad AI-led market selloff and interconnected financing are raising fears of wider financial consequences. The governance test inside that selloff is Anthropic: legal experts warn its founders and wealthy employees could dissolve the trust meant to protect its public-benefit safety mission. Beyond markets, researchers uncovered a suspected North Korean remote worker using a fake Virginia company and a real-time face swap to pursue U.S. contracts, Taiwanese Micron workers staged the country’s first semiconductor strike and demanded higher pay and profit-sharing, and Novartis said AI is accelerating drug discovery and trials while clinical benefits remain unproven. The boom’s promises are now meeting harder questions about governance, verification, labor, and proof.
