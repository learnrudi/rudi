AI News for November 12, 2026

The U.S. reportedly prepares wider AI-chip export controls, Oakland workers reach a tentative deal with AI retraining protections, a California suit seeks to halt new model development, and Nvidia’s leadership story splits across conflicting reports. All 110 stories from the day, below.

November 12, 2026110 stories · 10 categories

The U.S. Commerce Department is reportedly finalizing rules that would add seven countries to AI-chip export restrictions and curb Chinese companies’ remote access to overseas data centers. A coalition including Public Citizen has meanwhile asked a California court to halt new model development at Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceXSI. In federal court, a judge allowed 56 amicus briefs in the copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft as the court weighs fair use in AI training. Anthropic also banned Claude users from building fake sites and reviews to manipulate search and AI-answer sources, while loosening some rules on political personalization and automated media. Governments, courts, advocacy groups, and an AI lab were all drawing lines around who may build models, where chips may go, and how systems may shape information.

Fed Governor Michael Barr warned that the U.S. should prepare for serious short-term AI job disruption and widening inequality, with AI investment currently pushing inflation higher. Oakland city workers showed what a contract response can look like: a tentative three-year deal would require the city to retrain or reassign workers displaced by AI rather than fire them. The Guardian describes a broader public-sector labor push for protections against AI displacement and algorithmic management. Meanwhile, a running tracker ties many 2026 tech layoffs to increased AI spending and workforce reductions, and a Stack Overflow talent manager says AI has made résumés and coding tests less reliable, pushing recruiters toward evidence of experience, judgment, and trust. The labor response is already splitting across warning, bargaining, layoffs, and new hiring filters.

Nvidia’s leadership story did not settle cleanly. One Substack post said Jensen Huang resigned and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. A separate report said the board was preparing to remove him, putting a 680 trillion won Korean AI partnership at risk. The reported status differs, so the resignation claim remains single-source and unsettled. Elsewhere, Google rolled out Gemini 4 Carbon to AI Ultra subscribers across AI Mode, the Gemini app, and Antigravity, xAI reportedly began a limited Grok 4.8 test with a small group of subscribers, and Alibaba put Qwen4-Max-Preview on a two-week clock before retirement without benchmarks, a model card, parameter count, or token pricing. The model race had launches; Nvidia supplied the uncertainty.
