AI News for November 12, 2026

Conflicting reports put Jensen Huang’s Nvidia leadership in question, one report says Anthropic is moving toward a U.S. IPO targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, Oakland workers secure AI job safeguards, and Google rolls Gemini 4 Carbon to Ultra subscribers. All 75 stories from the day, below.

November 12, 202675 stories · 10 categories

Nvidia’s leadership was the day’s biggest—and messiest—story. One Substack report says Jensen Huang resigned and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, while Seoul Economic Daily says Nvidia’s board was preparing to remove him, putting a 680 trillion won Korean AI partnership at risk, and a market brief cites after-hours reports that the board may oust him. The accounts do not line up, so Huang’s departure should not be treated as settled here. At the same time, the U.S. Commerce Department was reportedly finalizing wider AI-chip export controls covering seven countries, extending the uncertainty from Nvidia’s boardroom into the chip supply chain.

Courts, advocates, companies, and schools drew separate lines around AI use. One report says a coalition including Public Citizen filed in San Francisco Superior Court for an injunction that would halt new model development at five companies. A Manhattan federal judge allowed 56 friend-of-the-court briefs before a fair-use ruling in the copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft, while Anthropic’s updated Claude policy bans fake sites and reviews designed to manipulate search and AI-answer sources. In Florida, Orange County Public Schools now requires instructor permission and district-sanctioned AI platforms. The mechanisms differ—injunction, fair-use litigation, vendor rules, classroom permission—but each places a gate between an AI system and unrestricted use.

Oakland city workers averted a strike after winning retraining and reassignment instead of layoffs, and Fed Governor Michael Barr warned that AI could cause serious short-term job disruption and widen inequality. Yet a FICO survey found that only 1.1% of financial-services firms broadly deploy AI agents. Meanwhile, Google rolled out its strongest model, Gemini 4 Carbon, to AI Ultra subscribers across AI Mode, the Gemini app, and Antigravity, while one report says Anthropic is moving toward a U.S. IPO targeting up to a $1.4 trillion fully diluted valuation—a single-source valuation worth reading skeptically. Large capital bets, limited broad deployment, and workers bargaining over consequences made the day’s practical tension hard to miss.

Did Jensen Huang resign as Nvidia CEO?

The reporting conflicts. One Substack report says Huang resigned and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, while separate reports say Nvidia’s board was preparing or considering his removal; his status should not be treated as settled. enoumen.substack.com →

One report says Anthropic is moving toward a U.S. IPO targeting up to a $1.4 trillion fully diluted valuation and potentially raising up to $64.4 billion. Those are reported, single-source figures, not confirmed terms. cryptobriefing.com →

What AI job protections did Oakland workers win?

Their union secured safeguards including retraining and reassignment instead of layoffs, allowing Oakland city workers to avert a strike. britbrief.co.uk →

Reportedly. The U.S. Commerce Department is said to be finalizing controls covering Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway, and South Africa, while Congress considers three export-control bills for the NDAA. en.sedaily.com →

How many financial-services firms broadly use AI agents?

Very few, according to a FICO survey: only 1.1% of financial-services firms have broadly deployed AI agents. rallies.ai →
