AI News for September 8, 2026

One report puts Mistral’s Series D at €3 billion, Alibaba Cloud opens its first Brazil region, Qualcomm and AWS pair up on inference chips, and China’s top court sets rules for AI disputes. All 43 stories from the day, below.

September 8, 202643 stories · 8 categories

One report says Mistral raised €3 billion in Series D funding at a €21 billion valuation to build a European alternative to U.S. AI companies. Alibaba Cloud announced its first cloud region in Brazil, with two data centers and plans for agentic AI services, while Qualcomm announced an AWS partnership to co-develop custom AI-inference chips, putting silicon at the center of its challenge to Nvidia. A separate roundup reports new models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, an OpenAI custom chip, and data showing AI job creation; those bundled claims remain one outlet’s account, not independent confirmation. Capital, cloud regions, and custom chips made the infrastructure contest unusually literal.

China’s Supreme People’s Court issued its first judicial interpretation on AI disputes, clarifying rules for deepfakes, data discrimination, and intellectual-property protection. South Africa’s election authority introduced a code requiring parties to correct false election claims within 36 hours and label AI-generated content. In the United States, the Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging their articles were used without authorization to train AI systems. Court rules, election procedures, and publisher litigation are drawing boundaries around different parts of the same technology stack.
