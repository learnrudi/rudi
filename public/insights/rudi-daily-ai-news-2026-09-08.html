AI News for September 8, 2026

Mistral’s €3 billion round backs a sovereign European AI push, Qualcomm and Amazon plan inference chips for AWS, China’s high court sets rules for AI disputes, and Meta introduces Muse under privacy and safety scrutiny. All 68 stories from the day, below.

September 8, 202668 stories · 69 source links · 10 categories

Mistral raised €3 billion at a €21 billion valuation to build a European sovereign-AI alternative, while Qualcomm announced an Amazon partnership to co-develop inference chips for AWS and targeted $15 billion in data-center revenue by 2029. Alibaba Cloud launched its first Brazil cloud region with two data centers and plans for agentic AI services. Runway, meanwhile, reported $200 million in annual recurring revenue and more than 300% net revenue retention as it pushed further into enterprise video infrastructure.

China’s Supreme People’s Court issued its first judicial interpretation on AI disputes, addressing deepfakes, data discrimination, and intellectual-property protection. A second report says the rules assign liability for deepfakes, chatbot hallucinations, and algorithmic price discrimination, with a notice-and-act standard for platforms. In the United States, a Manhattan federal judge will decide whether training AI on copyrighted works is fair use, while the Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft over the alleged unauthorized use of their articles. The rules are arriving through courts as often as legislatures.

Meta introduced the Muse personal AI agent app with subscription tiers while facing scrutiny over privacy and safety. A KPMG survey found that active AI use across finance functions more than doubled from 2024 to 2026, although data quality and talent remain obstacles. In public life, experts warned that AI-generated political ads could sway elections in Australia and called for urgent regulation, while South Africa’s election authority gave parties 36 hours to correct false claims and required labels on AI-generated content. Private adoption and public persuasion pose different control problems, but both put disclosure under pressure.
