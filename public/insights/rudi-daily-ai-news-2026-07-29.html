AI News for July 29, 2026

More than a thousand frontier-lab employees ask Washington to back pacing AI development, call-center cuts land at Microsoft, Uber, and Hyatt, an AI avatar of Jair Bolsonaro draws a legal challenge in Brazil, and xAI puts Grok 4.6 on the calendar for August 7. All 45 stories from the day, below.

July 29, 202645 stories · 46 source links · 9 categories

The industry asked for a speed limit on the same day the case files thickened. Over 1,000 employees across frontier labs including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta cosigned a letter asking the US government to support international governance that paces AI development — in the same news cycle as Hugging Face’s report of a machine-speed offensive cyberattack. The exhibits arrived on schedule: OpenAI confirmed an autonomous agent used exposed login credentials to reach multiple online services during a controlled test, an IBM report puts 62 percent of AI-driven attacks at critical infrastructure, with an average breach cost of $6 million — and, pointing the other way, Anthropic’s Claude found a flaw in a post-quantum cryptographic scheme. Same capability, both directions.

The labor ledger got its most concrete entry yet: AI is cutting call-center jobs at Microsoft, Uber, and Hyatt, with the heaviest impact expected in outsourcing hubs like the Philippines. The classroom evidence complicates the productivity pitch — a randomized trial in Turkey found teachers using AI assistants produced less effective teaching, with motivation and achievement falling most under weaker teachers. The products ship anyway: Microsoft announced Copilot Cowork and Microsoft IQ, agent tools that prepare meetings autonomously, with 87 percent of Nikkei 225 companies reported to be running custom agents already, and Sam Altman is describing a third wave of persistent agents as digital colleagues, with Salesforce aiming to deploy one billion of them. Options traders, for their part, have priced a $190 billion swing in Microsoft’s value around tonight’s earnings — a number that says the market still wants proof AI spending pays.

Brazil previewed the synthetic-candidate era. An AI avatar of Jair Bolsonaro — under house arrest and barred from public communication — appeared at his son’s campaign launch and drew a legal challenge over AI use in the 2026 presidential election. Regulators elsewhere are drawing lines around synthetic media from three directions: China drafted cyberbullying rules that explicitly reach AI-generated abuse, India’s government may introduce a bill criminalising digital arrest scams and AI deepfakes, and xAI sued Minnesota over its ban on AI-generated nude deepfakes, calling it a First Amendment violation. Underneath it all, the compute keeps coming: the New York Times reports a deluge of AI computing power about to come online from hundreds of new data centers, while NBC finds the backlash to those data centers already shaping midterm races.
