AI News for July 20, 2026

A US judge approves Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement, AMD's first rack-scale system Helios heads for delivery with Microsoft aboard, Hugging Face reaches for a Chinese open model to fight off an autonomous cyberattack, and Illinois enacts frontier-model safety law. July 20, 202670 stories · 71 source links · 9 categories

The week opened with a $1.5 billion receipt. A US judge approved the Anthropic copyright settlement over the books used to train Claude — described in one report as the largest US copyright settlement yet. And Fortune reports Hugging Face turned to a Chinese open-source model to fight off an autonomous cyberattack after US model guardrails hindered its response — a safety-versus-competitiveness debate compressed into a single incident. In the same accountability register, the UK’s auditors told the government to do the math before banking on £45 billion in annual AI savings.

AMD put hardware behind years of talk about challenging Nvidia: its first rack-scale AI system, Helios, is headed for delivery with Microsoft as its newest customer, joining Meta, OpenAI, and Oracle. AMD shares rose more than 3%, with one report calling Helios the first strong rival Nvidia has faced in years — a judgment deliveries, not announcements, will settle. The agent economy, meanwhile, started metering: Microsoft’s Copilot Cowork now consumes credits as it rolls out globally, xAI shipped a free Grok for Excel add-in powered by Grok 4.5, and McKinsey finds half of consumers now start product research in AI search — brands are chasing citations now, not rankings.

The governance ledger ran from statehouse to street. Illinois enacted an AI Safety Measures Act imposing governance, transparency, audit, and incident-reporting obligations on developers of advanced frontier models — a state law reaching directly into the frontier tier. At the UN, leaders argue global AI governance cannot wait as the digital divide widens, pressing for guardrails and capacity building in developing countries. And Vox examines the rise of AI populism — protests, and in some cases violence, aimed at AI and data centers, driven by anger over corporate power exercised without democratic input.
