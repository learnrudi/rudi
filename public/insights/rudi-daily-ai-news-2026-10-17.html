AI News for October 17, 2026

China proposes export controls on 23 AI technologies, a daily roundup reports Anthropic’s C$10 billion Nova Scotia data-center lease, OpenAI offers eligible U.S. academic labs free access to GPT-Rosalind, and Kaiser nurses challenge AI-driven monitoring. All 35 stories from the day, below.

October 17, 202635 stories · 8 categories

China proposed export controls on 23 AI technologies as its rivalry with the United States intensifies. Experts and industry leaders are calling for government and global oversight, CBC reports, while warning that rapid capability gains and international coordination make regulation difficult. A legal-news roundup covering a $400 million TikTok settlement and California’s kids’ algorithm law shows regulators working through those questions case by case.

The infrastructure buildout is meeting pressure from several directions. Rich Miller’s weekly roundup says Microsoft plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments in 2027, while BlackRock’s Larry Fink warns that a data-center moratorium could trigger a recession. Inside the workplace, Kaiser advice nurses say AI-driven monitoring and time metrics are shortening patient conversations. In entertainment, AI film and video studios are clustering in Culver City even as unions and industry skeptics push back.

A daily roundup spans Pudu robots, Anthropic’s C$10 billion Nova Scotia data-center lease, GLM-5.3 reasoning research, ChatGPT Slides, and AI liability concerns. OpenAI also announced free GPT-Rosalind access for eligible U.S. academic labs for up to 12 months. Beyond products, seven of 18 New York City Council responses about affordable housing were flagged as partly or fully AI-generated, with some officials acknowledging that AI helped draft their statements.

What AI technologies does China want to control as exports?

Why is AI regulation so difficult?

CBC reports that rapid improvements in AI capabilities and the need for international cooperation make effective government oversight difficult, even as experts and industry leaders call for it. cbc.ca →

Are New York City Council members using AI to write statements?

Some appear to be. City & State found that seven of 18 responses from New York City Council members about affordable housing were flagged as partially or fully AI-generated, and some officials acknowledged using AI to draft statements. cityandstateny.com →

How is AI monitoring affecting Kaiser nurses?

Kaiser Permanente advice nurses say AI-driven call monitoring and time metrics pressure them to end patient conversations sooner; California regulators and their union are pushing back. kvia.com →

What is Microsoft planning for its Maia AI chips?

Rich Miller’s weekly roundup says Microsoft plans to scale its Maia custom silicon to multi-gigawatt deployments in 2027. datacenterrichness.substack.com →

What is OpenAI’s Rosalind Academic Plan?

OpenAI announced a program giving eligible U.S. academic labs free access to its GPT-Rosalind life-sciences model and workbench for up to 12 months. releasebot.io →

Are AI studios moving into Culver City?

Yes. The Los Angeles Times reports that AI film and video studios are forming a cluster in Culver City, attracted by Hollywood infrastructure and cheaper real estate, despite opposition from unions and industry skeptics. latimes.com →

How widely is AI being used in Chinese manufacturing?

China reported AI use in key manufacturing scenarios at 34.2% in early 2026. That figure cannot be directly compared with the EU’s expansion of AI Factories and its Gigafactory tender because the measures differ. tokenpost.com →
