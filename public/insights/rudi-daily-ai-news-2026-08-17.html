AI News for August 17, 2026

Meta faces a landmark child-addiction trial, Alibaba brings an open-weight model to laptops, and ByteDance agrees to copyright guardrails for AI-generated content. One report also says OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team amid leadership exits. All 36 stories from the day, below.

August 17, 202636 stories · 9 categories

Four state attorneys general took Meta to court over allegations that it designed addictive social features for children, misled the public, and violated child privacy laws. The Motion Picture Association announced its first AI copyright agreement, with ByteDance adding guardrails to its video and image tools after legal threats from major studios. Abroad, Taiwan’s DPP warned that China is using deepfakes and targeted disinformation to interfere in elections, while one report says Ukrainian intelligence found an Nvidia AI chip in a Russian missile despite restrictions on Western technology. The cases are different; each tests whether rules written for platforms, intellectual property, elections, or exports can constrain how AI is actually used.

Alibaba introduced a laptop-ready model and open-sourced its most powerful model in a challenge to Meta’s open-weight lead. The safety story moved in the opposite direction: one report says OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team amid leadership exits and an approaching IPO—a single-source account worth treating cautiously. An analysis of recent incidents argues that unpredictable agent behavior has turned alignment into an immediate technical and social problem, and a Connecticut court sanctioned a litigant for concealing prompts intended to manipulate AI systems in legal filings. Meanwhile, Microsoft set September 14, 2026, as the retirement date for Excel’s COPILOT() function and directed users to its side pane. Capability, control, and product design did not move in lockstep.

A BBVA analysis describes the EU’s decade-long regulatory effort, the AI Act’s delay, and a growing recognition that rules alone will not secure global competitiveness. At work, an Australian Financial Review opinion piece argues that union demands for AI protections could produce excessive workplace regulation, while a Mint analysis argues that AI, technology, and flexible work have structurally weakened the 9-to-5 office model. In science, industry experts said AI can accelerate drug discovery but still faces hard questions about clinical efficacy and safety. Across policy, employment, and medicine, deploying the technology remains easier than proving that the surrounding system can govern it.
