AI News for August 17, 2026

Alibaba took Qwen to laptops, the EU postponed much of the AI Act until December 2027, and the Motion Picture Association reached an AI copyright agreement with ByteDance. One report says OpenAI also dissolved its Preparedness team amid leadership exits. All 73 stories from the day, below.

August 17, 202673 stories · 74 source links · 10 categories

The EU postponed much of its AI Act until December 2027, according to BBVA, as policymakers also turn to initiatives such as the Cloud and AI Development Act. The Motion Picture Association then reached its first AI copyright agreement with ByteDance, adding guardrails to Seedance and Seedream after legal threats from studios. Four state attorneys general brought Meta to trial over allegations that it designed addictive features for children, misled the public, and violated child privacy laws. And Ukrainian intelligence found a $999 Nvidia AI chip in a Russian missile, evidence of how gray markets can frustrate controls on civilian hardware. The rules took four different forms: a delay, a negotiated deal, a courtroom test, and diversion through gray markets.

Alibaba unveiled a laptop-ready model and open-sourced its most powerful Qwen model, intensifying its open-weight contest with Meta. Nvidia introduced Nemotron 3.5 Lightning for single-GPU consumer hardware. A separate analysis of Qwen 3.8 said its headline benchmark claims still await independent verification. And one source reports that Stripe paid more than $7 billion for OpenRouter—a single-source figure worth treating skeptically. The open-model contest now runs from the laptop and single GPU to the routing layer that chooses among models.

IBM’s 2026 breach report found that AI-related breaches mostly came from API and cloud misconfigurations, not compromised models. A Connecticut court sanctioned a litigant for hiding AI prompt injections in legal filings, which Security Affairs describes as the first documented attack of its kind on a U.S. court. A Gallup report and experts said manager support is critical for workplace AI adoption, even as many companies lack training and organizational changes. Meanwhile, one report says OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team amid leadership exits and an upcoming IPO. With misconfigured APIs, hidden prompts in briefs, and managers short on training, much of the risk sits around the model rather than inside it.
