AI News for October 13, 2026

One report says Meta launched Muse Wonder, NVIDIA readied local AI software for RTX Spark laptops, the White House restored UK access to unreleased frontier models, and more than 80 publishers backed a standard for tracking AI use of news. All 36 stories from the day, below.

October 13, 202636 stories · 9 categories

KuCoin reports that Meta launched Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, agent models trained on its Prometheus data center. That is one outlet’s report, so the launch and benchmark comparisons deserve confirmation. Ahead of October 16 laptop availability, NVIDIA is shipping Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B, a personal AI router, Llama for Windows, and creator-tool updates for RTX Spark. Workday says its Sana agents will operate inside Claude, Gemini Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot, Slack, and Teams while applying company permissions, while Intel positioned its 480GB D270 inference GPU as a value alternative to Nvidia and AMD hardware. Together, those moves put agent systems on new chips, personal computers, and the workplace tools employees already use.

Access and control were the other half of the story. The White House reversed a reported restriction on UK pre-release model testing, according to UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan. The New York Times, AFP, and The Economist joined more than 80 publishers backing a standard to track and monetize AI use of news. Anthropic says anti-distillation safeguards cut extraction of Claude reasoning data by 90–99%, even as Reuters reports that Anthropic and OpenAI sought approval rights over some evaluator disclosures. Denmark proposed banning realistic AI deepfakes shared without consent while preserving satire and parody exceptions, and a security roundup described FortiBleed credential theft, suspected LLM-tool-driven bank intrusions, and a third-party ASOS breach. Models are spreading; the arguments over who can test, inspect, monetize, imitate, and secure them are spreading with them.

What People Are Asking

What is Meta Muse Wonder?

KuCoin reports that Meta launched Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, agent-focused frontier models trained on its Prometheus data center and benchmarked against OpenAI and Anthropic models. Treat the launch and benchmark claims as a single-outlet report. kucoin.com →

When do NVIDIA RTX Spark laptops launch?

RTX Spark laptops are scheduled to become available October 16. Ahead of that date, NVIDIA is shipping Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B, the PAIR personal AI router, Llama for Windows, creator-tool updates, and a faster DLSS 5 model. gadgetpilipinas.net →

What is Intel’s D270 AI chip?

Intel’s D270 is an AI inference GPU with up to 480GB of LPDDR5X memory, 1.3 petaflops of FP8 compute, and a 350W air-cooled design. Intel is positioning it as a value alternative to Nvidia and AMD hardware. time.news →

Did the White House restore UK access to unreleased AI models?

Yes. According to UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan, the White House reversed a reported restriction and restored UK AI Security Institute access to unreleased frontier models for pre-release safety testing. shattered.io →

How are publishers trying to make AI companies pay for news?

More than 80 publishers in the SPUR coalition are backing a technical Content Telemetry Standard intended to track and monetize AI use of news content. New members include The New York Times, AFP, and The Economist. startupfortune.com →

How is Anthropic trying to stop Claude model distillation?

Anthropic says new safeguards reduced extraction of Claude reasoning data by 90–99%, at the cost of rejecting paying traffic. The same report says Reuters found that Anthropic and OpenAI sought approval rights over what some independent evaluators could publish. thedeepdive.ca →

Is Denmark banning AI deepfakes without consent?

Denmark is proposing legislation that would prohibit sharing realistic AI-generated versions of a person’s face, voice, or body without consent. The proposal preserves exceptions for satire and parody. whatsthebigdata.com →

What are the FortiBleed firewall attacks?

A weekly security roundup describes FortiBleed as credential-harvesting attacks targeting Fortinet firewalls. It also covers suspected LLM-tool-driven intrusions at South Korean banks, an ASOS third-party breach, and other incidents. dmarcreport.com →
