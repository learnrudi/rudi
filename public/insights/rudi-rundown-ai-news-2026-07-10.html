OpenAI turned its model launch into a product launch. The workplace aim isn’t subtle: scheduled tasks, desktop integration, and web-portal creation for enterprise workflows.

The rest of the market answered on price. Meta’s Muse Spark 1.1 opened its paid API at a quarter of Anthropic and OpenAI’s rates. SpaceXAI pitched Grok 4.5 as a cost-cutter, claiming four times better token efficiency. Perplexity fine-tuned Zhipu’s open-weight GLM 5.2 and says it matches Claude Opus 4.8 at 34% of the cost. Alibaba’s Qwen crossed one billion downloads, overtaking Meta’s Llama as the most-used open-source family. One roundup drew the split plainly: OpenAI and Anthropic hold the top benchmarks while Meta and SpaceXAI undercut them on cost.

Washington and the money moved the same day. The Commerce Department reclassified the UAE into a privileged export tier — license-free AI chips, satellites, and some military items — while one investigation argued that hardware-focused export rules have already let restricted Chinese entities reach US models. Fortune counted a record $412.7 billion of US venture capital deployed in the first half — 86% of it to AI, 91% in deals over $100 million — and Big Tech is borrowing for the buildout, with data-center bond issuance approaching $400 billion. The labor answer came from the docks: Australian port workers are demanding a 28-hour week as DP World’s automation plans threaten more than a thousand jobs.
