AI News for October 28, 2026

Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon, Nvidia expands its Nemotron lineup and locks in chip capacity, Anthropic faces mounting copyright pressure, and Nevada unions confront AI’s split jobs ledger. All 77 stories from the day, below.

October 28, 202677 stories · 79 source links · 8 categories

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon as its strongest frontier model yet, claiming leading intelligence and cybersecurity benchmark results while limiting the first rollout to vetted cyber defenders. DeepMind also released Genie 4 with audio, improved physics, longer scene coherence, and Gemini-assisted planning. Nvidia moved in the other direction on size: Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B targets local voice assistants on RTX and Jetson hardware, while Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe covers as many as 40 languages. The model race was stretching from frontier cyber systems to software meant to run close to the user.

The hardware and legal bills arrived alongside the models. Nvidia secured capacity across substrates, packaging, testing, and optics through 2028, with reported supply commitments rising to $279 billion. Anthropic, meanwhile, warned investors that adverse copyright rulings could bring licensing costs, injunctions, or model retraining. Rights holders for 274 Elvis Presley compositions filed a separate copyright suit against Anthropic, and Microsoft and Epic Games issued takedowns against AI-assisted clones of their games. Building with AI is getting easier; deciding what it may absorb or reproduce is not.

The consequences were already visible in workplaces and public life. Nevada unions are split between data-center construction jobs and AI-driven displacement in hospitality and transportation. Teachers are replacing some take-home work with classroom writing and oral exams to counter generative-AI use. Ahead of the midterms, a Cybernews analysis found Democrats were targeted negatively more often in reported political deepfakes, while a 61,000-person survey found sharp regional differences in optimism about AI-created jobs. AI’s benefits and burdens are landing unevenly, and institutions are rewriting their rules in response.

What People Are Asking

What is Gemini 4 Carbon?

Google describes Gemini 4 Carbon as its strongest frontier model yet and claims leading results on intelligence and cybersecurity benchmarks. Its rollout begins with vetted cyber defenders before a broader release. blog.google →

What is Nvidia Nemotron 3.5 ASR?

It is a free open-weight speech-recognition model supporting 40 languages, recordings up to an hour, and as many as eight speakers. It handles recorded audio rather than live streams. cryptobriefing.com →

Can New York lawyers use AI in court filings without disclosing it?

Yes. New York’s court system says lawyers may use generative AI in filings without disclosure, but attorneys remain fully responsible for checking accuracy. hoodline.com →

Why does Anthropic say copyright law could threaten its business?

Anthropic warned in its SEC filing that unfavorable fair-use rulings could produce large licensing costs, injunctions, or even require model retraining. The warning comes amid expanding copyright litigation. indiatoday.in →

Is there a Senate bill that could block AI model releases?

Not yet, based on the available report. One aide-sourced account describes a draft that would impose a duty of care, permit federal model-release blocks, and preempt state laws, but says no bill text exists. orcarouter.ai →

Why are Nevada unions divided over AI?

Data-center construction is creating union trade jobs near Reno, while autonomous vehicles, robot bartenders, and AI call centers threaten hospitality and transportation work. That split is shaping union politics ahead of Nevada’s Nov. 3 elections. allwork.space →

Who is being targeted by political deepfakes before the 2026 midterms?

A Cybernews analysis of 104 reported political deepfakes found Democrats were targeted negatively more often than Republicans. It also attributed more than 40% of the examined synthetic media to Trump and GOP-linked sources. cybernews.com →

How are teachers changing assignments because of AI?

Teachers surveyed by Reuters said they are reducing take-home work and moving toward in-class writing and oral exams. The changes are intended to limit undisclosed generative-AI use in student assessments. 933thedrive.com →
