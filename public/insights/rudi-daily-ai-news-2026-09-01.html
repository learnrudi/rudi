AI News for September 1, 2026

Brazil moves toward new rules for AI-generated campaign content, Instagram ties reach to labels on synthetic profiles, Nvidia reportedly puts $3.5 billion into MediaTek, and Alibaba Cloud launches Qwen3.8-Max internationally. All 35 stories from the day, below.

September 1, 202635 stories · 10 categories

Brazil’s top electoral court is set to restrict AI-generated campaign content, addressing deepfakes, mandatory labels, and chatbot behavior ahead of the October elections. Instagram will require AI-generated influencer profiles to carry labels or face reduced algorithmic reach. And MIT is proposing oral and in-person assessments in place of take-home exams. Election officials, a social platform, and a university are setting different rules for the same practical problem: deciding when synthetic content can be trusted.

One report says Nvidia invested $3.5 billion in MediaTek to integrate custom silicon through NVLink Fusion as hyperscalers pursue their own chips. Another says Andreessen Horowitz expanded its growth fund to $9.6 billion alongside a projection that global AI investment will reach $1 trillion in 2026; that trillion-dollar figure remains a projection, not a result. Gridsight raised $36 million to expand software for managing grid capacity as data centers and EVs drive electricity demand, while Alibaba Cloud launched Qwen3.8-Max internationally with 2.4 trillion parameters, a one-million-token context window, and multimodal capabilities. The model race still runs through chips, capital, and the power grid.

Consulting firms face pressure as clients use AI to cut costs and reduce demand for traditional IT services. Meanwhile, a Zoom-commissioned survey found that 61% of knowledge workers dread returning from vacation, prompting Zoom to introduce AI-driven out-of-office workflows. AI appears here on both sides of office work: cutting the services companies buy and managing the inboxes employees return to.
