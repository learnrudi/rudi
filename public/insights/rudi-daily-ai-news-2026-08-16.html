August 16, 202656 stories · 10 categories

The Atlantic reports that the Trump administration weakened CISA, leaving U.S. infrastructure vulnerable to Iranian cyberattacks. Help Net Security’s weekly review adds a 17-month exposure of Salesforce and ServiceNow portals and an exploited Metabase zero-day, and Check Point Research reports 93 active ransomware groups, a 33% annual increase in victims, and AI tools accelerating attack development. In Australia, ASIC reports a sharp rise in deepfake scams that impersonate public figures for false investment endorsements. The warning signs now run from national infrastructure to the investment pitch in someone’s feed.

Capital is piling into AI compute. A 24/7 Wall St. analysis examines SpaceX’s potential $500 billion data-center buildout and the inference economics behind it, while Riot Platforms has signed a $9 billion compute deal with Anthropic as Bitcoin miners pivot toward AI data centers. Another analysis ties higher consumer-electronics prices and reduced specifications to AI-driven memory demand, and Mint reports that Indian chip startups face a funding gap between design and commercial production. At the software end of that demand, Global Times says, citing Hugging Face, that Alibaba’s Qwen has surpassed 3 billion downloads. The spending is arriving before the supply chain is comfortable: memory prices are rising, and chip designers still need funding to manufacture.

Brazil’s 2026 campaign opened with rules requiring labels on AI-generated content, prohibiting deepfakes, and regulating digital ad boosting. Kenya’s proposed Intellectual Property Bill would create a new authority and address AI-assisted inventions. In Wyoming, a woman is suing xAI after alleging that Grok generated thousands of explicit images from her childhood photo, while one Spanish report says Hollywood studios are replacing actors with AI clones in microdramas without compensation or union protection. The disputes are political, too: a Washington Post analysis found that AI and data centers have become a top issue for U.S. candidates, with Democrats emphasizing harms and Republicans national security.
