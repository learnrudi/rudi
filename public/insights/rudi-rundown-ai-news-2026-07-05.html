AI News for July 5, 2026

Newspaper publishers take OpenAI and Microsoft to court over training data, the NDAA drops its AI export-control provisions, and the UN opens its global AI governance dialogue. All 48 stories from the day, below.

July 5, 202648 stories · 9 categories

The copyright fight became a class action. Reports describe a newspaper lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft over AI training — one puts it at 400 U.S. newspapers, another describes a coalition of 35 publishers. The reported scale differs; the direction doesn’t. Authors separately pressed a $75 million suit against Anthropic over pirated books. And in Geneva, the UN’s Global Dialogue on AI Governance opened with expert warnings of “catastrophic harm.”

Export controls lost a round in Congress. The FY2026 NDAA dropped its AI export-control provisions after executive-branch opposition, leaving the rules with Commerce — even as Washington moved to control allied access to frontier AI. On the product side, Microsoft announced Copilot Cowork, an autonomous agent that executes multi-step workflows inside Microsoft 365.

The money data kept saying the same thing, louder. Global VC hit $510 billion in the first half, 43% of it to OpenAI and Anthropic, with Gulf sovereign funds emerging as price-setters. Nearly 40 AI startups reached unicorn status in six months, led by Promethus’s $12 billion Series B. And tech money kept flowing into AI-focused midterm campaigns while voter backlash over jobs and costs grew.
