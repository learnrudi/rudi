AI News for September 16, 2026

TypeSafe AI emerged with $40 million; a daily roundup reported Google's Gemini 3.8 Live launch; Tech Insider covered OpenAI's cross-lab safety talks; and Law Commentary reported New York's seizure of 12 deepfake pornography domains. All 40 stories from the day, below.

September 16, 202640 stories · 41 source links · 8 categories

A daily roundup reports Google's Gemini 3.8 Live launch. Meanwhile, TypeSafe AI emerged from stealth with $40 million and says its Jev model can deliver frontier intelligence for software up to 100 times faster and cheaper. A ten-company funding roundup counted $471 million in disclosed dollar-denominated capital across four startups, alongside separate rounds in Japanese yen, Chinese yuan and British pounds. These reports span voice interaction, software decisions and funding for specialized technical work.

Tech Insider reports that OpenAI confirmed on September 15 active talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind about a shared frontier-AI safety standards framework. Law Commentary reports that New York prosecutors seized 12 deepfake pornography domains on September 14, involving images of about 1,200 people allegedly depicted without consent. Publishers are applying pressure through the courts, too: a legal blog, using its own count, calls the new Times Publishing complaint the 143rd copyright lawsuit against AI companies, while a plaintiff-side announcement says Platkin LLP now represents more than 550 publications in litigation against OpenAI and Microsoft. And an arXiv paper shows a more technical failure point: altered collision meshes that leave robot policies behaving normally in simulation but failing in the real world. No single institution owns the whole problem: labs are negotiating standards, prosecutors are removing sites, publishers are suing, and researchers are probing robot assets.

What People Are Asking

How common are AI-generated political ads in the 2026 midterms?

NPR reports that the Wesleyan Media Project tracked at least 164 political ads produced or enhanced by AI nationwide this year. Much of the coverage concerns parody or satire, although some viewers can still mistake it for real footage. NPR →

Are OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google working on shared AI safety standards?

Tech Insider reports that OpenAI publicly confirmed talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on September 15 about a shared frontier-AI safety standards framework. These are talks, not an established standards body. tech-insider.org →

Did New York take down AI deepfake porn sites?

Law Commentary reports that New York prosecutors seized 12 domains on September 14 over alleged nonconsensual deepfake pornography involving about 1,200 people. Prosecutors described it as the largest known seizure of AI-generated celebrity deepfake websites; the report says no individual criminal charges had been announced. Law Commentary →

How many publications are in the copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft?

A plaintiff-side announcement published by Insider NJ says Platkin LLP now represents more than 550 publications after 26 additional publishers joined litigation against OpenAI and Microsoft. This is the law firm's reported coalition size, not a count of separate lawsuits or a finding of infringement. Insider NJ →

What is TypeSafe AI’s Jev model?

Jev is a machine-native model launched by TypeSafe AI for software work. The company claims it can provide frontier intelligence up to 100 times faster and cheaper; that performance claim comes from the startup itself. Tech Startups →

What is a collision mesh poisoning attack on robots?

Collision Mesh Poisoning modifies only the collision mesh of a 3D asset, allowing robot policies to behave normally in simulation but fail or create safety risks in the real world. The paper reports that existing defenses are insufficient. arXiv →

Why can AI preference scores differ from real user engagement?

An arXiv paper found that LLMs over-value explicit logical structure, while real engagement tracks affect and expression more closely. Its proposed OMRA method reduced that “logic overbinding” gap by 54.4%. arXiv →

How much startup funding was reported on September 16, 2026?

The ten-company Tech Startups roundup counted $471 million in explicitly dollar-denominated capital across four startups. It separately reported rounds in Japanese yen, Chinese yuan and British pounds; the $471 million is not a total converted across all ten companies. Tech Startups →
