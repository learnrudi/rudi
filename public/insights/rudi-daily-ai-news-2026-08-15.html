One report says AI data-center spending is approaching $1 trillion even as power constraints, labor shortages, and regulatory hurdles create a supply crisis. Lenovo’s CIO Playbook says 96% of enterprises plan to increase AI investment, while governance lags. The spending plans are colliding with the less glamorous work of power, networking, staffing, and governance.

California lawmakers are trying to regulate a flood of AI-generated political campaign content. South Korean lawmakers introduced bills that would charge employers for AI-related job cuts, shifting the proposed cost toward the companies making workforce decisions. Education policy is moving too: Singapore’s Ministry of Education introduced compulsory AI-literacy training for primary, secondary, and junior-college teachers, while Google opened Gemini in Classroom to students of all ages, prompting transparency and privacy concerns among U.S. educators. Employers, campaigns, and classrooms are all getting rules alongside the tools.

Google says its SL2T sign-language-to-text model will power new Pixel 11 features using privacy-focused pose-landmark processing. OpenAI announced cybersecurity-focused models and access tiers, and Anthropic updated Claude Code with cross-session messaging, self-hosted environments, and auto mode as the default. Outside developer tools, Alpha Schools plans a two-hour academic model in Greenwich with no teachers or homework, as a report describes growing AI use in Hollywood despite continuing resistance. The releases arrived beside harder arguments about access, oversight, and replacement.
