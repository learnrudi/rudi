AI News for October 23, 2026

Music publishers sue Anthropic over lyric training, a GAO report finds gaps in Pentagon responsible-AI practices, China proposes new AI technology export controls, and NBC News traces nearly $137 million in AI-produced or AI-enhanced political ads. All 59 stories from the day, below.

October 23, 202659 stories · 60 source links · 10 categories

A GAO report found that some Pentagon AI weapons projects do not consistently follow the department’s six responsible-AI tenets, while the office overseeing that guidance is severely understaffed. In electoral politics, NBC News reports nearly $137 million spent on at least 220 AI-produced or AI-enhanced ads—a single-outlet count worth treating as reported—while two Democratic lawmakers say Russia is running an AI-accelerated disinformation campaign against pro-Ukraine candidates and more voters are turning to chatbots for candidate and ballot information. The common problem is verification: AI is already inside defense programs, political persuasion, and voter research while the institutions checking it are trying to keep up.

Music publishers including Universal Music Group are suing Anthropic over training Claude on song lyrics, with Anthropic arguing fair use. Elsewhere, a federal appeals panel overturned a Mississippi DEI order after finding that AI used to help draft it hallucinated case law and cited evidence outside the record. China, meanwhile, proposed adding 40 technologies, including 23 related to AI, to its export-control catalogue and issued a broader policy joining frontier-technology commercialization with sandbox regulation. Training data, judicial citations, and frontier technologies crossing borders all landed in somebody’s rulebook.

Uber is expanding an agentic work platform to more than 30,000 employees after a 1,000-employee pilot, backed by training, approved integrations, and an internal skills library. Universities including Bentley, MIT, WPI, and Northeastern are racing to offer undergraduate AI majors, with many programs also requiring AI ethics coursework. The investment signal is less exuberant: Skift’s tracker says AI-native travel funding rounds fell to 12 from 25, while one $1 billion deal supplied most of the sector’s reported $1.38 billion total. And in law, a roundup describes a mandatory AI trainee seat, proposed attorney-AI rules, and a court-software cyberattack. Employers and universities are building AI fluency into work and degrees before the workplace rules have settled.

What People Are Asking

Are Pentagon AI weapons projects following responsible AI guidelines?

Not consistently. A GAO report found that some projects do not follow all six Pentagon responsible-AI tenets, while the office overseeing the guidance is severely understaffed. NPR →

How much has been spent on AI political ads this election cycle?

NBC News reports nearly $137 million was spent on at least 220 AI-produced or AI-enhanced political ads this election cycle, with few disclosing their AI use. Treat those figures as a reported count rather than a settled total. NBC News →

Is Anthropic being sued over using song lyrics to train Claude?

Yes. Music publishers including Universal Music Group are suing Anthropic over training Claude on song lyrics, while Anthropic argues that the training qualifies as fair use. Seoul Economic Daily →

Did AI hallucinations affect a Mississippi DEI ruling?

Yes. A federal appeals panel overturned the district judge’s order after finding that AI used to help draft it hallucinated case law and cited evidence that was not in the record. Fox News →

Is China adding AI technologies to its export-control list?

It is proposing to. China’s MOFCOM and MOST proposed adding 40 technologies, including 23 related to AI, to the export-control catalogue and also updated UAV controls. Expeditors →

Is Russia using AI disinformation in the U.S. midterm elections?

According to two Democratic lawmakers, a classified intelligence briefing revealed an AI-accelerated Russian campaign targeting pro-Ukraine candidates ahead of the U.S. midterms. That claim rests on the lawmakers’ account of the briefing. The Kyiv Independent →

How many Uber employees are getting AI productivity tools?

More than 30,000 employees globally are set to receive the agentic work platform, along with training, approved integrations, and an internal AI skills library after a 1,000-employee pilot. HRKatha →

Are colleges offering AI as an undergraduate major?

Yes. A growing group that includes Bentley, MIT, WPI, and Northeastern is offering or developing undergraduate AI majors, and many of the programs also require AI ethics coursework. WBUR →
