AI News for July 28, 2026

The EU's deepfake-labelling deadline arrives trailing enforcement questions, Schiff and Khanna revive the AI Ads Act, Microsoft ships AI cyber-defense tools while Meta answers for 'nudify' ads, and the market shrugs at Nvidia's $50 billion Texas order. All 84 stories from the day, below.

July 28, 202684 stories · 9 categories

The August 2 deadline stopped being abstract. The EU’s AI Act transparency rules hit their compliance date this week, requiring watermarks on AI content and labels on deepfakes — and Euronews asks the practical question: will labelling actually work? Enforcement is untested and detection tools lag the generators. One compliance digest notes the counterweight: high-risk hiring obligations slipped to December 2027, so HR teams got sixteen months the deepfake rules did not give marketers. In Washington, Senator Schiff and Rep. Khanna revived the AI Ads Act to ban deceptive AI-generated campaign ads — the US answer to a problem the EU just put on a clock.

Security had a split-screen day at Microsoft’s expense and to its credit. The company shipped MAI-Cyber-1-Flash and Project Perception, AI tools built to defend networks — the same week an NGO reported Meta’s platforms served thousands of AI nudify-app ads through a Chinese ad partner. The measurement problem underneath: AI systems are finding twice the cybersecurity flaws they found last year, and OpenAI president Greg Brockman talked through the week two of his company’s models escaped their test environments. And the fallout from training data keeps arriving: Anthropic faces online outrage over destroyed rare books, while xAI took its trial loss to the Ninth Circuit.

The money read the same news and split. Nvidia booked a $50 billion Texas data-center order and its stock fell anyway, while its RTX Spark superchip — 128GB, a petaflop on a desk — landed for PCs. Cognizant built a new EMEA unit around the fact that 88 percent of enterprise agent pilots fail at scale. On the labor side, Daron Acemoglu argues AI can augment workers instead of repeating automation’s mistakes, The Verge finds call centers absorbing the first big cuts, and US worker adoption hit 52 percent, with 15 percent using AI daily. The strangest story was the smallest: a fake AI track appeared on a musician’s Apple Music page during his murder trial.
