AI News for October 9, 2026

USA TODAY Co. brings a $250 million-plus copyright case against OpenAI, Google opens SynthID Detector worldwide, and a Gaza charity plans AI monitoring in classrooms. Meanwhile, funding, data-center plans, and model releases keep the buildout moving. All 39 stories from the day, below.

October 9, 202639 stories · 9 categories

USA TODAY Co. and 19 affiliated publications sued OpenAI in Manhattan federal court, seeking more than $250 million and an injunction over the alleged unlicensed use of their articles. A US charity plans to monitor conversations in its Gaza classrooms for speech it considers hateful or inciting, raising privacy, safety, and ethical concerns. And Google expanded SynthID Detector worldwide, giving English-language users a way to check media for AI watermarks from Google and participating companies.

Money and infrastructure kept moving. A weekly funding roundup reports $5 billion in debt financing for Waymo and a $500 million raise for Manus, while Gorilla Technology signed a non-binding deal for its first US data center. On the product side, an AI roundup covers Mistral’s Large 4 preview, Reflection AI’s Beam debut, and Google’s staged Gemini 4 Argon release, and Harvey says it added new legal-search, document, spreadsheet, and mobile-agent features. The day’s launches are varied; the demand for models, compute, and specialized tools is not.

What People Are Asking

Why is USA TODAY suing OpenAI?

USA TODAY Co. and 19 affiliated publications allege that OpenAI trained ChatGPT on hundreds of thousands of their articles without a license. They are seeking more than $250 million and an injunction. runtimewire.com →

Can anyone use Google’s AI content detector?

Google has expanded SynthID Detector worldwide in English. It checks images, video, and audio for AI watermarks from Google and participating companies, including OpenAI, NVIDIA, and Kakao. nairametrics.com →

Is AI being used to monitor classrooms in Gaza?

A US charity plans to use AI to monitor classroom conversations for speech it considers hateful or inciting. The reported plan raises ethical, safety, and privacy concerns. mendocinobeacon.com →

What is Mistral Large 4?

A weekly roundup reports that Mistral previewed Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter open model. The same roundup also covers Reflection AI’s open-weight Beam debut and Google’s staged Gemini 4 Argon release to cyber defenders. trewknowledge.com →

How much US data-center capacity is Gorilla Technology planning?

Gorilla Technology signed a non-binding letter of intent to acquire a US data center with about 6 MW of current capacity and a path to 36 MW. The company is targeting more than 100 MW of US AI infrastructure within 12 to 18 months. stocktwits.com →

How much financing did Waymo raise?

A weekly startup-funding roundup reports that Waymo secured $5 billion in debt financing. The same roundup says Manus raised $500 million after exiting Meta. analyticsinsight.net →

What new features did Harvey release in October 2026?

Harvey says its October releases include a connector library, agentic search across SharePoint and Google Drive, a LexisNexis-powered motion-to-dismiss workflow, Excel formula understanding, mobile agents, and access to additional models. harvey.ai →

Can skodun run AI code reviews without API keys?

The skodun repository documents a local code-review workflow that reuses existing AI coding CLI subscriptions instead of API keys. It includes adversarial review passes, trust gates, and a triage ledger. github.com →

Can an AI influencer make $10,000 a month?

The guide describes building an AI influencer character and outlines four revenue streams that it says could produce $10,000 per month. That figure is presented as a possible outcome, not a guaranteed income. theblueprintai.substack.com →
