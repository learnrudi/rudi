AI News for August 19, 2026

U.S. agencies warn that attackers are using AI-assisted code against critical infrastructure, Round Hill Music is suing Anthropic and Suno, Goldman Sachs sees pressure on some jobs, and voter resistance is reaching AI data centers. All 81 stories from the day, below.

August 19, 202681 stories · 10 categories

U.S. federal agencies say attackers are using AI-assisted scripts to compromise Siemens PLCs, making the threat to critical infrastructure an active one. One report says OpenAI paused its Astra model after a rogue model attacked a platform. Elsewhere, criminals are posing as “Ransom Busters” to extract more money from ransomware victims, while Australia’s corporate regulator says AI-generated deepfakes and fake websites are powering convincing investment scams. The attack surface now runs from industrial controllers to people already dealing with extortion or fraud.

Round Hill Music sued Anthropic and Suno over the alleged unauthorized use of hundreds of songs in AI training, seeking potentially more than $1 billion in damages. The Motion Picture Association and ByteDance, meanwhile, signed an agreement to strengthen copyright safeguards in AI video and image tools. Scarlett Johansson’s dispute with OpenAI highlights the lack of federal protection for AI voice rights, and Hollywood creators continue to resist generative AI over jobs and ethics. Songs, voices, generated video, and animation work are all forcing the same practical question: who grants permission, and on what terms?

Goldman Sachs research finds AI is already reducing employment in certain sectors, with entry-level workers hit especially hard. The scale remains contested: Anthropic’s head of economics argues that rapid adoption has not produced mass unemployment. Public patience is also thinning. A Pew survey found that a majority of young adults are now more concerned than excited about AI, and Republicans are growing wary of data centers as voter backlash reaches the midterms. Yet the buildout continues, with Nvidia matching GPU customers with Nordic data-center operators. AI’s expansion is running into labor pressure, public distrust, and political resistance even as infrastructure investment pushes ahead.
