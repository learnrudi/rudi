AI News for November 3, 2026

The UK warns that AI-powered malware is likely to threaten critical infrastructure by 2028, Anthropic commits $895.4 billion to compute through 2045, Gemini 4 Carbon takes a narrow benchmark lead, and AI-enhanced political advertising draws scrutiny. All 104 stories from the day, below.

November 3, 2026104 stories · 105 source links · 10 categories

The UK's cyber agency warned that AI-powered malware is highly likely to be used in ransomware attacks against power stations, airports, and hospitals by 2028. The warning puts a date on the risk while leaving the attacks themselves in the future. In the political arena, campaigns have spent more than $187 million on nearly 300 AI-enhanced ads, prompting scrutiny of platform enforcement amid uneven state laws and stalled federal bills. OpenAI opened an election operations center as it and Anthropic introduced safeguards for the 2026 midterms.

The infrastructure race kept getting more expensive. Anthropic has committed $895.4 billion through 2045 to computing power and chip deals ahead of a potential $100 billion IPO. Meanwhile, AMD reported 45% revenue growth as data-center sales nearly doubled, and Google released Gemini 4 Carbon with a one-point benchmark lead over Claude Opus 5.5. That lead, including reported hallucination and recall results, rests on benchmark claims rather than a settled verdict.

Courts, publishers, and employers kept working out who captures AI's value. The 10th Circuit ruled that using AI does not by itself justify cutting a law firm's fees, while four newspapers sued Brave over AI answers, paywalled journalism, and ad blocking. At work, an October survey found 60% of U.S. workers say getting good AI output takes at least as much effort as training a new hire. AI may save labor, but somebody still has to supervise it—and managers are often getting that assignment.

Does a lawyer's use of AI justify lower legal fees?

No—not by itself. The 10th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals upheld a roughly $115,000 award and ruled that AI assistance alone does not make legal fees unreasonable. Court Cast →

What is China's full-lifecycle AI safety law?

China is accelerating legislation intended to put AI development and use under safety oversight across the full lifecycle. The planned framework covers major model developers and addresses data and computing resources. TokenPost →
