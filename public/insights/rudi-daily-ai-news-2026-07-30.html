The frontier went retail. OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 launched publicly as a three-tier family — Luna, Terra, Sol — after a 13-day restricted preview requested by the US government. Meta answered on price: Muse Spark 1.1 arrives priced up to 83 percent below Claude Opus 4.8, the company’s first paid API and a shift away from open weights. xAI shipped Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0, a lower-latency voice model available via API, and Microsoft stopped pretending otherwise: Satya Nadella is openly pitching Microsoft’s own models against OpenAI and Anthropic while the company plans to merge Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Copilot Cowork, and its Autopilot agents into one app. Four labs, one day, and the sales pitch has moved from capability to price and control.

The security story kept compounding. After an AI system weakened the HAWK post-quantum algorithm in 60 hours, South Korea certified a hybrid post-quantum encryption module — a hedge against exactly this class of break. A Darden School analysis draws the governance lesson from last week’s agent incident: rapid disclosure and collaborative remediation matter more than containment theater. And the EU’s clock ran out: machine-readable AI content labels and deepfake marking become mandatory August 2 under Article 50, with fines up to €15 million — a practitioner’s guide maps what still takes effect despite the Digital Omnibus deferrals.

The classroom and the lab both pushed back. OpenAI is giving 100,000 scientists free ChatGPT access under a $250 million program — which critics read as a strategic land grab on research workflows, in one analysis’s framing. High-school students from all 50 states drafted their own rules for AI in schools to hand to administrators, while UK universities are abandoning AI-detection tools over accuracy problems and bias against international students. And the jobs AI isn’t killing are pouring concrete for it: electricians, plumbers, and carpenters are in bidding wars to build the data centers.
