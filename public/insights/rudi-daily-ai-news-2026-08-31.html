AI News for August 31, 2026

Music publishers accused Anthropic of mass copyright infringement, CNN described bipartisan midterm backlash against data centers, the Pentagon expanded its internal AI platform to ChatGPT and Grok, and a German survey attributed 37% of corporate cyberattacks to foreign spies. All 58 stories from the day, below.

August 31, 202658 stories · 10 categories

Anthropic’s training practices moved into court: 35 publishers, including Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music, filed suit over allegedly acquired copyrighted works, while another account says music publishers are seeking billions over alleged mass infringement in training Claude. The surrounding fight over synthetic media was broader: Instagram is restricting reach for AI-influencer accounts that lack proper labels, actor Shalini Pandey condemned a deepfake video of herself and urged people not to share it, and a federal appeals court upheld First Amendment protection for privately possessing AI-generated child sexual abuse imagery that depicts no real children. Law, platform rules, and the people being imitated are setting different boundaries for generated media.

Data-center expansion also became an electoral argument. CNN reports candidates from both parties are spending millions on ads attacking AI infrastructure over energy costs and local effects, and President Trump warned opposing communities could end up “backwards and poor” as Republican strategists worried about midterm backlash. At the federal level, the Pentagon broadened its internal AI platform to ChatGPT Mil and Grok for Government; across the Pacific, Korea’s new AI policy chief warned about U.S. export controls and promised support for domestic AI capabilities. The infrastructure race is now being negotiated through local costs, national security, and access to frontier models.

A German survey says foreign spies were behind 37% of cyberattacks on companies, five times their share three years earlier, with AI escalating the risk, while Financial Stability Board chair Andrew Bailey warned G20 finance ministries about cyber threats and concentrated AI suppliers. The Trump administration’s Project Watershed 250 is testing AI and private-sector cybersecurity tools on Texas water systems before a planned national expansion. Schools and workers supplied a different measure of strain: one education report put UK AI adoption at 94% but cheating detection at only 40%, and a Resume Genius report found that more than half of laid-off workers suspected AI played a role despite employers offering other explanations.
