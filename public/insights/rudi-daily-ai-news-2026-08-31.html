AI News for August 31, 2026

Music publishers accuse Anthropic of mass copyright infringement, data-center backlash reaches campaign advertising, the Financial Stability Board chair warns frontier AI could threaten financial stability, and a German survey reports that foreign spies account for 37% of corporate cyberattacks. All 34 stories from the day, below.

August 31, 202634 stories · 9 categories

Thirty-five music publishers, including Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music, have filed a copyright lawsuit accusing Anthropic of illegally acquiring works to train its models. A federal appeals court ruled that private possession of AI-generated child sexual abuse imagery not depicting real minors is protected by the First Amendment, raising concern that existing law has fallen behind the technology. Infrastructure is running into politics, too: candidates from both parties are spending millions on ads attacking data centers over energy costs and local effects. Meanwhile, Financial Stability Board chair Andrew Bailey warned G20 finance ministries that frontier AI poses systemic financial risks through cyber threats and supplier concentration. South Korea’s new AI policy chief warned that U.S. export controls could restrict access to frontier models, while India’s government plans to adapt its EASE banking reforms for public-sector general insurers to improve technology adoption and operational efficiency.

A German survey attributed 37% of cyberattacks on companies to foreign spies and said the share had risen fivefold in three years. In Texas, Project Watershed 250 is testing AI and private-sector cybersecurity tools to protect water systems, with nationwide expansion planned. Workers are reading the same technological pressure through their paychecks: a Resume Genius report found that more than half of laid-off workers suspect AI contributed to their job cuts, even when employers gave other explanations. Bollywood actor Shalini Pandey condemned a deepfake video featuring her and urged people not to share it. On the product side, one report says Meta plans a consumer agent platform called Hatch and a flagship model called Watermelon. A weekly regulation briefing gathered policy developments spanning the United States, Europe, Asia, and Africa, a useful measure of how many jurisdictions are now writing their own terms for AI.
