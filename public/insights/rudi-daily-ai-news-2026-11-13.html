AI News for November 13, 2026

Jensen Huang’s resignation puts Nvidia’s funding role and China chip exports under pressure, Google rolls out Gemini 4 Hydrogen, nonprofits ask a court to freeze frontier AI development, and Whitehall turns back to face-to-face interviews after a surge in AI-generated applications. November 13, 2026107 stories · 11 categories

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO amid reports of a Chinese intelligence targeting effort and congressional scrutiny of AI chip export controls. Venture capitalists and founders told TechCrunch that Nvidia’s 105 investments this year are expected to slow under interim CEO Colette Kress, potentially depressing dealmaking, valuations, and the neocloud and open-source ecosystem. Reuters documented more than $3.2 billion in Chinese data-center projects facing delays, supply disruption, or redesign because they were built around export-restricted Nvidia chips, while congressional leaders reported growing bipartisan support for tighter export limits. A nonprofit also found at least 400 fake accounts promoting defenses of Huang and support for Nvidia chip exports, with at least 260 accounts deactivated or suspended on X.

Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a speed-focused model adapted from Gemini 4 Carbon and rolling out globally in Search and the Gemini app. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, claiming gains in speed, cost, coding, reasoning, vision, and cybersecurity. Perplexity released Apache-licensed Decider v1.3 and Flash v1.3 classifiers, reporting 91.6% mean accuracy across 63 tasks. Against those releases, four nonprofits asked a San Francisco Superior Court judge to halt development of frontier models more capable than current systems. The technical race and the legal effort to stop it now occupy the same news cycle.

Whitehall plans more face-to-face interviews and less reliance on written statements after AI-generated applications produced a growing number of unqualified candidates. In Bournemouth, The Guardian found anxiety about AI threatening back-office and entry-level finance roles. The redesign inside companies remains uneven: Capgemini research says 59% of banks and insurers are piloting or deploying multi-agent AI, but only 10% have enterprise-wide orchestration, and Lenovo found that eight in ten IT decision-makers see the digital workplace as critical to AI transformation even though only 10% have fundamentally redesigned it. Employers are changing the gates around hiring faster than they are rebuilding work itself.
