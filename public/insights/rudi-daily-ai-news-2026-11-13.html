AI News for November 13, 2026

Jensen Huang’s resignation collides with a new fight over AI-chip exports, nonprofits ask a California court to freeze frontier-model development, Google launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen, and Whitehall turns back to in-person interviews after a surge in AI-generated applications. All 72 stories from the day, below.

November 13, 202672 stories · 11 categories

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned amid reports of a Chinese intelligence targeting effort and congressional scrutiny. In the fallout, congressional leaders described growing bipartisan support for tighter AI-chip export limits to China. A Reuters-based report says more than $3.2 billion in Chinese data-center projects built around restricted Nvidia chips now face delays, disruption, or redesign, while a nonprofit found at least 400 fake accounts amplifying defenses of Huang and support for Nvidia exports; at least 260 were deactivated or suspended on X. The leadership crisis is now entangled with export policy, infrastructure already built around restricted chips, and an online influence campaign.

Four nonprofits asked a California court to freeze frontier-model development beyond current capabilities at five AI companies. Model and infrastructure expansion continued around that challenge: Google announced the speed-optimized Gemini 4 Hydrogen for Search and the Gemini app, a release tracker reported Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, and Nscale said its active GPU fleet doubled to about 50,000 in two and a half months. The lawsuit targets further development just as model vendors and compute suppliers keep expanding.

Employers are adjusting the human side of AI use. Whitehall plans more face-to-face interviews after AI-generated applications produced a growing number of unqualified candidates. In Bournemouth, JP Morgan’s 5,300-person campus and the surrounding finance sector face anxiety over automation of back-office and entry-level roles. Meanwhile, a Lenovo report found eight in ten IT decision-makers call the digital workplace critical to AI transformation, but only 10% have fundamentally redesigned it. And at a Hollywood panel, SAG-AFTRA and Vermillio laid out opposing approaches to digital replicas, consent, compensation, and unauthorized use. Across hiring, finance, workplace design, and performance, institutions are deciding where AI changes the work and where a person still has to be present, protected, or paid.
