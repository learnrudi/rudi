AI News for September 18, 2026

One report projects next-generation Nvidia and AMD consumer graphics cards slipping to 2028, Alibaba's Qwen team launches Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, Jev offers probability outputs for automation, and Anthropic documents malicious uses of Claude. All 52 stories from the day, below.

September 18, 202652 stories · 53 source links · 9 categories

Publication note: This edition was recovered and reviewed on September 21, 2026, after a publishing interruption. It covers reporting associated with the edition date and was published on September 21. One report says advanced-memory and grid-power shortages are delaying data centers, while a GDDR7 shortage could push next-generation Nvidia and AMD consumer graphics cards to 2028—a single-source projection worth treating cautiously. A funding roundup, meanwhile, highlights Crusoe’s $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion valuation as evidence that capital is concentrating on scarce compute. Researchers are attacking the same constraint from inside the stack: the Weave paper reports 2.89x MoE-layer and 1.33x end-to-end speedups on four H100 GPUs, while MarkTechPost reports that Alibaba's Qwen team launched a model with a one-million-token context for audio-video understanding and tool use. Compute pressure is shaping both hardware schedules and model design.

TechCrunch reports that TypeSafe AI’s Jev produces calibrated probabilities instead of text, targeting lower-cost, faster automation without generating free-form text. The probability outputs still require judgment about confidence and possible errors. The Times of India reports that Anthropic opened a Bay Area biology lab for physical biological experiments, days after its CEO urged slower frontier-model development. One market report says AI healthcare stocks rose after Novo Nordisk’s Anthropic collaboration and a clearer Tempus AI reimbursement outlook. In the labs themselves, C&EN reports that software vendors and chemical executives are still working through scientist skepticism and trust. The science may be moving faster than institutional confidence.

Anthropic’s September threat report, as covered by TechBarrista, describes disrupted uses of Claude involving fabricated dating personas, mass-surveillance development, and a hotel WiFi compromise. Adoption looks less dramatic inside ordinary workplaces: a University of Konstanz survey of 1,105 employees conducted in May 2026 found workplace AI use had risen only slightly, to 38%. A separate cross-sectional study of 106 Portuguese professionals associated higher organizational support with a weaker link between AI insecurity and job-security worries, but found no significant buffering effect for career satisfaction. Outside the office, creators are beginning to sell brands on their influence over answers generated by LLMs. AI is changing the sales pitch before it has become routine at work.

What People Are Asking

Are next-generation Nvidia and AMD consumer graphics cards delayed until 2028?

La Opinión reports that a GDDR7 memory shortage could push next-generation Nvidia and AMD consumer graphics cards to 2028. Treat that as a single-source projection, not a confirmed schedule for all GPUs or AI accelerators. laopinionline.ar →

What is Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash?

MarkTechPost reports that Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is Alibaba Qwen's model with a one-million-token context for audio-video understanding and tool use, offered through a hosted API. It accepts text, images, audio and video, and returns text. marktechpost.com →

What is TypeSafe AI’s Jev model?

TechCrunch reports that Jev is a non-language transformer model from TypeSafe AI that outputs calibrated probabilities instead of text. Its developers target cheaper, faster automation; predefined outputs avoid invented prose but do not make every decision correct. TechCrunch →

How is Anthropic’s Claude being misused?

Anthropic’s September 2026 threat report, as covered by TechBarrista, describes disrupted malicious uses including fabricated dating personas, mass-surveillance platform development, and a hotel WiFi compromise in an espionage campaign. techbarrista.com →

How many employees are using AI at work?

A University of Konstanz survey of 1,105 employees conducted in May 2026 found workplace AI use had risen only slightly, to 38%. Adoption remained largely employee-driven and uneven across occupations, education levels, and organization sizes. phys.org →

Can organizational support prevent AI-related career dissatisfaction?

A cross-sectional study of 106 Portuguese professionals found that higher perceived organizational support was associated with a weaker relationship between AI insecurity and job-security worries. It found no significant buffering effect for career satisfaction. The study does not establish causation. Frontiers →

Why are industrial chemists hesitant to use AI in the lab?

C&EN reports that attendees at the CIEX conference focused on scientist skepticism and the need to build trust in AI tools for laboratory research and materials discovery. C&EN →

Are creators selling brands on answer engine optimization?

Yes. Digiday reports that creators are beginning to pitch brands on their ability to influence LLM-generated answers, shifting creator marketing toward answer engine optimization. Digiday →
