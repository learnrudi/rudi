AI News for September 3, 2026

The Justice Department backs OpenAI’s fair-use argument, Microsoft’s MAI-Transcribe-2 challenges transcription pricing, the Institute of Foundation Models opens K2 Horizon’s weights, code, and training data, and a study finds chatbots miss 29% of basic election questions. All 46 stories from the day, below.

September 3, 202646 stories · 47 source links · 9 categories

The Trump administration filed a brief backing OpenAI’s fair-use position in The New York Times copyright lawsuit, arguing that restrictions on AI training would hurt American competitiveness. A separate account says the DOJ’s position leaves creators looking to Congress or licensing deals for compensation. That legal contest surrounds an increasingly practical model race: one report prices Microsoft’s MAI-Transcribe-2 at 10 cents per audio hour, the Institute of Foundation Models says K2 Horizon provides six models with weights, code, and training data, and Meta claims Muse Spark 1.3 has caught up with OpenAI and Anthropic. Price, disclosure, benchmark standing, and permission to train are all parts of the same competition.

A study reported by NBC News found that chatbots gave inaccurate or outdated answers to 29% of basic election questions, with particularly poor Spanish-language responses. One report says Nigeria’s election commission is planning technology audits, mock elections, and media-literacy efforts after its chairman warned about AI-powered disinformation and algorithmic micro-targeting. Elsewhere, India is expanding AI adoption and skills programs while New York City restricts AI for younger students, and a Polish report says growing AI use is making job candidates harder for employers to assess. The tools are spreading faster than institutions can settle how to evaluate their answers or their users.
