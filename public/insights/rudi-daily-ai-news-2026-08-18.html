AI News for August 18, 2026

Nvidia’s reported $105 billion OpenAI data-center commitment pushes the chipmaker deeper into AI finance, Round Hill alleges Anthropic and Suno used lyrics from 500 songs for AI training, and 19 U.S. lawmakers press Meta over election deepfakes. Z.ai’s open-weight GLM 5.3 adds dual-use cybersecurity capabilities to the mix. All 84 stories from the day, below.

August 18, 202684 stories · 85 source links · 10 categories

CNBC reports Nvidia is using its capital reserves to finance AI infrastructure, including a $105 billion commitment to an OpenAI data center, as its chip lead narrows. A separate funding roundup counted $10 billion across 40 AI deals from August 11–17. On the model side, Z.ai released the open-weight GLM 5.3 with automated cybersecurity capabilities and corresponding dual-use risks, while Alibaba released a laptop-friendly Qwen3.8-27B and announced revenue sharing for Qwen3.8 Max. The competition now spans capital, model access, and the machines capable models can run on.

Round Hill Music sued Anthropic and Suno, alleging unauthorized use of lyrics from 500 songs for AI training and seeking up to $1 billion. Elsewhere in entertainment, the Motion Picture Association and ByteDance reached a copyright agreement that calls for stronger safeguards around AI video and image tools. The accountability questions extend beyond copyright: a federal court preserved judicial immunity for a judge accused of relying entirely on AI for a ruling, and 19 U.S. lawmakers demanded answers from Meta about election safeguards and AI deepfakes. Courts, rights holders, and lawmakers are each testing a different boundary on how AI may be used.

USA TODAY reports that AI-assisted elder fraud is leaving adult children to manage the emotional and financial fallout. OpenAI responded to another vulnerable audience with a version of ChatGPT for teens that adds safety measures and Study Mode. Education and work are moving at the same time: Vietnam’s new regulation requires universities to integrate AI and digital competencies into their curricula, while a survey found 88% of trade professionals reporting increased demand, with many adopting AI and digital tools to manage workloads. Meanwhile, audiences and platforms are pushing back against low-quality AI-generated content—easy production has not guaranteed willing viewers.
