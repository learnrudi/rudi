AI News for September 6, 2026

The DOJ backs OpenAI’s fair-use defense as publishers press new copyright claims and OpenAI seeks judgment in a 10.8 million-article case. Elsewhere, rapid releases produce “model fatigue,” data-center construction slows, and organized labor responds to AI-driven job losses. All 54 stories from the day, below.

September 6, 202654 stories · 55 source links · 9 categories

The federal government entered a consequential AI copyright fight: the DOJ filed in support of OpenAI’s fair-use defense in The New York Times lawsuit. Meanwhile, The Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft over alleged unauthorized use of news content, while OpenAI asked for summary judgment in a separate case involving 10.8 million articles. The courts are being asked to define the boundaries that licensing agreements have not settled.

Users are struggling to keep pace with frequent model upgrades and the security concerns that accompany them, CNBC reports. One separate report says Nvidia acquired Hugging Face for $12.9 billion; that single-source account deserves caution. The release race may be frenetic, but control of the tools and platforms remains the larger prize.

U.S. data-center construction faces its first slowdown since 2020 because of power shortages, equipment delays, and local opposition, according to one report. At the same time, tech workers are increasingly organizing for better conditions, and union leaders are pointing to AI-driven job losses in back-office work. The infrastructure and labor stories meet at the same question: who absorbs the costs of scaling AI?
