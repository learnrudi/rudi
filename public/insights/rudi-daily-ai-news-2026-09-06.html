AI News for September 6, 2026

OpenAI faces new copyright claims while asking a court to end a separate 10.8 million-article case, AI users contend with a relentless model-release cycle, and one report says U.S. data-center construction is slowing despite record investment. All 34 stories from the day, below.

September 6, 202634 stories · 35 source links · 9 categories

The Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft over alleged copyright infringement, while OpenAI asked for summary judgment in a separate 10.8 million-article case. The company argues that its limited verbatim reproduction, implied licenses, and fair use justify ending that case. In the New York Times litigation, the Justice Department filed a statement supporting OpenAI’s fair-use position. Publishers, OpenAI, and now the federal government are pressing competing views of how copyright law applies to model training.

Users are struggling to keep up with the release pace at Anthropic, Meta, Google, OpenAI, and Nvidia, according to CNBC, with complexity and safety among the concerns. The systems behind that competition face a physical constraint too: one report says U.S. data-center construction has slowed for the first time since 2020 because of power shortages, equipment delays, and local opposition. The release calendar moves faster than the infrastructure beneath it.

The business and workplace consequences are moving alongside the technology. One report says Nvidia acquired Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, with Jim Cramer arguing that the deal blocks rivals; treat both the acquisition claim and its competitive interpretation as single-source reporting. Meanwhile, labor unions are gaining support as workers fear AI-driven job displacement and are pushing back against unchecked workplace deployment. The companies are consolidating tools while workers organize around who bears the disruption.
