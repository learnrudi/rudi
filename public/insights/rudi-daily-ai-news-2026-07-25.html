AI News for July 25, 2026

Big Tech urges Washington not to restrict open-weight models — though reports disagree on who actually signed — OpenAI ships the GPT-5.6 family and ChatGPT Work, the EU's Digital Omnibus lands with its August dates intact, and Korea courts a gigawatt AI buildout. All 50 stories from the day, below.

July 25, 202650 stories · 8 categories

The companies that spent July being regulated pushed back — though the reporting disagrees about who joined. Business Insider has Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, and Palantir signing an open letter urging US policymakers not to regulate open-weight AI models, and TechRadar notes OpenAI signed as the White House accuses Moonshot AI of stealing from Anthropic. A later account describes a 25-firm coalition letter that OpenAI and Anthropic both skipped, calling the absence a lobbying split. The accounts may describe different letters; treat the signatory list as unsettled. What is not in dispute is the ask: no premature restrictions on open weights.

The model shelf refilled. OpenAI launched the GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — alongside ChatGPT Work, an agent pitched to manage apps, files, and long-running projects on its own. Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5 drew strong benchmark notices at competitive pricing. And Google’s absence kept compounding: one tracker puts Gemini 3.5 Pro at 67 days past its promised release while rivals ship.

Korea spent the day assembling an AI buildout. NAVER, NVIDIA, and Brookfield will expand a national AI factory to 200 megawatts with gigawatt ambitions — a $1 billion NVIDIA investment and up to $9 billion from Brookfield — while President Lee met Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Broadcom CEOs in San Francisco, a meeting one report says included a $500 billion Nvidia–SK Group partnership (a single-source figure worth reading carefully). Regulators moved in parallel: the Digital Omnibus is approved, and not everything is delayed, Delhi’s High Court warned an injunction against OpenAI could hurt Indian AI development, and Brazil’s AI election-ad rules took effect. Meanwhile the jobs panic had a skeptic day — the Guardian argues the AI jobs apocalypse probably isn’t coming anytime soon — even as CNBC describes a coalition of religious leaders, unions, and local activists forming against AI’s societal costs.
