AI News for July 25, 2026

Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, and Palantir urge Washington not to restrict open-weight models — as one report says the White House accuses Moonshot of stealing from Anthropic — the EU's Digital Omnibus lands with its August dates intact, Korea courts a gigawatt AI buildout, and the jobs-apocalypse narrative takes fire. All 32 stories from the day, below.

July 25, 202632 stories · 6 categories

The companies that spent July being regulated pushed back in one voice. Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, and Palantir signed an open letter urging US policymakers not to regulate open-weight AI models — and TechRadar notes the timing: OpenAI signed as the White House accuses Moonshot AI of stealing from Anthropic. Europe’s answer arrived the same day: the Digital Omnibus is approved, and not everything is delayed — high-risk obligations slip, but transparency and AI-literacy duties still land in August. Delhi’s High Court published its reasoning too: an injunction against OpenAI could hurt Indian AI development, with training on copyrighted news ruled fair dealing. And Brazil’s AI election-ad rules took effect as campaign season opened.

Korea spent the day assembling an AI buildout. NAVER, NVIDIA, and Brookfield will expand a national AI factory to 200 megawatts with gigawatt ambitions — a $1 billion NVIDIA investment and up to $9 billion from Brookfield — while President Lee met Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Broadcom CEOs in San Francisco, a meeting one report says included a $500 billion Nvidia–SK Group partnership (a single-source figure worth reading carefully). The demand side explains the courtship: one analysis of OpenAI’s $30 billion Georgia campus argues frontier compute stays scarce until at least 2028. And SemiAnalysis asks the week’s hardware question straight: can AMD break the CUDA moat?

The jobs-apocalypse narrative had its skeptic day. Eduardo Porter argues in the Guardian that the AI jobs apocalypse probably isn’t coming anytime soon, and a data analysis finds displacement concentrated among entry-level workers, with companies overstating AI as the cause of layoffs. The politics are moving anyway: CNBC describes a coalition of religious leaders, unions, and local activists coalescing against AI’s societal costs. A New York district paused its robot-teacher plan over student-data privacy. An appeals court left the block on the $100,000 H-1B fee in place. And Taylor Swift trademarked her own face to fight AI likeness misuse, by one account.
