AI News for September 7, 2026

OpenAI unveils GPT-6 Astra, copyright battles widen around AI training, data-center investment runs into power and water constraints, and AI-designed drug research reaches a Phase IIa trial. All 71 stories from the day, below.

September 7, 202671 stories · 72 source links · 9 categories

OpenAI unveiled GPT-6 Astra with computer-use, coding, and cybersecurity capabilities, describing it as its most intelligent and aligned frontier model and rating its cybersecurity capability Critical. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang then said AGI had arrived with Astra, a claim that critics and researchers dispute. The model arrived with a superlative from its maker and a verdict from a major chip executive; neither settles the argument over what Astra can actually do.

The legal fight over training data widened at the same time. The Seattle Times and Newsday sued OpenAI and Microsoft over the use of their journalism, while OpenAI and Microsoft argued that training on copyrighted news is fair use. The Justice Department filed in support of the fair-use position. Publishers are testing the boundary in court while the federal government signals where it wants that boundary drawn.

The physical and scientific stakes were just as concrete. PwC projects $31.6 trillion in global data-center investment by 2050, with power supply identified as a key constraint; tech companies separately say closed-loop cooling can sharply reduce data-center water use, though experts note trade-offs and inconsistent industry practices. In drug development, Insilico says its AI-designed rentosertib reversed biological-age measures in a Phase IIa trial, based on six proteomic aging clocks. AI's next claims will be tested not only by benchmarks, but by courts, grids, water systems, and clinical evidence.
