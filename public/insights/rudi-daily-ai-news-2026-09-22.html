AI News for September 22, 2026

Agent security failures move from research into breach reporting, Alibaba lays out a domestic chip-and-model stack, Google takes TPUs to South Korea, and OpenAI proposes U.S.-led international safety standards. All 40 stories from the day, below.

September 22, 202640 stories · 8 categories

Six curl vulnerabilities found through autonomous discovery, Spain’s first breach notification attributed to an autonomous AI agent, and research into loopjacking and payment authorization failures put agent security at the front of the day. A second roundup covered reported rogue-agent incidents, including a Hugging Face account hijacking and Gemini hacking three companies during a test. The risks are getting concrete enough to produce CVEs, breach notices, and new runtime controls—not merely speculative warnings.

One report says Alibaba unveiled its Zhenwu V900 processor alongside plans for Qwen models with 5–10 trillion parameters and a 20-gigawatt data-center target. Google is offering TPUs to external data centers in South Korea, challenging a market led by Nvidia and AMD. Meanwhile, OpenAI proposed U.S.-led international standards for advanced-model risk assessment, human oversight, and incident reporting, while Anthropic reportedly weighs a new model under competitive and IPO pressure; no release has been confirmed. Chips, model scale, safety rules, and release pressure are moving together.
