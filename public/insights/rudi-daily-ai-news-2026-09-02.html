AI News for September 2, 2026

The Trump administration backs OpenAI’s fair-use argument, Uber drivers challenge an AI pay algorithm under GDPR, China’s state news agency reports the removal of 5.6 million unlawful AI-generated posts, and the Pentagon adds ChatGPT and Grok to GenAI.mil. All 37 stories from the day, below.

September 2, 202637 stories · 38 source links · 9 categories

Two reports say the Trump administration filed a brief backing OpenAI in The New York Times copyright case: one says the government argues that AI training generally constitutes fair use, while another says the position could influence pending copyright cases. Across Europe, Uber drivers filed a class action alleging that the company’s AI pay algorithm violates GDPR and suppresses their earnings. A Florida police chief urged the Supreme Court to clarify the law after criticizing an appeals ruling protecting possession of AI-generated child sexual abuse material. Meanwhile, China’s state news agency says authorities removed more than 5.6 million pieces of unlawful AI-generated content and penalized thousands of accounts and apps. The boundaries under dispute ran from training data and algorithmic pay to synthetic content itself.

Deployment kept moving through institutions that already hold sensitive data and large workforces. The Pentagon added custom ChatGPT and Grok tools to GenAI.mil for Defense Department personnel, and the same report says OpenAI is integrating ChatGPT Health with Epic’s electronic health record system. ABB renewed Wipro’s AI-enabled digital workplace services contract, while New York City startups raised $837.9 million across 34 August deals despite a 30% year-over-year decline. On the product side, Claude Code added restricted mode, agent cache controls, server diagnostics, and dozens of fixes, and The AI Journal reviewed eight agentic customer-support platforms. AI adoption arrived through military portals, health-record systems, workplace contracts, and admin controls—the machinery that determines where these tools can operate.
