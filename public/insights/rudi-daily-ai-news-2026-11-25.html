AI News for November 25, 2026

A U.S. chip-licensing rule narrows remote access to advanced Nvidia compute, GPT-6.2 Sol reaches paid ChatGPT tiers, dockworkers set six labor standards for AI and automation, and Korea’s proposed defense AI budget rises to 361 billion won. All 66 stories from the day, below.

November 25, 202666 stories · 11 categories

A new U.S. Commerce Department licensing rule is pushing Asian data-center operators to cut Nvidia B300 rental prices and court U.S. buyers, while Chinese AI firms including Moonshot AI, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent are pursuing Nvidia compute through overseas data centers as a BIS rule closes the remote-access loophole. Public spending is pulling on the same market: Korean telecom and cloud firms are forming task forces to compete for defense AI contracts as the proposed budget more than doubles to 361 billion won. In Ukraine, official figures describe about two dozen firms building AI interceptor drones and more than 100 training on shared data, although no list or definition substantiates those counts.

India Today reports that OpenAI is rolling GPT-6.2 Sol into ChatGPT’s Chat tab for Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers, but not Free or Go users. Another report says Nvidia released open weights for its 30B-parameter Nemotron-Labs image model. An agent-tools roundup says Anthropic brought Skills to MCP into public beta alongside Claude’s Install as app beta, while Perplexity’s November release notes list broader Computer access, an enterprise browser, Slack integration, hardware, and custom MCP connectors.

The International Dockworkers Council adopted six minimum labor standards for port automation and AI, warning that terminal software and algorithms threaten jobs. A daily fintech briefing says new FCA guidance requires banks to prepare for third-party AI agents. In South Korea, financial regulators and KT demonstrated deepfake and deepvoice detection while warning about AI-generated investment scams. And the University of Warsaw received a $1 million Google.org grant to help scientists adapt existing AI models to their own research data. Deployment is expanding, but so are the demands for labor protections, fraud controls, and usable research tools.

Can Chinese AI companies still rent Nvidia chips through overseas data centers?

They are trying. One report says Moonshot AI, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent are pursuing access through overseas data centers as a new U.S. BIS rule closes the advanced-chip remote-access loophole. wpnews.pro →

Who gets GPT-6.2 Sol in ChatGPT?

India Today reports that Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers are getting GPT-6.2 Sol globally; Free and Go users are not. It also says OpenAI plans to retire older GPT-5.6 models in December. India Today →

Did dockworkers set labor standards for AI and automation in ports?

Yes. The International Dockworkers Council adopted six minimum labor standards for port automation and AI for its 2026–2030 collective-bargaining period, warning that terminal software and algorithms threaten jobs. El Estrecho Digital →

How much is South Korea proposing to spend on defense AI?

The proposed defense AI budget is 361 billion won, more than double the previous level, according to CHOSUNBIZ. Korean telecom and cloud firms are forming task forces to compete for the resulting contracts. CHOSUNBIZ →

Did Nvidia release an open-weight image model?

Yes. A report says Nvidia released open weights on Hugging Face for Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B, a 30B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts image-generation model. Wisevoter →

What does the FCA want banks to do about third-party AI agents?

A daily fintech briefing says new FCA guidance requires banks to prepare for third-party AI agents. Behind Fintech →

Are AI agents conducting mass hacking campaigns?

Italy’s ACN cybersecurity agency warns that AI agents are conducting mass compromise campaigns, according to one explainx.ai blog report. Treat that as a secondhand, single-source claim. explainx.ai →

What is the University of Warsaw’s Google.org AI grant for?

The $1 million grant will fund an open-source framework that helps scientists adapt existing AI models to their own research data. EdTech Innovation Hub →
