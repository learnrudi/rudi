AI News for October 24, 2026

Nvidia’s AI financing strategy meets tighter credit, Amazon warehouses unpowered “dark GPUs,” Texas seeks a ChatGPT ban for minors, and a GAO report finds gaps in Pentagon responsible-AI compliance. All 54 stories from the day, below.

October 24, 202654 stories · 10 categories

Nvidia’s financing strategy is facing its first major stress test as neocloud shares fall and credit tightens around partners including CoreWeave. One report says Amazon is buying billions of dollars in Nvidia racks each month without enough data-center power to run them, while Google Cloud and NVIDIA say A5X systems could scale to 960,000 Rubin GPUs across sites. The ambition is still enormous; financing and electricity are becoming the checks on it.

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and Sam Altman, seeking more than $1 million and an order barring ChatGPT for minors in Texas. A GAO report found that several Pentagon AI projects fall short of the department’s own responsible-AI tenets, and Canadian news publishers filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against Anthropic in Ontario. Safety claims, internal governance, and training data are now separate accountability fronts.

Spain’s opposition PP party released an AI-generated deepfake ad depicting the prime minister’s wife and other Socialist figures. Washington and Los Angeles are pushing to develop driverless subway lines as unions resist, while one Louisiana woman lost more than $10,000 to a scammer using AI videos to impersonate Jon Bon Jovi. Elections, transit jobs, and household fraud put the costs of synthetic media and automation in public view.

What People Are Asking

Why is Nvidia’s AI financing strategy under pressure?

CNBC reports that Nvidia’s equity stakes, backstops, and lease guarantees for AI buildouts are being tested as neocloud stocks tumble and credit tightens around partners such as CoreWeave. CNBC →

Why is Amazon buying GPUs it cannot use?

Because power, not procurement, is the reported bottleneck. TechSpot says Amazon is buying billions of dollars in Nvidia AI racks each month but lacks enough data-center power to run them, leaving hardware warehoused. TechSpot →

Is Texas trying to ban ChatGPT for minors?

Yes. Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and Sam Altman, seeking a court order barring ChatGPT for minors in Texas as well as more than $1 million in penalties over alleged deceptive safety claims and harms. Dallas Express →

Are Pentagon AI projects following responsible-AI guidelines?

No, not consistently. OPB’s account of a GAO report says several Pentagon AI projects do not fully follow the department’s responsible-AI tenets, while the office overseeing compliance remains badly understaffed. OPB →

When is Alibaba’s Qwen4 coming out?

No release date has been announced. GitHub pull requests and new inference-library support indicate that Alibaba’s Qwen4-Exp architecture is spreading ahead of an official release, but there is no model card yet. Startup Fortune →

What is NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B?

Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B is NVIDIA’s open 8-billion-parameter, 4-billion-effective small language model for local voice agents, game characters, and robots running on RTX, Jetson, and Spark hardware. MarkTechPost →

Did Spain’s opposition use an AI deepfake in an election ad?

Yes, according to The English Chronicle. Spain’s opposition PP party released an AI-generated deepfake ad depicting Prime Minister Sánchez’s wife and other Socialist figures, prompting legal complaints and condemnation ahead of the November 29 vote. The English Chronicle →

Are US transit systems considering driverless trains?

Yes. Washington, D.C., and Los Angeles are pushing to develop driverless subway lines, reviving debate over automated-transit safety and potential driver-job losses as unions resist. The Daily Reporter →
