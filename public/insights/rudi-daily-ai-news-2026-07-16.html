AI News for July 16, 2026

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines releases Inkling, a 975-billion-parameter open-weights model, Apple Intelligence wins approval to launch in China with Alibaba and Baidu, SpaceXAI announces Grok 4.5, and Hyundai workers strike over humanoid robots on the line. All 73 stories from the day, below.

July 16, 202673 stories · 74 source links · 9 categories

The open-weights frontier got its biggest American entry yet: Mira Murati’s Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling, a 975-billion-parameter model under Apache 2.0, aimed — by The Register’s reading — at the Chinese open models that have set that category’s pace. SpaceXAI announced Grok 4.5, pitched by its maker for coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work. A daily roundup adds Moonshot launching Kimi K3 at 2.8 trillion parameters to the same news cycle. Google supplied the counterpoint: per one report, its rebuilt Gemini 3.5 Pro missed a third launch deadline over persistent hallucinations, with a stopgap release under consideration. The frontier is getting more open and more crowded while one of its incumbents slips.

Governments spent the day redrawing access lines. Apple Intelligence won approval to launch in China with Alibaba’s Qwen and Baidu aboard — confirmation of the filing that surfaced a day earlier. The US Commerce Department, per one report, reclassified the UAE to a trusted trade tier, clearing license-free Nvidia chip exports for the Stargate project even as Congress probes a related crypto conflict. Washington is also scrutinizing GLM-5.2, the Chinese model now driving policy debates on safety and security. And Australia went the other direction on training data: Prime Minister Albanese called unlicensed training on copyrighted work theft, ruled out a text-and-data-mining exception, and promised legislation — a stance one report says could complicate Anthropic’s reported $21.6 billion investment in the country.

The robots expanded their footprint and met resistance on the same day. NVIDIA announced Cosmos 3 Edge alongside Japanese robotics and manufacturing partners, and Japan’s healthcare giants are putting AI into surgical robots, CT scanners, and drug discovery, with several products already shipping. On the factory floor, Hyundai workers in South Korea went on strike over humanoid robots — one report says the deployment came without union consultation and hit production of 5,000 vehicles; another frames the dispute around wages and safeguards against job losses to Boston Dynamics machines. The accounts differ on emphasis; the trigger — robots on the line — doesn’t. Upstream, server maker Inventec warns component lead times have passed 40 weeks as AI demand reallocates memory and CPU capacity.

What People Are Asking

What is Inkling, the new AI model from Mira Murati?

Inkling is a 975-billion-parameter open-weights model released by Murati's Thinking Machines Lab under an Apache 2.0 license — positioned, per The Register, to compete with the Chinese frontier models leading the open category. The Register →

Is Apple Intelligence launching in China?

Yes. Apple Intelligence received regulatory approval to launch in China through partnerships with Alibaba's Qwen and Baidu. TechCrunch →

What is Grok 4.5?

SpaceXAI's newly announced model, optimized — by its maker's account — for coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work, with competitive performance and pricing claims. SpaceXAI →

Is Gemini 3.5 Pro delayed again?

Per one report, yes — the rebuilt model has missed its third launch deadline over persistent hallucinations and inconsistent output, and Google is said to be exploring a stopgap release. Tech Times →

Why are Hyundai factory workers on strike?

Over humanoid robots. One report says Hyundai deployed them without union consultation, affecting production of 5,000 vehicles; another describes a fight over wages plus safeguards against job losses to Boston Dynamics robots. Gizmodo →

Did Australia ban AI training on copyrighted material?

Australia's prime minister called unlicensed AI training theft and confirmed there will be no text-and-data-mining exception, with new legislation planned — a stance one report says could affect Anthropic's reported $21.6 billion investment. Tech Times →

Are AI chatbots spreading government censorship?

A new study — attributed in one report to Meta's Oversight Board — found major chatbots are more likely to refuse to criticize restrictive governments, which researchers warn risks extending state speech controls globally. OC Register →

Does Geoffrey Hinton think AI is conscious?

Yes — on the Big Technology Podcast he argued current AI systems are already conscious, and that corporate fiduciary duties overriding safety commitments make the risk structural and present rather than future. Tech Times →
