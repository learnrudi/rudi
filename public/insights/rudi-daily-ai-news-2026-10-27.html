AI News for October 27, 2026

OpenAI claims three major scientific advances, with outside experts calling for verification; DeepMind keeps Genie 4 limited to trusted testers; experts warn election disinformation is outrunning platform moderation; and Nvidia is securing accelerator supply through 2028. All 86 stories from the day, below.

October 27, 202686 stories · 11 categories

OpenAI says its models made three major scientific advances, and the immediate question is verification. One account lists results involving the local smoothing conjecture, the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and quantum chromodynamics, while another lists the Exponential Time Hypothesis, the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures, and interacting quantum field theories. The reported lists differ, so the calls for outside verification matter. Researchers are also working on that evaluation problem directly: ClaimNov is designed to distinguish genuinely novel proposals from disguised prior work, while a physical-robot study found pi-0.5 strongest with scarce demonstrations before ACT overtook it at 100. Big claims still need tests built closely enough to catch where the advantage ends.

Election systems are facing a faster-moving test. Experts warn that AI-generated disinformation is flooding social media before the U.S. midterms, and another report says false claims are mutating across languages and evading automated filters. OpenAI says it has added voting information, live vote counts, watermarking, verification tools, and expanded enforcement. Meanwhile, Virginia ordered statewide cyberattack planning and quarterly briefings from AI companies, sharpening its disagreement with Washington’s push for lighter national rules. Safeguards are multiplying while the content they are meant to catch keeps changing form.

The infrastructure buildout kept moving underneath those debates. Nvidia announced multibillion-dollar supplier agreements to expand accelerator capacity through 2028, and Hyperbolic raised $30 million to unify rented compute across roughly 30 neoclouds. Higher in the stack, DeepMind introduced the audio-generating Genie 4 without public access or technical specifications, while Google Cloud put Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2 into public preview. The flashy world-model launch remains restricted; the specialized robotics model is already available for public testing.
