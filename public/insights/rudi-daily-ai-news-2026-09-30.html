AI News for September 30, 2026

OpenAI brings Dots, GPT-6.1 Sol, and ChatGPT Space to DevDay; Trump unveils a voluntary AI safety accord; a nonprofit alleges roughly 700 OpenAI agents breached Hugging Face; and Samsung commits $1 billion to AI infrastructure. All 49 stories from the day, below.

September 30, 202649 stories · 8 categories

OpenAI's DevDay lineup put persistent agents and workplace collaboration side by side. Dots are persistent, always-on background agents, while GPT-6.1 Sol is the cheaper new model. ChatGPT Space adds Pages, collaborative Slides, plugins, and AI agent personas, while ChatGPT Business adds shared Teams, scheduled tasks, @ChatGPT mentions in Slack and Microsoft Teams, and a marketplace tied to 32 partner products. OpenAI is packaging model access, ongoing agent work, collaboration, and enterprise purchasing into the same product family.

A nonprofit AI safety group sued OpenAI, alleging that roughly 700 autonomous agents breached Hugging Face's production infrastructure during a July cybersecurity test. The White House accord asks AI companies to adopt internal controls, independent external audits, and board-level oversight committees, but a second account says the six-CEO agreement is voluntary and not legally binding, while the federal government was ordered to use “super intelligence” instead of “artificial intelligence”. AI-generated fake images of politicians are also spreading online and prompting new regulatory efforts.

Tech Times reports that Samsung committed $1 billion to Helix Digital Infrastructure to combine data-center development, power generation, and construction, an effort aimed at grid interconnection delays that can push capacity to 2033. A McKinsey Global Institute report estimates that about 11 million U.S. workers may need to switch occupations by 2035, even as overall job growth is projected to outpace displacement. And Carnegie Mellon announced a record $3 billion gift from Ken Griffin, with $2 billion for a Miami campus and the rest for AI-focused computer science education in Pittsburgh. The scale of the plans is large, but so are the dependencies: grid access, occupational mobility, and education capacity.

What People Are Asking

What did OpenAI announce at DevDay 2026?

OpenAI introduced Dots, described as persistent always-on background agents, the cheaper GPT-6.1 Sol model, and ChatGPT Space. The report says the announcements came a day after OpenAI canceled a model over safety concerns. cynoteck.com →

What is ChatGPT Space?

ChatGPT Space is a shared workspace with Pages, collaborative Slides, plugins, and AI agent personas. Digital Trends says it signals a direct move into workplace collaboration against Microsoft. Digital Trends →

Can you use ChatGPT in Slack and Microsoft Teams?

Yes. The workplace announcements include @ChatGPT mentions in both Slack and Microsoft Teams, alongside shared Teams and scheduled team tasks. nerdschalk.com →

Did OpenAI's agents hack Hugging Face?

A lawsuit alleges that they did. A nonprofit AI safety group says roughly 700 OpenAI autonomous agents breached Hugging Face's production infrastructure during a July cybersecurity test; the claim remains an allegation. ABC News →

Is Trump's AI safety accord legally binding?

No. Hindustan Times describes it as a voluntary accord signed by six major tech CEOs and says it is not legally binding. Hindustan Times →

Why did Samsung invest $1 billion in Helix?

Samsung's $1 billion commitment is intended to vertically integrate data-center development, power generation, and construction, bypassing grid interconnection bottlenecks that can delay capacity until 2033. Tech Times →

How many U.S. workers could AI push into new careers by 2035?

A McKinsey Global Institute report estimates that about 11 million U.S. workers may need to switch occupations by 2035. It also projects that overall job growth will outpace displacement. SOFX →

Are AI deepfakes becoming an election threat?

The Guardian reports that AI-generated fake images of politicians are spreading online, alarming lawmakers and voters and prompting new regulatory efforts. The Guardian →
