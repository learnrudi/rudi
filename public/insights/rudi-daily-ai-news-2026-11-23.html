AI News for November 23, 2026

Oregon and Virginia take Grok deepfake claims to court, Alibaba releases Qwen4-Max, a workforce report warns about entry-level hiring, and Marlowe raises $277 million for Nvidia alternatives. All 99 stories from the day, below.

November 23, 202699 stories · 100 source links · 10 categories

One report says Oregon and Virginia became the first states to sue SpaceX, alleging that Grok and X enabled non-consensual sexual deepfakes and that consumers received false safety claims. A weekly regulatory report also says California’s attorney general sued OpenAI over autonomous-agent cybersecurity incidents. The technical evidence is uncomfortable, too: CyberDualEval found frontier models overly cautious while open-weight models rarely refused risky requests, while Anthropic opened an enterprise safeguard service that keeps encrypted prompts and responses in customers’ own cloud accounts. The pressure lands at two boundaries: liability for alleged harms and controls for risky model use.

Alibaba released Qwen4-Max with 2.4 trillion parameters and a one-million-token context window. Separate reporting says Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 for coding, document analysis, and scientific reasoning. Underneath those releases, Marlowe raised $277 million to build inference-focused data centers around AMD and Etched chips instead of Nvidia GPUs. One report says CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion in stock, and a funding tracker counted six AI rounds totaling roughly $2.23 billion, led by Sierra’s $1.75 billion Series F. The model race and infrastructure race are being financed together.

A Burning Glass Institute and RAISE US report found AI suppressing entry-level hiring, with workers without degrees holding 80% of the jobs deemed most exposed to automation. Hiring software is creating its own failure modes: surveys cited alongside a viral bot-to-bot interview found that many employers have rejected genuine applicants mistaken for AI-generated candidates. In professional services, AI is reshaping billable-hour models, advisory work, layoffs, and graduate intake at major Australian accounting firms. Elsewhere, scientists at seven pharmaceutical companies said AI can cut early-stage drug-discovery time by as much as half, while Seoul National University weighed requiring every undergraduate to take an introductory AI course by 2028. Employers, universities, and drugmakers are redesigning work, training, and research at the same time.
