AI News for September 15, 2026

Open-weight Chinese models narrow the frontier gap to about 4.4 months, Salesforce rolls out Koa and AIforce, and funding flows to AI search, chips, robotics, and financial infrastructure. All 49 stories from the day, below.

September 15, 202649 stories · 51 source links · 10 categories

Mozilla’s State of Open Source AI report puts the gap between U.S. closed frontier models and top open-weight Chinese models at about 4.4 months; for tasks estimated to take human experts 8 to 12 hours, that head start costs roughly five times more per task. Salesforce answered the same cost-and-control pressure with Koa, a reasoning model post-trained with Nvidia on an open-weight base for sales, marketing, and customer-support work. It also introduced AIforce, an interface layer that carries Salesforce business logic, security, and governance into third-party agent workflows. A Dreamforce live blog also reported a broad Customer 360 update and an expanded Anthropic partnership. Enterprise AI is being packaged around the practical gap between model capability and controlled deployment.

Money followed both software and infrastructure. AlleyWatch’s funding report lists a $180 million Series D for AI-search-visibility company Profound and a $20 million seed round for cross-border payments startup Fin. Another funding roundup says Dutch AI-chip startup EUCLYD raised more than €200 million while Chinese rounds targeted robotics, semiconductors, sovereign AI infrastructure, and drug discovery. At the customer end, fintech executives said security, cost, traceability, and accountability are slowing efforts to bring AI to millions of banking customers. Adoption is uneven: Statistics Canada data puts wholesale trade at 7.9%, against a 19.2% national average. Meanwhile, FutureVault launched governed agents for multi-step document workflows with permissioning and audit trails. Capital is moving quickly; accountable execution remains the harder part.
