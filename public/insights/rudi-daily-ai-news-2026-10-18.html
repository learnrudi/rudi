AI News for October 18, 2026

A Wesleyan Media Project study counted more than $137 million behind AI-related political ads, an appeals court upheld Thomson Reuters’s copyright win, and Anthropic’s proposed frontier-model safety threshold drew criticism. Claude Code added official plugins while OpenAI offered eligible U.S. academic labs free access to GPT-Rosalind. All 40 stories from the day, below.

October 18, 202640 stories · 10 categories

A Wesleyan Media Project study found 220 AI-generated or AI-enhanced political ads backed by more than $137 million ahead of the U.S. midterms. Republican sponsors accounted for 81% of the identified ads, while only 27% carried AI disclaimers—a large campaign investment paired with limited disclosure.

The legal and regulatory boundaries were just as concrete. A U.S. appeals court upheld Thomson Reuters’s copyright victory over Ross Intelligence, affirming that training on scraped legal data was not fair use, although the ruling remains sealed. Separately, one report says Anthropic circulated a draft safety bill with a $1 billion or 50-victim intervention threshold; critics argue that bar is too high. Apple, meanwhile, is accepting claims in a $250 million settlement over iPhone 16 AI and Siri marketing, with eligible buyers given until December 21, 2026 to file.

The product news moved from general-purpose chat toward narrower tools and institutions. Anthropic launched official Claude Code plugins, customizable Mods, and expanded cloud sessions, while OpenAI offered eligible U.S. academic labs free GPT-Rosalind access and up to 30 seats for 12 months. Perplexity also released a 27B model that returns probability-based judgments instead of generated prose—a model designed to decide, not draft.
