AI News for August 8, 2026

OpenAI holds Astra while it tests for critical cyber capabilities, Demis Hassabis moves from DeepMind CEO to Alphabet chair and chief scientist, Singapore mandates AI literacy training for every teacher, and a German court rules that Suno reproduced copyrighted songs. All 38 stories from the day, below.

August 8, 202638 stories · 39 source links · 9 categories

The frontier-model story opened with a brake and a handoff. Sam Altman says OpenAI cannot release Astra yet because of cybersecurity risk, while a second account says internal evaluations could not rule out critical cyber capabilities and triggered more safety testing. At Google, Demis Hassabis is moving from DeepMind CEO to chair and chief scientist at Alphabet. Alibaba pushed in the other direction, launching Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model with an open-weights release planned.

The policy ledger reached courts, classrooms, and elections. A German court ruled that Suno reproduced copyrighted songs and ordered revenue disclosure. Singapore’s education ministry will require compulsory AI literacy modules for every primary, secondary, and junior-college teacher. In the United States, a challenge backed by xAI is targeting the constitutionality of citizen suits used for environmental enforcement. Nigeria, meanwhile, is being urged to prepare for AI-driven disinformation ahead of its 2027 election, while an Indian op-ed argues that responsible identity and lawful traceability are now central to preserving trust in digital systems.

The physical and labor constraints kept catching up with the adoption story. CNBC examines how the next automation wave could translate into job losses, and CGTN reports that US data-center expansion faces local opposition, water limits, grid strain, and shortages of skilled labor. Healthcare offers the clearest gap between investment and proof: one analysis counts $8.9 billion in AI drug-discovery funding but no FDA-approved AI-discovered drug, even as a separate report projects the pharmaceutical AI market growing from $2.3 billion in 2025 to $13.8 billion by 2033. The capital is real; the clinical validation is still pending.
