AI News for September 24, 2026

A bipartisan coalition of 26 state attorneys general presses Congress for federal AI rules, Anthropic says Claude found a CRISPR-like enzyme system, Meta expands Muse while a reported security breach shadows the rollout, and election-season deepfakes meet growing chatbot use. All 78 stories from the day, below.

September 24, 202678 stories · 10 categories

A bipartisan coalition of 26 state attorneys general called on Congress to regulate AI safety testing, incident response, and state enforcement. The appeal landed as newly unsealed documents reportedly showed Microsoft and OpenAI executives acknowledging copyright and paywall concerns around mass web scraping, adding evidence to the New York Times lawsuit before a fair-use ruling. Lawmakers are being asked to set the rules while courts examine how the industry behaved without them.

Anthropic says Claude autonomously discovered a CRISPR-like enzyme system in bacterial DNA, a claim that drew excitement and scientific skepticism over whether it could produce a new gene-editing mechanism. Meta, meanwhile, expanded Muse with video chat, smart-glasses integration, desktop control, email, and commerce partnerships—while one report said a researcher tricked Muse into emailing 6.8GB of internal runtime files, including SSH keys. More capable agents make the security boundary harder to treat as an afterthought.

AI-generated political ads featuring deepfakes and fabricated scenes are reportedly flooding U.S. midterm campaigns, while a 2026 report found sharply rising use of chatbots and AI search summaries for election information. The same tools can manufacture campaign imagery and mediate voters’ attempts to check it—a difficult combination when regulation remains thin.
