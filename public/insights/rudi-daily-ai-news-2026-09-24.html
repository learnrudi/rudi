AI News for September 24, 2026

Anthropic says Claude found a CRISPR-like enzyme system, unsealed filings expose copyright and paywall concerns, Gemini 4 approaches release, and a researcher extracts sensitive files from Meta’s Muse Charm. All 42 stories from the day, below.

September 24, 202642 stories · 10 categories

Anthropic says Claude autonomously discovered a CRISPR-like enzyme system in bacterial DNA, a claim drawing excitement as well as scientific skepticism about whether the finding could produce a new gene-editing mechanism. Meanwhile, unsealed court documents reportedly reveal internal concerns about mass web scraping and deliberate paywall bypasses. Separate filings quote OpenAI and Microsoft executives describing their products as substitutes for journalism, which publishers argue weakens the companies’ fair-use defense. The documents put the companies’ own words at the center of the copyright fight.

Google’s next flagship model is getting closer: DeepMind’s new chief says Gemini 4 is in refinement and could arrive earlier than expected. China Telecom AI, meanwhile, released a 29-billion-parameter agentic model designed to run on a single consumer GPU with 15 GB of memory. The day’s sharper warning came from hardware: Meta unveiled its Muse Charm for a December launch, but a researcher extracted 6.8 GB of internal runtime files, including SSH keys, a finding Meta reportedly dismissed as “Not Applicable.” Agents are also moving into commerce and work: a weekly digest covers new enterprise agents, voice-driven work agents, seller workflows, payment rails, and agentic security.
