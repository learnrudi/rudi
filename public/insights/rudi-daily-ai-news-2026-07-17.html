AI News for July 17, 2026

Sources tell CNBC the White House now decides who gets access to frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI, Xi Jinping launches a 29-nation AI alliance in Shanghai, reports conflict on whether Gemini 3.5 Pro launched or slipped months, and Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 goes open weights. All 70 stories from the day, below.

July 17, 202670 stories · 71 source links · 9 categories

Who gets frontier AI became a government question on both sides of the Pacific. Sources tell CNBC the Trump administration is now deciding which companies and entities can access advanced models from Anthropic and OpenAI — moving a gate that used to belong to the labs. Some of the industry asked for a version of this: one account describes labs actively requesting regulation after voluntary safety failures, down to a proposed FINRA-like oversight body, while the White House stands up its Gold Eagle security program. The same day in Shanghai, Xi Jinping launched WAICO, a 29-nation AI cooperation organization, framed explicitly against US efforts to check China’s AI development. Europe’s dates firmed up too: the Digital Omnibus pushes high-risk obligations to late 2027 and 2028, while Article 50 transparency duties land August 2. Three capitals, three different answers to who sets the terms.

Google had the kind of day that produces contradictory headlines. The Los Angeles Times describes Gemini 3.5 Pro months behind schedule amid coding stumbles, clashing teams, and frustrated engineers; a markets piece ties the delay to a stock drop and doubts about Google’s standing in AI programming; and one outlet reports Google released Gemini 3.5 Pro the same day Xi opened the Shanghai summit. Delayed by months and launched the same day — the reports conflict; what’s consistent across them is a flagship badly off schedule. The rest of the field moved while Google wobbled: Moonshot released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model its coverage describes as approaching Anthropic’s Fable; Berlin’s Prior Labs raised €1 billion and exited to SAP eighteen months after founding; and chip stocks slid in a rotation one report pins partly on AI cost worries — AMD down 5%, Intel 4%, Nvidia 3% before a partial recovery.

The courts spent the day assigning responsibility. Apple sent preservation letters to 40 former employees now at OpenAI as part of its trade-secret lawsuit. Bloomberg Law reports a new wave of shareholder derivative suits accusing Big Tech executives of concealing illicit training on copyrighted material — the copyright fight arriving on the fiduciary-duty docket. San Francisco’s city attorney demanded Apple and Google pull 13 nudify apps that generate nonconsensual deepfake images. Kaiser nurses say AI surveillance and call-time metrics are making patient care worse as contract negotiations approach. And Anthropic launched a free Claude for Teachers with the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative — drawing immediate questions about de-skilling the profession it’s courting.
