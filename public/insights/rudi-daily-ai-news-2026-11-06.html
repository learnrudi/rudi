AI News for November 6, 2026

Big Tech rethinks where data centers get built, Goldman Sachs restricts advanced coding tools, Perplexity introduces always-on workplace agents, and the Justice Department forms an AI enforcement task force. All 63 stories from the day, below.

November 6, 202663 stories · 9 categories

Major technology companies are reportedly reconsidering where to build AI infrastructure after candidates opposing data centers won key U.S. midterm races. OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic are weighing overseas and desert locations, tighter regulation, and construction delays. The pressure is reaching the workplace, too: Goldman Sachs restricted advanced AI coding tools to engineers and strats after testing their productivity and risk, while European trade unions pressed for binding rules on AI management at work instead of voluntary guidance.

Government and courts are drawing their own boundaries. The Justice Department formed a task force to coordinate AI litigation and enforcement, including cases involving malicious foreign and criminal uses. Thirty-five music publishers led by Sony and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic over alleged pirated training data and lyric reproduction. In Texas, a federal judge criticized three attorneys over AI-generated legal filings and threatened sanctions. AI use is spreading; so is the insistence that someone remain accountable for its output.

The product push kept moving. Perplexity launched always-on agents that share a workspace and handle ongoing work, and Cursor introduced managed deployment for AI-generated applications. GitHub put Copilot agents inside JetBrains Air and Xcode’s built-in assistant. Outside software, pharmaceutical executives supplied a useful brake on the sales pitch: no AI-designed drug has cleared the FDA, they noted, and biology remains more complicated than an algorithm alone can solve.
