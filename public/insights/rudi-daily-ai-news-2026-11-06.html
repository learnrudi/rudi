AI News for November 6, 2026

Political resistance is pushing AI infrastructure toward new locations, Goldman Sachs is narrowing access to advanced coding tools, and Perplexity is selling always-on workplace agents. Meanwhile, music publishers and federal judges are testing who bears responsibility for AI-assisted work. All 97 stories from the day, below.

November 6, 202697 stories · 98 source links · 9 categories

Major AI companies are reconsidering where they build. Following midterm wins by candidates opposed to AI data centers in key states, OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic are reportedly weighing overseas and desert locations, tighter rules, and construction delays. Markets registered the uncertainty: U.S. tech and AI chip stocks partially recovered after a sharp sell-off, while infrastructure money kept moving. A deal roundup reported Marvell’s $115 billion acquisition of Lumentum for data-center optical connectivity, and Fal raised $1.3 billion at a $16 billion valuation for AI inference services. The appetite for capacity remains large; where that capacity can be built is becoming less certain.

Access to AI tools is also becoming a management decision. Goldman Sachs restricted Claude Code and GitHub access to engineers and strats after testing productivity against risk. In schools, Google says institutions that disable AI tools on student computers will lose access to its search feature, while South Portland is considering a temporary generative-AI ban for younger students. Teachers are already working through the gap: a Bett survey found 80% of roughly 1,000 UK teachers use AI, but 41% said their school had provided no AI training.

Vendors are asking customers to delegate more work just as courts and regulators demand clearer responsibility. Perplexity launched always-on agents for ongoing workplace tasks, available to its $200-per-month Max subscribers, while the Justice Department formed a task force for AI litigation and enforcement. Thirty-five music publishers led by Sony and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic over training data and reproduced lyrics, and a Texas federal judge threatened sanctions after three attorneys filed motions containing what the judge called “blatant AI slop”. AI can run continuously; accountability still lands on people and institutions.
