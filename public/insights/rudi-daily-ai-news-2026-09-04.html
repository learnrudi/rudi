AI News for September 4, 2026

OpenAI announces GPT-6 Astra, Google DeepMind adds a cybersecurity model, publishers challenge the fair-use defense in AI training, and AI infrastructure attracts multibillion-dollar funding rounds. All 61 stories from the day, below.

September 4, 202661 stories · 9 categories

OpenAI announced GPT-6 Astra as a staged upgrade to ChatGPT and Codex, adding more capable computer use, coding, and cybersecurity functions. Some cybersecurity features are initially gated behind a safety program, while Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 3.8 Flash and a cyber variant for trusted defenders. The model race is increasingly a security-access question as well as a capability contest.

The money and legal stakes moved alongside the models. One report says Crusoe raised more than $3 billion and Gimlet Labs raised $300 million— single-source figures worth treating cautiously, but a clear sign of investor interest in compute and efficiency. Meanwhile, news organizations asked a court to reject OpenAI and Microsoft’s fair-use defense, and a federal judge gave final approval to Anthropic’s $1.5 billion copyright settlement. AI’s commercial expansion is arriving with the ownership disputes attached.

Away from the launch cycle, the returns remain uneven. McKinsey’s 2026 survey found a gap between individual productivity gains and enterprise financial impact, while Fortune reports that workers’ share of U.S. income is at a record low. Better tools do not settle who captures their value.
