AI News for July 18, 2026

Anthropic makes Claude Fable 5 permanent for Max subscribers while Pro users get a credit then API pricing, a US judge declines to block Meta from laying off workers who sued over AI discrimination, France's competition authority flags three companies holding 84% of the AI agent market, and Etched chases a $20 billion valuation on $1 billion in pre-ship demand. All 55 stories from the day, below.

July 18, 202655 stories · 56 source links · 9 categories

Saturday’s theme was the bill. Anthropic, per one report, made Claude Fable 5 permanent for Max and Team Premium subscribers at reduced limits, while Pro users get a one-time usage credit and then API pricing. France’s competition authority put a number on the market those prices live in: OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic hold 84% of the AI agent market, and the regulator wants data-access and interoperability rules before lock-in hardens. Investors keep paying in advance regardless: inference-chip startup Etched is running back-to-back rounds at $10 billion and $20 billion valuations, backed by a reported $1 billion in customer demand for a chip that hasn’t shipped. And McKinsey supplied the quiet stat of the day: as enterprises scale agents, nearly 60% of operating costs go to verification — paying people and systems to check the machines’ work.

A US judge declined to block Meta from laying off 26 workers who allege the company’s AI tools discriminated against employees with disabilities or on medical leave — the discrimination suit continues, but the layoffs proceed. The year’s broader tally, by one count: more than 165,000 people laid off across nearly 40 companies with AI adoption cited as the primary driver. A separate analysis describes career-ladder compression, with junior roles splitting into seniorized tracks and declining ones. And a workplace-consent gap got measured: only 35% of workers say they are always asked before AI transcribes their meetings.

Governance did not take the weekend off. Brazil’s electoral court added OpenAI, Anthropic, and ElevenLabs to its election disinformation pact ahead of the 2026 vote, extending deepfake rules from platforms to the model vendors themselves. One analysis reads the WAIC chair’s statement as a Chinese bid to write global AI governance rules. The scam economy moved faster than the policy: fraudsters are impersonating the White House Gold Eagle security initiative to harvest credentials from financial institutions, and a post-mortem examines the JadePuffer ransomware and its autonomy claims alongside the industry pushback it provoked. The EU’s Article 50 transparency duties still arrive on August 2, omnibus delay or not.
