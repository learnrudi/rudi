AI News for October 20, 2026

Microsoft reportedly exits a $2.7 billion UK data-center plan over delayed grid power, Gemini agents head into U.S. agencies, Perplexity moves onto institutional servers, and election platforms confront AI-generated political media. All 29 stories from the day, below.

October 20, 202629 stories · 8 categories

One report says Microsoft ended plans to fund a supercomputer at a roughly $2.7 billion AI data center that may wait until the 2030s for grid power. While electricity constrains one build-out, Accenture Federal Services and Google Public Sector announced Gemini launchpads for U.S. agencies, backed by a planned 1,000-person engineering workforce. Perplexity, meanwhile, partnered with Valarian to put Perplexity Computer on institutional and air-gapped servers. A daily investment brief also cites a forecast of roughly 50 gigawatts of new data-center capacity in 2027, with memory and advanced logic chips as constraints. The demand is expansive; the bottlenecks are stubbornly physical.

The election-information problem is moving onto familiar screens. AI-generated political ads are appearing on Texas television without a state disclosure requirement, according to KSAT, while experts quoted by the station say the fakes are getting harder to spot. Bluesky has responded to the wider election environment with rules for removing, labeling, or leaving midterm-election posts untouched. Elsewhere, Twinnin offers people a registry for licensing their facial likeness or formally opting out of AI use, as incoming ACTU president Emma Kingdon warns that AI can automate entire jobs and expand employer surveillance. Consent, disclosure, and worker protection are becoming practical tests of who gets control when AI enters public life.

What People Are Asking

Why did Microsoft walk away from the UK AI data center?

According to one report, Microsoft ended its plan to fund a supercomputer at Nscale’s roughly $2.7 billion Loughton data center after the site was told grid power would not arrive until the 2030s. yournews.com →

Is Google deploying Gemini agents in U.S. government agencies?

Accenture Federal Services and Google Public Sector announced a program to deploy Gemini-based agents in U.S. federal agencies, supported by a planned 1,000-person Forward Deployed Engineer workforce. shattered.io →

Can Perplexity Computer run on private or air-gapped servers?

Yes. Perplexity partnered with Valarian to run Perplexity Computer on institutions’ own servers, including air-gapped networks, for regulated sectors such as finance, government, and defense. unite.ai →

Does Texas require political ads to disclose AI-generated content?

Texas law does not require disclosure of AI use in political ads, according to KSAT, even as experts say AI-generated material is becoming harder to identify. ksat.com →

How will Bluesky moderate misinformation during the 2026 midterms?

Bluesky says it will remove false voting information and has detailed which election posts it will remove, label, or leave untouched, along with plans for an election command center and specialist review. ppc.land →

Can people opt out of having their face used by AI companies?

Twinnin is a facial-likeness consent and provenance registry that lets people license their face to AI companies for payment or formally opt out through its Defend declaration. creativeboom.com →

What is the ACTU saying about AI job losses and workplace surveillance?

Incoming ACTU president Emma Kingdon warns that AI is automating entire jobs and enabling employer surveillance, and she is calling for stronger worker protections and government action. jokericildihbph.com →

Could the Supreme Court change K–12 magnet-school admissions?

The Supreme Court agreed to hear a case that could extend its 2023 affirmative-action rollback to K–12 magnet-school admissions and affect Latino students’ access. latintimes.com →
