AI News for November 24, 2026

GPT-6.2 reaches paid ChatGPT tiers, BBC Verify traces more than 500 AI-generated videos to a China-linked Taiwan influence network, an Australian committee proposes frontier-model audits, and a Maine report counts more than 171,000 AI-exposed jobs. All 85 stories from the day, below.

November 24, 202685 stories · 89 source links · 10 categories

OpenAI is rolling GPT-6.2 into ChatGPT’s regular chat tab for paid subscribers, with reported improvements to instruction-following, image understanding, browsing, and safety. One weekly roundup says GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol drew mixed reviews while Google launched Gemini 4 Neon, Anthropic became public, and Microsoft introduced in-house models. Elsewhere, a daily briefing pairs OpenAI’s proposed development limits and prediction-loss measurements with research-only medical models from Ant and OpenRouter’s removal of three providers and 125 zero-retention endpoints. Another roundup puts benchmarks showing agents struggling with everyday tasks beside reported GPT-6 protein-design capabilities. The model race now includes an increasingly visible argument over where systems ship, who can use them, and which routes remain open.

BBC Verify found a China-linked network spreading more than 500 AI-generated videos against Taiwan’s ruling DPP politicians. The same manipulation tools are reaching commerce: a reported network of more than 10,000 AI-built counterfeit shops imitates 166 brands across 26 countries, with some sites appearing in AI shopping recommendations. Regulators and courts are drawing boundaries in response. An Australian parliamentary committee proposed frontier-AI audits, disclosure, and copyright reform in a blueprint tied to major companies training models in Australia, while a U.S. federal court allowed a wrongful-arrest case to proceed after police relied solely on an AI facial-recognition result. A Congressional Research Service report also warns that agentic AI could automate cyberattack campaigns and questions the Defense Department’s readiness.

A Maine labor report identifies more than 171,000 jobs as highly exposed to AI, as unions and employers begin negotiating over automation and layoffs. A warehouse study found that 57% of organizations expect AI to make more than a quarter of operational decisions autonomously by 2028, while 90% plan to increase investment. Governments are building training programs at very different scales: Iran’s education minister said roughly 250,000 teachers received AI training last year and outlined plans for smart classrooms, while Tamil Nadu launched a 40-hour program for 2,000 government and aided-school teachers. Those efforts sit beside reported raises of $1.75 billion for Sierra and $277 million in seed funding for Marlowe.
