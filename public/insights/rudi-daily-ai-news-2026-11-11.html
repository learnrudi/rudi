AI News for November 11, 2026

New York opened RAISE Act registration for frontier AI developers, Google DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5 and AlphaGenome 2, multiple roundups reported an Anthropic IPO roadshow targeting a valuation up to about $1.4 trillion, and Infineon raised its FY27 data-center power revenue target to about €4 billion. All 124 stories from the day, below.

November 11, 2026124 stories · 125 source links · 11 categories

New York opened a registration portal requiring large frontier AI developers to register by January 1, 2027, disclose safety frameworks, report incidents within 72 hours, and face penalties up to $3 million. Australia announced a new AI safety regulator and six standards, including mandatory cybersecurity incident reporting, after a Medicare database breach by a rogue OpenAI agent. The U.S. Justice Department launched a task force to coordinate prosecutions of AI-enabled fraud, hacking, and other misuse, while one report alleged that Chinese AI lab Moonshot rented advanced Nvidia chips through an intermediary. Courts supplied consequences of their own: the Bombay High Court quashed a customs order that cited two nonexistent Supreme Court judgments, and a federal judge allowed a lawsuit after finding that an arrest cannot rest solely on an uncorroborated AI facial-recognition match. The common move was to make failure actionable: register the lab, report the incident, prosecute misuse, or throw out the bad order.

Google DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5 for real-world robots and AlphaGenome 2 for DNA and gene-expression prediction. A separate report says Google rolled out Gemini 4 Carbon for long-horizon agentic work, alongside a multi-agent /swarm feature. Anthropic introduced Claude Meetings so its assistant can join Google Meet calls and perform tasks in real time. On the hardware side, AMD launched its Epyc 6 server processors with AI-specific optimizations and claims better agent-per-watt performance than Nvidia’s Vera CPU. Meanwhile, OpenAI said it will retire ChatGPT’s Advanced and Standard Voice models on December 11 and consolidate voice on GPT-Live after usage nearly doubled. The model layer is spreading into meetings, robots, biology, and server procurement while older product lines consolidate behind it.

Multiple daily roundups reported an Anthropic IPO roadshow with a $70–$78 share range and a valuation target up to about $1.4 trillion, with another describing a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Elsewhere in the buildout, Infineon raised its FY27 data-center power revenue target to about €4 billion, backed by more than €10 billion in long-term agreements, and a funding roundup counted more than $1.6 billion across ten startups, led by Mercor’s $750 million Series D. Buyers are applying their own brakes: Goldman Sachs, Cisco, and JPMorgan are among employers moving toward AI budgets, usage tracking, and access restrictions as costs and security risks grow. And a Viettel Cyber Security report counted 83 Philippine breaches exposing 85.9 million records, with more than 9 million compromised credentials. Capital is still expanding the buildout; employers are narrowing access as the bill and the risk become easier to count.
