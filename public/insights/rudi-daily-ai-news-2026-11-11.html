AI News for November 11, 2026

Goldman Sachs, Cisco, and JPMorgan gate workplace AI access as Google rolls out Gemini 4 Carbon; Reuters reports that a blacklisted Chinese university obtained Nvidia H200 chips; New York opens RAISE Act registration; and two daily briefings put Anthropic’s potential IPO valuation as high as about $1.4 trillion. All 80 stories from the day, below.

November 11, 202680 stories · 11 categories

Goldman Sachs, Cisco, and JPMorgan are narrowing which AI tools employees can use as rising costs and security risks turn broad adoption into a budget-and-permissions problem. At the same time, Google is rolling out Gemini 4 Carbon for long-horizon agentic work, Anthropic introduced a Claude beta that can join Google Meet calls and perform tasks in real time, and AMD launched Epyc 6 server CPUs with Zen 6 cores and AI-specific optimizations. The agents are getting new jobs; the people paying for them are adding turnstiles.

Reuters reports that a U.S.-blacklisted Chinese university with PLA ties bought Nvidia H200 chips and rented access through domestic suppliers, and one report alleges Moonshot rented Nvidia’s most advanced chips through an intermediary. The policy response is getting specific: New York opened RAISE Act registration for large frontier-model developers, with a January 1 deadline and 72-hour incident reporting, while Australia announced a new AI safety regulator and six standards, including mandatory cybersecurity incident reporting. Governments are specifying who must register and report, while the chip stories show how indirect access can frustrate a rule on paper.

One daily briefing reports Anthropic opened its IPO roadshow at $70–$78 a share, targeting a valuation as high as about $1.4 trillion; a separate fintech briefing gives the same valuation ceiling and says 21 banks committed about $270 million to a stablecoin venture. Elsewhere, Rox raised $100 million at a $1.3 billion valuation to challenge Salesforce with AI sales agents, and a funding roundup reported more than $1.6 billion across ten startup deals, led by Mercor’s $750 million round. Capital is accelerating the buildout while employers and governments add gates around use.

What does New York’s RAISE Act require AI companies to do?
