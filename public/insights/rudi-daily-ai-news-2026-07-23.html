AI News for July 23, 2026

AMD unveils its full-stack AI portfolio with Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta aboard, the Trump administration launches a $5 billion Genesis Mission to put AI across federal agencies, model-picker evidence points to a Claude Opus 5 rollout, and Munich prepares Europe's first major AI music ruling. All 51 stories from the day, below.

July 23, 202651 stories · 53 source links · 10 categories

AMD turned Monday’s Helios delivery news into a full product line. At its AAI 2026 event the company unveiled a full-stack AI portfolio — Helios rack-scale systems, 6th Gen EPYC CPUs, and Instinct MI400 GPUs, with Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta named as partners. A disaggregated inference system built with Cerebras claims 30% better price-performance than Nvidia’s Vera Rubin — the vendor’s math, but a benchmark Nvidia now has to answer — and Supermicro detailed a 72-GPU rack around AMD silicon. Two days ago the story was one system with one anchor customer; today it is an ecosystem.

The model labs kept moving underneath. Evidence in a model picker and quota catalog suggests Anthropic is preparing a Claude Opus 5 rollout — reported, not announced. Black Forest Labs unveiled FLUX 3, a frontier model trained jointly on images, video, audio, and actions. OpenAI introduced Presence, an enterprise platform for voice and chat agents with built-in guardrails. Google, still without its delayed flagship, shipped enterprise tools instead. And routing became the week’s quiet infrastructure pattern: Cursor’s Router cuts AI coding costs 30–60% by its maker’s account, and Runway launched a Media Router that picks generative models by quality, speed, or cost.

Government moved from reviewing AI to running on it. The Trump administration launched the $5 billion Genesis Mission to integrate AI across federal agencies, while one analysis describes a broader shift toward active regulation — safety-testing mandates and hardware as a geopolitical lever. The politics are arriving with the infrastructure: Democrats are seizing on rural backlash against AI data centers that is dividing Republicans in states like Texas. In Munich, a court is about to deliver Europe’s first major ruling on AI music in GEMA’s case against Suno. And the LA Times closed the loop on the week’s settlement: the largest copyright settlement in US history, now approved.
