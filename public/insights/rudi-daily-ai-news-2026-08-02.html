AI News for August 2, 2026

The EU AI Act's August 2 obligations reach schools, recruiters, and audit firms while the high-risk tier stays postponed. August 2, 202643 stories · 10 categories

The EU AI Act’s August 2 date arrived, and it landed on the people who do the paperwork. Italian schools now carry binding transparency and risk-management duties — labeling AI interactions, banning emotion recognition, and their teachers have to be trained to an AI-literacy standard, because the school itself is the deployer. Dutch accountants inherited inventory, documentation, and control obligations for the high-risk systems they run, Big Four tooling included. What did not arrive is the tier everyone spent the year preparing for: the high-risk obligations were pushed back and the training-data requirements relaxed, which a German public-radio report treats as an open question about whether the Act still bites. A compliance explainer sorts the two piles — what applies now, what the Digital Omnibus postponed, and how a deployer quietly becomes a provider.

Employers got the sharper version of the same day. Poland’s recruiters woke up owing human oversight, candidate transparency, and heavy fines for skipping either — and eleven governments chose that week to warn that the candidate may be the synthetic one. Per one report, North Korean IT operatives are using real-time deepfake video to get through hiring screens, with $800 million said to be flowing back to Pyongyang’s weapons programs. A single-source figure worth reading skeptically, but the mechanism is the point: the new rules govern the machine screening the candidate, not the candidate screening as a human. Inside the companies already hiring, a Forbes contributor reads survey data showing two thirds of employees stay online to hide the time AI saves them, and argues that paying for presence instead of results is what converts a productivity gain into busywork. A European adoption guide pitches the balance employers are now supposed to strike, department by department.

The capacity argument moved east. One report has DeepSeek planning a gigawatt-scale data center in Inner Mongolia — the lab that built its reputation on training efficiency now buying physical plant. A CNBC op-ed argues the U.S. lead in AI is all but gone, and that China’s coordinated ecosystem strategy is why, while an analysis of NATO’s posture makes the supply-chain version of that case: alliance systems that cannot be inspected, running partly on Chinese components. At home the appetite for rules is not the constraint — an opinion piece citing University of Maryland surveys puts support for federal AI oversight at 79 percent among Republicans. Two numbers frame the rest: automated requests overtook human web traffic for the first time, by one measure, and a trading-desk preview expects Nvidia to post 96 percent revenue growth on August 26, which will settle more of this argument than any op-ed.
