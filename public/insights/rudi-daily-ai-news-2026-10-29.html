AI News for October 29, 2026

The UK commits nearly £700 million to public-sector AI and defenses against rogue agents, Poland activates AI Act enforcement powers, Nevada weighs hospitality job losses against a data-center boom, and more than $1.3 billion flows into chips, cybersecurity, robotics, and automation. All 49 stories from the day, below.

October 29, 202649 stories · 11 categories

The UK Budget committed nearly £700 million to AI, pairing a new GovAI unit for civil-service adoption with a National Agentic Security Unit and cyber defenses aimed at rogue agents. Poland’s AI systems law took full effect on October 28, giving a national commission authority to inspect systems, investigate complaints, and impose administrative fines under the EU AI Act. Governments are funding adoption and building enforcement capacity at the same time.

Nevada unions and political candidates face a sharper tradeoff: AI threatens jobs in Las Vegas hospitality while a large data-center boom promises to create others ahead of the November 3 elections. Elsewhere, KT says its generative AI voicebot handles 1.2 million calls a month with an 81% first-contact resolution rate and 11% shorter call times, figures that come from the company itself. AI’s workplace consequences are no longer one-directional: displacement, new infrastructure jobs, and automated service work are arriving together.

More than $1.3 billion was committed across ten startup financing rounds, led by FuriosaAI’s $620 million Series D and Upwind Security’s $400 million Series C. A separate roundup reports Mistral released the open-weight, trillion-parameter Large 4 and identified eight vLLM vulnerabilities, including a Ghostscript remote-code-execution flaw. Capital is chasing the infrastructure layer, while the software serving models is still accumulating security debt.
