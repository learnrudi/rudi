AI News for October 29, 2026

Microsoft flattens engineering teams around agentic AI, workers press for bargaining rights and job protections, Poland activates AI inspections and fines, and the UK puts nearly £700 million behind government AI and agent security. All 95 stories from the day, below.

October 29, 202695 stories · 11 categories

Microsoft’s new Copilot and Office boss Charles Lamanna is removing middle-management layers from engineering teams, citing agentic AI, while tightening office requirements for senior staff. The labor response is getting more specific: Yale’s clerical and technical workers union is seeking AI-related job protections in contract talks, while a UK tech union is demanding a legal right to refuse workplace AI, bargaining before implementation, and safeguards against AI-justified layoffs. The argument is no longer whether AI will enter the workplace. It is who gets to redesign the job.

Poland’s AI system law took full effect on October 28, giving a new commission authority to inspect systems, investigate AI Act violations, and impose fines. In the UK, the Budget allocates nearly £700 million to AI, including a GovAI unit and a National Agentic Security Unit. Australia’s lower house also passed an AI-chatbot duty-of-care bill, according to AI Weekly’s October 29 roundup. Governments are building enforcement, operating capacity, and security controls at the same time.

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon for software engineering and cybersecurity, expanded Gemini 4 Argon access, and said the Gemini app reached 1 billion users. OpenAI’s October release slate included a $500-a-month Pro 500 tier, GPT-6.1 Sol, always-on “dots” agents, Codex Cloud, and new collaboration features. Research agents are moving beyond software tasks, too: one arXiv paper reports five consecutive autonomous runs that produced revised, re-computable papers judged at or near workshop level by external AI reviewers, and another demonstrates agents critiquing one another and rejecting unsupported conclusions while investigating quantum spin ensembles. The products are shipping; the research question is whether agents can also police their own work.
