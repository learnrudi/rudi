AI News for July 27, 2026

A US court approves the largest AI copyright settlement while music publishers widen their own suit against Anthropic, two federal courts split on whether chatbot conversations stay privileged, the EU's watermarking and deepfake-labelling rules reach their August 2 date, and xAI compresses two Grok releases into six weeks. All 88 stories from the day, below.

July 27, 202688 stories · 93 source links · 11 categories

Anthropic spent the day in court records. A US judge approved the $1.5 billion settlement over pirated training books, the largest copyright resolution an AI company has agreed to. Music publishers, meanwhile, widened their separate lyrics suit, alleging Claude was trained on copyrighted lyrics and built to reproduce them. The people typing into chatbots got legal news of their own: two federal courts split on whether consulting an AI waives attorney-client privilege. Until an appeals court resolves it, what you tell Claude or ChatGPT sits in discoverable limbo.

The rulebooks picked up dates and sponsors. The EU’s AI Act transparency rules take effect August 2, requiring AI watermarking and deepfake labels, with fines for firms that skip them. Israel’s Central Elections Committee will require campaign ads to disclose AI use. In Washington the argument is over who writes the rules at all: Rep. Jay Obernolte is pitching the bipartisan Frontier Act, a federal AI-safety framework, while federal proposals to preempt state AI laws are drawing resistance from the states they would overrule. And one foreign-policy analysis argues US export controls on frontier models are pushing allies to reduce their dependence on American technology.

The workplace numbers cut both ways. An OpenAI study finds 43.5 percent of occupation-specific ChatGPT messages involve tasks outside the user’s own occupation — workers reaching past their job descriptions — while recruiters report candidates running AI live during video interviews and the Irish Times channels worker nostalgia for the pre-AI office. The money kept moving: Axe Compute, a pharma company a year ago, signed $1.3 billion in AI compute contracts, and enterprise AI returns are landing in insights and customer engagement rather than cost savings. Punjab put AI on the core curriculum for 3.15 million students, a New York district paused its humanoid robot-teacher pilot, and President Trump posted 43 AI-generated images on Truth Social in a single stretch.
