October 5, 202646 stories · 10 categories

One report says researchers tracked $80 million in spending on about 170 AI-generated U.S. midterm ads, finding deepfakes, a partisan gap favoring Republicans, and inconsistent disclosure. Another report counted at least 164 AI-influenced political ads and said their electoral impact remains unclear. Beyond campaigns, a federal appeals court temporarily blocked Minnesota's AI “nudification” ban while xAI's free-speech lawsuit proceeds, and a non-consensual deepfake of actor Janhvi Kapoor renewed scrutiny of India's three-hour takedown rule. The reported ad totals differ; the common problem is synthetic media moving through elections, platforms, and courts without a uniform rulebook.

A federal judge dismissed Chegg and Penske Media's antitrust claims over Google AI Overviews, leaving copyright and compensation questions unresolved. In the labor market, a McKinsey report estimated that about 11 million U.S. workers may need to change occupations by 2035 even as the economy creates more jobs than it displaces. Robert Half data found that 72% of employers plan to pay more for AI skills, while higher-education technology leaders put determining where AI adds real value atop their 2027 priority list. Employers may pay a premium for the skills, but institutions are still deciding where those skills produce results.

Model competition is widening: Reflection AI is reportedly preparing a U.S. open-weight competitor to DeepSeek and Qwen. Model design is also reaching beyond benchmarks. Anthropic co-founder Christopher Olah held confidential meetings with religious scholars from multiple faiths to explore AI consciousness and moral codes for Claude. Elsewhere, a Frontiers in Oncology review mapped AI uses across cancer drug discovery, and Lucen launched an AI-assisted flowchart tool inside PowerPoint.
