AI News for August 29, 2026

WIRED reports that AI companies expect AI-enabled cyberattacks within months, while New York officials put 2025 losses from AI-powered crypto scams at $11 billion. August 29, 202635 stories · 36 source links · 10 categories

WIRED reports that AI companies expect AI-enabled cyberattacks within months and are urging industry and government to respond. New York officials, meanwhile, say AI-powered crypto scams caused $11 billion in losses in 2025, with deepfakes and fake platforms used to defraud thousands. Separately, a ransomware group claims it encrypted 4.2 TiB of data from TRC Companies—a claim that is not independently confirmed here. A federal judge also upheld New York’s social-media disclosure requirements against X’s First Amendment challenge. The warnings are sweeping; the reported losses, access claims, and disclosure rules show where the consequences are becoming concrete.

Two reports describe growing public anger over AI job fears and data-center expansion, but the effects are landing unevenly. A Fortune survey of 1,250 U.S. workers found only 3% said AI had cost them a job so far, while NYC public schools’ software-purchasing freeze has extended into the new school year, blocking routine non-AI purchases as the district revises its policy. Corporate movement has not stopped: one report says OpenAI is ending its Cursor partnership after SpaceX acquired the coding platform, and Knight Frank says foreign financial firms leased a record 7.32 million square feet of Indian office space for global capability centers. Fear, policy friction, corporate rivalry, and investment are all moving on different clocks.
