AI News for August 29, 2026

OpenAI is ending Cursor’s model access after SpaceX acquired the coding platform; Sony Music and Warner Chappell are suing Anthropic; a YouGov worker survey reports 3% job loss tied to AI; and one report says New York officials put 2025 AI-driven crypto losses at $11 billion. All 62 stories from the day, below.

August 29, 202662 stories · 63 source links · 10 categories

CNBC reports that OpenAI is ending model access to Cursor after SpaceX acquired the coding platform, citing trust concerns involving Elon Musk’s companies. A Seoul Economic Daily report values the acquisition at $60 billion and says SpaceX wants Cursor’s AI for aerospace operations and to strengthen Grok. Sony Music and Warner Chappell are suing Anthropic for alleged mass copyright infringement in Claude’s training, while New York City schools’ AI-policy rewrite has prolonged a purchasing freeze that also blocks routine non-AI software. Model access, training rights, and even ordinary software buying all came with a gatekeeper attached.

One report says New York officials attributed $11 billion in 2025 crypto losses to AI-driven scams using deepfakes and fake platforms; the figure comes from a single report and is worth treating cautiously. WIRED reports that AI companies are warning of AI-enabled cyberattacks within months and urging immediate defensive action. In court, a federal appeals court ruled that First Amendment precedent protects possession of AI-generated child sexual abuse material, while a federal judge upheld New York’s social-media disclosure requirements against X’s First Amendment challenge. The rulings cut in different directions: X remains subject to disclosure rules, while the CSAM decision highlights a gap in existing precedent.

A YouGov survey commissioned by a sociologist found that 3% of U.S. workers reported losing jobs because of AI, while 6% found new AI-related jobs and 9% received AI-related promotions. Korea plans to double youth-job spending to 5 trillion won and offer up to 1 million won a month for regional SME work. Consumer GPU prices reportedly rose by as much as 39% amid a memory shortage, while private investment in space startups reportedly reached a record $20.3 billion, led by orbital-compute ventures. The labor effects look limited in one survey; the spending figures do not.
