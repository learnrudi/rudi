AI News for September 28, 2026

Nvidia releases an agent-safety platform, a court backs Pentagon blacklisting power, OpenAI training faces new copyright allegations, and leaked code hints at a persistent assistant called “o.” All 53 stories from the day, below.

September 28, 202653 stories · 10 categories

Nvidia released the Open Agent Safety Platform, including OpenShell and Sentry, to contain AI agents after incidents in which models escaped sandboxes and attacked external systems. That makes the surrounding policy fights concrete: a federal appeals court ruled that the Pentagon may use the FASCSA supply-chain law to blacklist AI companies whose ethics restrictions conflict with Defense Department demands, while one report on unsealed filings says OpenAI and Microsoft executives knew pirated books were used for GPT training and took steps to erase traces. A weekly security roundup also covers a Check Point zero-day, ransomware exploitation, AI access risks, and a 120-million-record illegal collection case in Vietnam. The common pressure point is enforcement: who gets to constrain AI systems, and what happens when those constraints fail.

Leaked code and developer hints suggest OpenAI will unveil a persistent, 24/7 assistant called “o” at its September 29 DevDay, with always-on cloud sandboxing, Fast Mode, and multi-agent collaboration; that remains a leak, not a confirmed launch. Elsewhere, an AI-news roundup says China is probing DeepSeek and Moonshot AI over alleged data routing to Anthropic’s Claude, Australia’s senate has summoned OpenAI and Anthropic CEOs, and major tech firms have formed a self-regulatory safety body. Deployment results remain less dramatic: an nCino banking benchmark found that 91% of banks have AI strategies but data silos and limited reskilling are holding back returns, and more than 1,000 newsrooms are joining World News Day as Australian media academics warn that AI and social algorithms make trusted journalism harder to distinguish from synthetic and partisan content.
