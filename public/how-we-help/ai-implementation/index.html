05 · AI Implementation

Build, test, and launch AI workflows your team can use.

RUDI helps organizations move validated use cases from concept to working practice—connecting tools, agents, workflows, data, governance, human oversight, and adoption.

Implementation answers

What do we actually deploy—and how?

Each workflow needs an owner, approved access, quality checks, and a plan for ongoing support.

What implementation includes

Define the workflow, connect the tools, and assign oversight.

A reliable implementation accounts for the work, the people doing it, the information involved, the systems it touches, the decisions it influences, and the oversight it requires.

Workflow

Process and role design

Define the starting state, future workflow, human decisions, handoffs, exceptions, and responsibilities.

Technology

Tools, agents, and integrations

Select the appropriate interaction model, systems, data access, connectors, automation, and technical boundaries.

Governance

Controls and oversight

Establish privacy, permissions, verification, review, monitoring, fallback, accountability, and escalation.

Quality

Testing and evaluation

Define useful outputs, test realistic scenarios, evaluate failure modes, and set acceptance criteria.

People

Enablement and adoption

Prepare users and managers, document the workflow, support practice, collect feedback, and reinforce responsible use.

Operations

Ownership and improvement

Assign ongoing responsibility, monitor performance, manage changes, capture learning, and decide whether to scale.

From pilot to production

Test a pilot before expanding it.

A pilot tests a specific workflow with representative users and data. We assess output quality, time saved, errors, and the support needed to keep it running.

Frame

Define the problem, users, workflow, value hypothesis, boundaries, dependencies, and responsible-use requirements.

Design

Map the future workflow, choose the interaction model, identify human checkpoints, and design the evaluation.

Build

Configure or integrate the selected tools, instructions, knowledge, data access, permissions, and controls.

Test

Use realistic scenarios, edge cases, and user feedback to evaluate quality, safety, usability, and operational fit.

Adopt

Enable users, integrate the process into work, support behavior change, monitor outcomes, and improve.

Scale—or stop

Expand what proves valuable and supportable. Redesign or retire what does not.

Implementation questions

Decisions that belong before production.

01

What exact workflow and outcome are we trying to improve?

02

What information can the system access—and under what permissions?

03

Where must a person review, decide, approve, or intervene?

04

How will we test quality, risk, edge cases, and failure modes?

05

Who owns the system and the operating process after launch?

06

What evidence will justify scaling, redesigning, or stopping?

Before implementation

Validate readiness, strategy, and adoption conditions.

Implementation works best when the organization has already clarified the value, the people and workflows involved, the governance requirements, and the capabilities needed to sustain the change.

Start with readinessClarify strategy

Implementation readiness

  • Clear problem and value hypothesis
  • Representative users and workflow owners
  • Appropriate data and system access
  • Defined human oversight and governance
  • Testing and measurement plan
  • Adoption and operating ownership

After implementation

Some digital work should remain a managed service.

RUDI can continue operating a digital worker after launch, maintaining its integrations, monitoring results, and managing exceptions within agreed rules and oversight.
